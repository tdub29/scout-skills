
# Marr: Data Monetization Scan

## When to Use

Use when the user asks whether data can be monetized, sold, packaged, embedded in a product, used in partnerships, or treated as a strategic asset.

## Do Not Use For

Detailed pricing, marketplace implementation, or ordinary product analytics.

## Framework

Marr separates internal value and direct monetization. Data can improve decisions, operations, products, valuation, partner offerings, premium features, data services, benchmarks, alerts, or licensed datasets.

## How to Apply

1. Inventory data assets and current business uses.
2. Identify potential data customers: customers, suppliers, partners, advertisers, researchers, or internal units.
3. Decide whether value comes from raw data, aggregated insight, prediction, benchmark, workflow integration, or analytic capability.
4. Score opportunities by customer value, uniqueness, freshness, defensibility, permission, and delivery feasibility.
5. Filter for privacy, consent, competitive sensitivity, security, and reputational risk.
6. Choose monetization form: internal optimization, premium feature, report/API, partnership, licensing, or new business unit.
7. Define proof of demand and a minimum viable data product.

## Output Format

- Data asset inventory.
- Monetization map across internal value, direct revenue, enhanced service, partnership, and valuation value.
- Customer/value hypotheses.
- Risk filter.
- Shortlist with MVP, packaging hypothesis, and validation step.

## Common Failure Modes

- Assuming data is valuable because it is large.
- Selling raw personal data when aggregated insights would create value with less risk.
- Ignoring rights to use, resell, or combine data.
- Creating products that cannibalize trust or reveal competitive secrets.
- Overlooking internal monetization.
- Treating one-off reports as monetization without a repeatable refresh model.
