
# Marr: Strategy Execution Risk Audit

## When to Use

Use when a data strategy exists on paper but may fail in execution: stalled initiatives, low adoption, unclear ownership, senior resistance, department silos, missing skills, or no measurement cadence.

## Do Not Use For

Technical incident response, platform debugging, or broad data-culture maturity scoring.

## Framework

Marr's execution discipline starts at the top, confronts anti-data attitudes, breaks strategy into owned milestones, communicates the why, builds data culture, and revisits the strategy as business needs and technology change.

## How to Apply

1. Summarize intended business outcomes.
2. Map initiatives to milestones, owners, timetable, and success metrics.
3. Identify attitude blockers and evidence needed to counter them.
4. Audit communication: who understands the strategy, who acts, and who is left out.
5. Audit cross-functional dependencies and single points of failure.
6. Check budget, skills, vendor, and technology assumptions against the timeline.
7. Define data culture actions such as leadership example, champions, transparent measurement, and visible wins.
8. Set review triggers: annual review, business goal shift, technology change, use-case result, or risk event.

## Output Format

- Execution health verdict.
- Risk register with symptom, impact, owner, mitigation, deadline.
- Ownership/milestone map.
- Communication and buy-in plan.
- Capability gap plan.
- Review cadence and renewal triggers.

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating strategy launch as success instead of tracking milestones.
- Assuming executive buy-in is enough.
- Training people on dashboards without explaining decisions to change.
- Ignoring gut-instinct resistance until it blocks adoption.
- Letting departments build isolated contradictory tools.
- Failing to refresh strategy as questions, sources, or technology change.
