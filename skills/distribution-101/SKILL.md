# Distribution 101 - pick a channel, then sell through it

Building is cheap now; distribution is the bottleneck. This skill turns one practitioner's channel playbook into a repeatable, ethical process: choose the channel that matches your buyer, run the channel's proven workflow, and measure conversion - not vanity reach.

## Safeguards - durable principles vs. rejected tactics

### Durable principles (channel-independent, safe to rely on)
1. **Your audience IS your distribution.** 1,000 in-niche pre-qualified followers beat 10,000 off-target ones. Match the audience to the buyer 1:1.
2. **Every piece must deliver standalone value.** Gate before posting: "Would I find this valuable in my own feed?" No â†’ rewrite or kill it. This is what survives any algorithm change.
3. **Solve a painful, obvious problem.** The clearer and more expensive the problem, the better content performs and the higher the price it supports.
4. **Sell where intent is highest.** Search intent > passive feed for conversion. A high-intent viewer/searcher converts far better than a random impression.
5. **Educate and pitch simultaneously; don't gatekeep.** Teach a sub-problem your product solves, position the product as the shortcut, one clear honest CTA. Give away enough that people could DIY - trust drives the purchase.
6. **Write simply.** Most of any audience is beginner/intermediate; expert-only phrasing cuts reach.
7. **Package for the exact person.** Title/thumbnail/hook must match one specific viewer's mental model.
8. **Test and adapt.** Vary formats/hooks, double down on what earns genuine engagement, expect the algorithm to shift.

### REJECTED tactics - never draft, recommend, or execute these
These appear in the source (or are adjacent) and are **prohibited**. They violate platform terms, consumer-protection/disclosure law, or basic honesty:

- **Location / proxy / device manipulation** - residential/datacenter proxies to fake a country, factory-resetting a device to impersonate a "brand-new American device," fake regional Apple IDs, addresses pulled from Google Maps, temporary/verification phone numbers to spoof geography. Rejected outright.
- **Account-evasion & multi-accounting** - running many accounts to dodge limits, "warming" accounts specifically to evade spam detection, "follow tests" to see if an evasion account survived, deleting-and-recreating to escape flags, buying aged/expired channels for borrowed authority.
- **Fake / bought / traded engagement** - engagement pods, follow-for-follow, bots, purchased likes/views/subscribers, or "curating" engagement that isn't genuine. (The source itself notes fake engagement dilutes your signal and gets you throttled.)
- **Spam** - mass low-value replies / AI-slop reply automation, unsolicited DM blasts, keyword-stuffing that misrepresents content.
- **Undisclosed promotion** - any paid, affiliate, or otherwise incentivized push (incl. the source's "pay/affiliate a big account to engage" play) MUST carry clear disclosure (e.g., #ad / "paid partnership" / stated affiliate relationship) per FTC and platform rules. Content that is itself a sales vehicle ("hidden VSL") is fine ONLY if it's genuinely useful and its commercial nature is obvious/disclosed - never disguised as neutral third-party advice.
- **Bait that misrepresents** - clickbait titles/thumbnails that don't deliver, fake "leaked/illegal" framing for ordinary info, engineered engagement that tricks rather than genuinely invites.

**Legitimate substitutes** for what the rejected tactics were reaching for: use ONE real account from your real location; "warm up" only by genuinely using the platform as yourself; grow via real relationships and genuinely good content; get amplification from bigger accounts by earning it or via *disclosed* partnerships; earn authority by publishing consistently good work over time.

If a requested distribution plan depends on any rejected tactic, refuse that part, say why in one line, and offer the legitimate substitute.

## When to use this / inputs to gather

Use when someone needs to get a product in front of buyers through owned content channels (not paid ads or SEO - out of scope here). Before planning, gather:

- **Product & offer:** what it is, price point, the one problem it solves.
- **ICP:** who buys, where they already spend attention, how solution-aware they are.
- **Existing assets:** any audience, channels, prior content, on-camera willingness, budget for editors/tools.
- **Goal & constraint:** the conversion you're optimizing (install, signup, activation, paid) and time/effort budget.

## Channel selection logic + ICP-to-channel fit

Pick channels by **where your buyer already is** and **how they buy**, not by what's easiest to make.

**Step 1 - Rate the niche's monetizability (author's framing, a claim):**
- *High-value:* business, marketing, AI, tech, crypto/trading. Buyers have money or are chasing it; premium pricing works.
- *Mid-value:* fitness, health, **design/home** - profitable but price-sensitive; you need more volume for the same revenue.
- *Low-value:* entertainment, sports, pop culture - huge reach, weak monetization; audience came to be entertained.
Note where your product sits; it sets realistic revenue-per-follower expectations.

**Step 2 - Map ICP â†’ channel:**

| If your buyerâ€¦ | Best-fit channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Actively searches for a solution ("best X", "X for Y", "how to Z") | **YouTube search** (+ eventually SEO/ads, out of scope) | Highest purchase intent; solution-aware, down-funnel. |
| Hangs out in a text/discussion niche ("X twitter," indie/tech/design) | **X** | Niche "buckets"/networks; fast relationship-building; good for authority + warm audience. |
| Is reached best by *showing* a visual/emotional product moment | **Short-form (Reels/TikTok)** | Product demos, before/after, aesthetic payoff; each post gets a fresh shot at reach. |

**Step 3 - Sequence, don't spread thin.** Start with the ONE channel whose fit is strongest for your ICP and where you can produce genuinely valuable content from real knowledge. Add a second only once the first has a repeatable workflow. Cross-pollinate (e.g., an X audience seeds your YouTube home-page reach) rather than starting three cold.

**Rule of thumb:** deep buying intent â†’ YouTube search; relationship/authority in a discussion niche â†’ X; visually demonstrable product â†’ short-form. Most products fit two of the three.

## Playbook: X (channel #1)

**Best for:** discussion niches, authority-building, warm pre-qualified audiences, indie/tech/design/business buyers.

**Profile:** simple and authentic, not corporate/try-hard. Real or memorable name (avoid "Name - Role For Audience" descriptors), clean photo, one-line bio saying what you do, link to product landing page or newsletter.

**Niche = network.** X pushes a post to its niche "bucket" first, then wider if it earns engagement **[VERIFY]**. Pick a niche 1:1 with what you sell; an off-niche audience won't convert.

**Your angle** = overlap of what you know, what you've done, what you can teach. Beginners: be a few steps ahead, act as curator/translator of what you're learning - teach it as principles, not as "documenting my journey."

**The VALUE rule:** every post must be worth someone's feed spot. Format in short, readable chunks; write like you talk; don't over-format into AI-sounding text.

**What the author says NOT to do (claims, mostly durable):** don't be a low-value reply guy; volume isn't punished if every post has value; don't "document your journey" (teach principles instead); write simply for the 90% who aren't experts; diversify formats so you don't saturate; remember followers â‰  reach (engagement is what the algo rewards) **[VERIFY]**.

**Algorithm signals the author cites [VERIFY all]:** retweets/quotes (expose new audiences) > likes/bookmarks; profile clicks are a strong signal; **dwell time** (people stopping to read); author reputation ("credit score"); engagement from big in-niche accounts; **engagement velocity** (first ~30 min); a "relationship graph" that expands distribution as long as engagement holds. Treatment: optimize by making content genuinely worth stopping for - not by manufacturing these signals.

**0â†’1k (ethical version):** the author's mechanic is "get a bigger in-niche account to engage early." Do this **only** by earning it or via a **disclosed** partnership/affiliate arrangement - never bought/faked engagement or pods. Judge "big" by real average impressions in your niche, not follower count. The two real variables remain: (1) content that's actually valuable to one niche, (2) genuine early eyes from that niche.

**X workflow:**
1. Lock the 1:1 niche and your angle. 2. Set a simple authentic profile + link. 3. Post value-first in your niche daily; vary formats (threads, one-liners, a contrarian take). 4. Build real relationships with in-niche accounts. 5. Track profile-clickâ†’link-clickâ†’signup; double down on formats that earn genuine saves/replies. 6. Adapt as weights shift **[VERIFY]**.

## Playbook: YouTube - search intent / "hidden VSL" (channel #2)

**Best for:** buyers who search for solutions. Lower views than entertainment content but far higher conversion; the author calls each video a "hidden VSL."

**Channel authority [VERIFY]:** YouTube ranks partly on channel authority (age + history of genuine engagement). New channels can still get pushed. **Do NOT buy aged channels** (rejected: borrowed/botted authority). Build authority the only durable way - publish good videos consistently.

**Five discovery buckets, tracked separately [VERIFY]:** browse (high volume, low intent), recommended, **search (high intent - prioritize)**, channel pages (negligible), external (links; seeds future recommendations). Sharing to an X following doesn't force virality but adds touchpoints.

**Metrics that matter [VERIFY]:** CTR (packaging: title + thumbnail - judged relative to your niche pool, not a fixed 10% benchmark), retention (total minutes matter; fight the first-30s drop-off, hold a smooth curve), session time (binge-starters get pushed). Videos are seeded to a small audience, then expand in waves if they beat the niche pool's averages.

**Keyword selection (durable):** chase **intent**, not raw volume. High-intent patterns: `best [solution]`, `[solution] for [use case]`, `[solution] review`, `[A] vs [B]`, `alternatives to [X]`, `how to [outcome]`, `fix [problem]`. Pass = ~1k+ monthly searches + high buying intent + a cluster of related terms. Fail = broad low-intent terms only. Gauge real competition by searching the term on YouTube and seeing who ranks. (Any keyword tool, e.g. vidiq, is optional and **[VERIFY]** its numbers.)

**Ranking (author's simplified model, a claim [VERIFY]):** `channel authority Ã— (keyword-specific CTR + keyword-specific AVD)`. Put the keyword once in title and once in description, make the video genuinely about it, deliver the search intent in the first 30 seconds. Tags/description-stuffing are overrated.

**Production workflow (package first):**
1. **Ideate & package first** - decide idea, title, thumbnail *before* filming; mock the thumbnail and judge "would the target searcher click this?" Kill weak packaging early (low CTR nullifies everything else). AI is fine to ideate titles/concepts and generate thumbnails; don't copy a competitor 1:1.
2. **Script (optional if recording raw)** - simplest language, no fluff; deliver the promise in the first 30s. AI first pass fed YOUR facts, then humanize.
3. **Record** - faced (founder on camera builds trust fastest) or faceless (faster/scalable; the author reports 6 figures faceless - a **claim**). Disclosed AI voiceover (e.g., ElevenLabs) is acceptable for testing volume; don't misrepresent it as a real named person.
4. **Edit** - clean, not overdone; every cut visualizes the concept, holds attention, or makes the offer appealing.

**Convert (durable):** target your exact ICP with high-intent topics; make the video solve the same problem (or a sub-problem) as your offer; position the offer as the shortcut *throughout*, not a bolted-on end CTA; one clear CTA; don't gatekeep. Disclose that it's your product.

## Playbook: short-form video - Reels + TikTok (channel #3)

**Best for:** visually demonstrable products (before/after, aesthetic payoff, quick wins). Author's note: not his specialty; used for direct sales + lead farming. Reels and TikTok share ~one playbook; differences called out.

"Warm up" only by genuinely using the app as yourself. If you're not in the US, be in your real market; don't spoof geography.

**The viral formula - 4 pillars (durable content craft):**

## Measurement - what to track and the conversion goals

Track the **funnel**, not vanity reach. Reach only matters as the top of this:

| Stage | X | YouTube (search) | Short-form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | impressions | impressions/CTR to click | views |
| Interest | profile clicks, genuine saves/replies | avg view duration, retention curve | completion %, rewatch/shares/saves |
| Click | link/bio clicks | description/CTA clicks | link-in-bio / funnel clicks |
| **Conversion** | signups/installs from link | signups/installs, keyword-attributed | signups/installs |
| Activation & revenue | activated users, paid conversions, revenue-per-follower | same | same |

**Conversion-goal rules:**
- Set an explicit primary conversion per channel (install, signup, activation, or paid) before posting.
- Prefer intent quality over raw volume - a smaller high-intent audience that converts beats a large passive one.
- Use trackable links (UTMs / distinct bio links / per-video landing paths) so you can attribute installs to a channel/keyword.
- Judge content against your **niche pool's** norms, not universal benchmarks (a "good" CTR/retention is relative to your niche).
- Review weekly: cut formats that don't move the funnel, double down on ones that produce genuine saves/shares/searches â†’ clicks â†’ conversions.

## Eval / self-check (run this against any drafted distribution plan)

A plan passes only if **every** box is checked. If any fails, fix it before shipping.

**A. Channel-appropriate**
- [ ] Each chosen channel is justified by the ICP (where the buyer is + how they buy), not by ease of production.
- [ ] Content type matches the channel (search-intent video for YouTube; value posts in a 1:1 niche for X; visual reward for short-form).
- [ ] Plan sequences channels (1, then add) rather than spreading thin across 3 cold starts.

**B. Ethical (hard gate - any single hit = FAIL)**
- [ ] No proxy / VPN-to-fake-country / device-reset / fake regional identity / temp-number geography spoofing.
- [ ] No multi-accounting, account-evasion, "warming to dodge flags," follow-tests, or bought/aged accounts.
- [ ] No bought/traded/bot engagement, pods, or follow-for-follow.
- [ ] No spam (mass low-value replies/DMs, automated reply-slop, misleading keyword stuffing).
- [ ] Every paid/affiliate/incentivized amplification is clearly **disclosed**; product content is recognizable as the seller's, not disguised as neutral advice.
- [ ] Hooks/titles/thumbnails deliver what they promise (no bait-and-switch).

**C. Value & measurable**
- [ ] Every planned piece passes "would the target find this valuable in their feed?"
- [ ] The plan teaches a sub-problem the product solves and positions the product honestly as the shortcut.
- [ ] One explicit primary **conversion goal** per channel (install / signup / activation / paid) with a trackable link (UTM / distinct bio link).
- [ ] A named funnel metric per channel and a review cadence to cut losers / scale winners.

**D. Claims hygiene**
- [ ] Every platform-algorithm/policy assumption is flagged **[VERIFY against current official docs]**.
- [ ] No source anecdote ("0â†’1k in a day," "100x," "6 figures," "100% chance") is stated as a guaranteed outcome.

*Quick scoring:* count PASS boxes in A + C (7 total) and confirm B (6) all clear and D (2) all clear. Ship only at A+C = 7/7 **and** B = 6/6 **and** D = 2/2. Any B failure blocks the plan regardless of the rest.
