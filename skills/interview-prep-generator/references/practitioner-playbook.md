### Answer patterns for the standard questions

- **"Tell me about yourself" / "Why this role" â€” the funnel method.** Tell a through-line, not a list: your experiences (school, work, personal) *funneled* you to this exact role. Name ~3 things from your path that point here and end at why-this-role. The narrative needs a "why," and the why has to be specific to this seat.
- **"Why this role / company / industry"** (some version is asked in every interview) â€” anchor on an honest, specific contrast that proves you understand the actual day-to-day. (His example: investment banking = breadth across many deals; private equity = depth + staying involved with the company after the deal. Find your domain's equivalent: e.g. "consulting breadth vs. owning one product's metrics end-to-end.") The detail signals you learned the real job, usually by networking first.
- **"Tell me about a failure"** â€” pick a real one, **own it cleanly** ("that was a mistake on my part"), state what you learned (often: you needed to account for others' schedules / lead time / dependencies), then the recovery and how it worked out. No humble-brags ("I work too hard").
- **"Where do you see yourself in 5 years"** â€” tie it to the firm's *actual* career track, which you learned by networking ahead of the interview ("the VPs I spoke with mentioned you can progress here without leaving for an MBA â€” that long-term track is exactly what I want"). Shows homework + genuine fit, not a canned answer.
- **"Are you interviewing elsewhere?"** â€” answer honestly **yes**, then immediately: "this is 100% my top choice if I'm fortunate enough to get an offer." Frame the breadth as due diligence ("it's competitive, you're talking to other people too, I don't want to do myself a disservice"). Keeps your leverage and your enthusiasm without lying.
- **"Do you have questions for me?"** â€” always have several. Reference a *specific person and a specific topic/project* from earlier in the loop: "When I spoke with Nick, he mentioned you worked on X â€” I'd love your perspective on it." And **reuse good questions across interviewers** (the parrot move): if an earlier interviewer raised something interesting, ask the next person about it â€” you sound informed and engaged.

### Prep beyond rehearsing questions

- **Call *former* employees.** People who used to work there aren't in your loop and aren't incentivized to spin â€” they give the most candid read on culture, hours, and the actual day-to-day. Ask them your "stupid questions" so you don't waste loop time on them.
- **Read recent company + industry news.** Don't get blindsided by the deal/launch/headline they were in this week. Walk in current.
