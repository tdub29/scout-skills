# Duhigg: Deep Questions and Responsive Reciprocity

## The Framework

> Deep questions ask people to describe beliefs, values, feelings, and experiences in ways that reveal something vulnerable. (Guide Part III)

Duhigg uses the Fast Friends Procedure and related research to show a cycle: deep questions create vulnerability; vulnerability triggers emotional contagion; emotional contagion helps people align. But connection requires reciprocity that is responsive, not performative.

### Three Sub-Rules

**1. Convert facts into values, feelings, judgments, or experiences.**

Shallow: "Where do you work?" Deep: "What's the best part of your job?" / "What part of your work changed how you see yourself?"

**2. Let the question feel like sharing.**

A deep question should disclose something about the asker too: curiosity, care, a willingness to enter the same territory. If it feels like a survey, interrogation, or trick, it fails.

**3. Reciprocate without stealing the spotlight.**

Reciprocity is not "you shared a painful story, so now I tell a bigger painful story." It is being responsive to the other person's needs. Sometimes the right reciprocity is a brief feeling: "I'm really moved hearing that." Sometimes it is asking permission to share.

**4. Use follow-ups as proof of listening.**

Follow-up questions make self-disclosure feel natural instead of self-absorbed: "What made that matter so much?" / "How did you know it changed you?"

## How To Apply

Build a deep-question ladder:

1. **Start with the surface question.** What would people normally ask?
2. **Move one rung deeper.** Ask for preference, value, judgment, feeling, or experience.
3. **Add a follow-up.** Invite the story behind the answer.
4. **Plan your reciprocity.** Decide what you can share briefly if invited or if the moment calls for it.
5. **Set a boundary.** Do not force disclosure; give the person an easy exit.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Interrogation disguised as intimacy.** Asking several deep questions without offering anything of yourself.
- **Spotlight theft.** Responding to their vulnerability with a longer story about you.
- **Overshooting the relationship.** Asking "What do you regret most?" when "What surprised you about that experience?" would fit.
- **Confusing depth with darkness.** Deep questions can be light: pride, joy, perfect days, favorite memories.
- **No follow-up.** The first question opens the door; the follow-up proves you noticed what came through it.
