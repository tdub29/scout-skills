# Rosenberg â€” Five Steps for Conflict Resolution

## The framework

Rosenberg's mediation procedure, drawn from decades of resolving conflicts in war zones, prisons, schools, and families. The sequence works whether you're a third-party mediator or one of the parties in the conflict.

> "Five steps" of conflict resolution (Ch 11, lines 6470â€“6485)

### The five steps

**Step 1: First, express our own needs.** (line 6470)

Before anything else, identify what YOU (or each party) actually needs. Not strategies (specific outcomes), but needs (the underlying value being sought). "I need you to be home by 6" is a strategy; "I need predictability and connection" is the need.

**Step 2: Search for the real needs of the other person, regardless of how they are expressing themselves.** (line 6472)

Underneath their position, demand, complaint, or attack is a need. Find it. This is where [[rosenberg-life-alienating-communication]] translation comes in â€” "you're so controlling" is the form; the need underneath might be autonomy, respect, or trust.

**Step 3: Verify that both parties accurately recognize each other's needs.** (line 6476)

Each party reflects back what they hear the other needing. Not strategies, not feelings â€” needs. Until each party can accurately name the other's needs, no resolution is possible. This step is the one most mediators skip; it's also the one that does most of the work.

**Step 4: Provide empathy as required to enable both parties to hear each other accurately.** (line 6481)

If either party is too activated to hear the other's needs, you (the mediator or self) provide empathy first. Sometimes Step 4 happens in the middle of Step 3 â€” somebody can't reflect because they're still in pain. Empathize with them; then return to verification. See [[rosenberg-empathic-receiving]].

**Step 5: Propose strategies in positive action language.** (line 6485)

ONLY after both parties have accurately heard each other's needs do you move to strategies. Strategies must be:
- **Positive** â€” what someone DOES do, not what they don't.
- **Action language** â€” specific, doable, present-tense.

If proposed strategies get rejected, return to Step 2 or 3 â€” there's a need that hasn't surfaced yet.

### The "no" translation

When someone says no (to a proposal, request, or strategy), translate it: every no is a yes to a different need. (Lines 6850â€“6865.) "No, I won't come home by 6" might be a yes to autonomy, or to a project they're absorbed in, or to relief from anxiety. The mediator's job is to surface what the no IS saying yes to.

### Why this order matters

Most conflict-resolution attempts skip to Step 5. People try to negotiate strategies before they understand needs. The strategies fail because they don't actually address what each party needs. Steps 1-4 are what makes Step 5 possible.

## How to apply

When mediating or in a conflict:

1. **Step 1 (express own needs):** If you're a party â€” get clear on your need before opening your mouth. Not your strategy. The need. Practice saying it aloud.

2. **Step 2 (find their need):** What's underneath their position? Often you have to ask: "Help me understand â€” what's really important to you about this?" Reach past the strategy/position to the need.

3. **Step 3 (mutual recognition):**
   - To party B: "What do you hear party A needing?"
   - To party A: "Is that what you need?" Correct if needed.
   - Then reverse: "Party A, what do you hear party B needing?"
   - "Party B, is that it?"
   - Don't move on until both are accurate.

4. **Step 4 (empathy as needed):** If at any point either party is too activated to hear, empathize first. "I can hear how painful this has been for you. Sounds like you've been needing..." (See [[rosenberg-empathic-receiving]].)

5. **Step 5 (strategies):** "Given what we now both understand each party needs, what could we DO that would meet both? â€” specific, doable action."
   - If a strategy is rejected, ask the rejecter: "What about that didn't work for you? What need of yours wasn't being met?"
   - Back to Step 2 or 3.

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping Step 1 â€” defaulting straight into solution mode.** "Let's find a compromise" said before either party has named their actual need.
- **Skipping Step 3 â€” assuming each party "gets" the other.** They almost never do. Force the reflection step.
- **Confusing strategies with needs.** "I need you to apologize" is a strategy. The need is being seen, validated, or having your hurt acknowledged. Multiple strategies could meet the need.
- **Pushing past Step 4 too fast.** If someone is still in pain, they CANNOT hear the other side. Empathy isn't a delay â€” it's enabling Step 3 to work.
- **Mediator imposing solutions in Step 5.** The mediator's job isn't to propose strategies â€” it's to set up the conditions where the PARTIES can propose strategies that meet both needs. Mediator-imposed solutions are demands wearing mediator clothing.
- **Treating "win-win" as the goal.** The goal isn't a 50/50 compromise. The goal is strategies that meet both parties' actual needs. Sometimes one party needs 90% and the other needs 10% â€” and the strategy reflects that. Other times the needs are creatively reconcilable in ways neither party initially imagined.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

Conflict mediation has structural power dynamics. Three guardrails:

- **Power asymmetries don't disappear in NVC mediation.** If one party has structural power (boss/employee, parent/child, husband/wife in certain cultures), the framework alone doesn't level the field. The mediator may need to do more empathy work with the lower-power party to prevent the process from being weaponized.
- **Don't use the framework to bypass safety.** If there's abuse, violence, or coercion, NVC mediation is NOT appropriate. Safety first; mediation second.
- **Watch for "false-needs" extraction.** Skilled manipulators can name needs they don't actually have. The mediator's check: do the strategies they propose actually serve the stated need? If not, the stated need wasn't the real one.
- **The point isn't to keep parties together at all costs.** Sometimes the resolution is "we both need X, and we can't get X together â€” here's how we separate well." That's a valid Step 5 outcome.
