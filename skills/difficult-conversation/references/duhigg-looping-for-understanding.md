# Duhigg: Looping for Understanding

## The Framework

> Ask questions. Repeat back in your own words. Ask if you got it right. Continue until everyone agrees we understand. (Guide Part III)

Looping is not parroting. It is a verification protocol for human understanding. The goal is to distill the other person's thought or feeling in your own language, show effort, invite correction, and repeat until the other person recognizes their meaning in your words.

### Three Sub-Rules

**1. Ask before paraphrasing.**

Start with curiosity: "Can I make sure I understand?" / "What matters most about this for you?" / "When you say X, what are you hoping I hear?"

**2. Paraphrase meaning, not wording.**

Do not echo their sentence. Compress the point: "It sounds like the delay itself matters, but the bigger issue is that you felt blindsided."

**3. Ask for correction explicitly.**

"Did I get that right?" / "What am I missing?" / "Is there a better way to say it?" The correction is the work, not an interruption.

**4. Continue until alignment is visible.**

One loop is often not enough, especially in conflict. Stop only when they say some version of "yes, that's it."

## How To Apply

Draft a loop script:

1. **Opening permission:** "Can I check that I'm understanding you?"
2. **Clarifying question:** Ask about stakes, feelings, or desired outcome.
3. **Paraphrase:** Restate the point in your own words.
4. **Accuracy check:** Ask if you got it right.
5. **Correction loop:** Incorporate correction and paraphrase again.
6. **Transition:** Only then answer, ask permission, or share your view.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Parrot loop.** Repeating their words without showing you understand the meaning.
- **One-and-done loop.** Asking "Did I get that right?" but ignoring the correction.
- **Defensive paraphrase.** "So you're saying I never do anything right?" is escalation, not understanding.
- **Premature answer.** Moving to advice, defense, or solution before the other person confirms.
- **Performative listening.** Using the loop as a tactic to get your turn, not to be changed by what you hear.
