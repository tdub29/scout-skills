# Rosenberg â€” From Emotional Slavery to Emotional Liberation

## The framework

Rosenberg's three-stage developmental model for taking responsibility for our own feelings (and ONLY our own).

> "From emotional slavery to emotional liberation." (Ch 5, line 2781)

### The three stages

**Stage 1: Emotional Slavery.** (line 2784)
- "We believe ourselves responsible for the feelings of others." (line 2784)
- We try to make others happy; if they're not happy, we feel responsible and try harder.
- Often shows up early in romantic relationships, in caregivers, in people-pleasers.
- Tip-off phrases in your own head: "If I just say it differently they won't be upset." "I shouldn't have brought it up â€” now they're hurt and it's my fault."
- Cost: exhaustion, loss of self, resentment building underneath.

**Stage 2: The Obnoxious Stage.** (line 2829)
- The pendulum swing. "We tend toward obnoxious comments like 'That's your problem! I'm not responsible for your feelings!'" (line 2829)
- We refuse to take responsibility for anything that ISN'T strictly ours â€” including things that genuinely call for empathy or compromise.
- Often follows a period of burnout from Stage 1. Necessary stage, but harsh.
- Tip-off phrases: "Not my problem." "Sounds like a you issue." "I don't care if you're upset."

**Stage 3: Emotional Liberation.** (line 2876)
- "We respond to the needs of others out of compassion, never out of fear, guilt, or shame." (line 2876)
- We accept responsibility for our own intentions and actions, but not for the feelings of others.
- We give to others because we want to â€” and we can say no cleanly when we don't.
- We empathize with others' pain without being responsible for fixing it.

The core principle running through all three: **the other's action is the stimulus, not the cause, of your feeling. The cause is your own unmet need.**

## How to apply

When you notice a recurring pattern of giving with resentment, saying yes you'll regret, or swinging between extremes:

1. **Locate your stage in this specific relationship.** People can be at Stage 3 with most people and Stage 1 with their mother, or Stage 2 with their boss and Stage 3 with friends. It's relationship-specific.

2. **If Stage 1 (emotional slavery):**
   - Notice when you're moving to soothe someone's feelings as if their feelings were yours to manage.
   - Try: "I see you're feeling [X]. I'm not feeling that's mine to fix â€” what would help YOU right now?"
   - Practice tolerating their discomfort without rushing to repair.

3. **If Stage 2 (obnoxious):**
   - Recognize that the "not my problem" defense is a step forward from slavery but it's not the destination.
   - The need: distinguish between being RESPONSIBLE for their feelings (you're not) and CARING about their feelings (you can).
   - Try: "I can see you're hurting. I don't think I caused it, and I'm also not willing to ignore it. What do you need right now?"

4. **For Stage 3 (liberation):**
   - Give because you want to, not because guilt demands it.
   - Say no without elaborate justification.
   - When saying yes, mean it. When saying no, mean it.
   - Tested by: how do you feel an hour after the yes? An hour after the no? Liberation feels clean either way.

5. **Use the "you-make-me-feel" filter.** Anytime you (or someone you're with) uses "you make me feel...," that's emotional-slavery language. Translate: the feeling comes from an unmet need; the other's action is the stimulus, not the cause.

## Common failure modes

- **Confusing Stage 2 with Stage 3.** "I'm just being honest" can be liberation OR obnoxious-stage callousness. The test: is there empathy present alongside the boundary?
- **Pretending to be at Stage 3 while still doing Stage 1.** Saying "I'm choosing this" while internally resenting. Watch the resentment â€” that's the tell.
- **Using the framework to dismiss.** "Sounds like you're in Stage 1, sorry not sorry" â€” Rosenberg's labels weaponized.
- **Believing Stage 3 means never being affected by others.** It doesn't. You still feel; you just locate the feeling in your need, not in their behavior.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

This framework has a darker misuse: justifying neglect or harm by claiming "your feelings are your responsibility, not mine." That's Stage 2 dressed up in Stage 3 vocabulary. Three checks:

- **Empathy is not enmeshment.** Caring about someone's feelings is not the same as being responsible for them. Stage 3 keeps both â€” care AND responsibility located correctly.
- **Don't use the framework to shut someone down.** "Your feelings are your responsibility" said in the middle of their pain is technically true and emotionally cruel.
- **Watch the asymmetry.** If you're using "I'm not responsible for your feelings" with intimates while demanding they manage yours â€” that's not liberation. That's exploitation with a label.
