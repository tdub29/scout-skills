# Crucial Conversations â€” AMPP (Exploring Others' Paths)

## The framework

> "AMPP: Ask, Mirror, Paraphrase, Prime â€” four powerful listening skills to help you Explore Others' Paths." (Ch 8)

When the OTHER person has stopped sharing meaning â€” either by going silent or by attacking â€” straight argument won't restore dialogue. AMPP is the four-tool sequence for inviting them back in.

### The four tools, in escalating-invitation order

**A â€” Ask**

Genuinely. The simplest version, used when there's enough safety that asking might work.

> "Ask for the other person's thoughts, feelings, and concerns. Use genuine curiosity, not disguised criticism."

Sample language: "What's your take?" "What am I missing?" "I'd really like to know how you see this."

Test: is your tone genuinely inviting, or is "what's your problem?" leaking through? People can hear the difference.

**M â€” Mirror**

When Ask doesn't get a real answer because the person's body language is screaming something their words aren't, MIRROR what you see.

> "Acknowledge the gap between what they're saying and what their tone or body language is showing. Reflect that observation, gently."

Sample language: "You say it's fine, but you sound frustrated." "You're saying you agree, and your face looks tense." "You're nodding, but you haven't said anything in the last 10 minutes â€” what's going on?"

Mirror is observation-only â€” no interpretation yet. You name what you're seeing/hearing and let them fill in the meaning.

**P â€” Paraphrase**

When the person HAS shared something but you're not sure you understand the deeper concern, paraphrase what you heard.

> "Put their meaning into your own words to check understanding. Go beyond surface meaning to understand the deeper concern."

Sample language: "So what you're really worried about is X â€” is that right?" "It sounds like the bigger issue isn't Y, it's Z."

Paraphrase keeps inviting deeper sharing because each correct guess validates them and each wrong guess invites correction.

**P â€” Prime**

When ALL of the above haven't moved them out of silence, GUESS what they might be thinking and offer it as a starting point.

> "When someone won't open up, prime the pump by guessing what they might be feeling or thinking. Make a guess and offer it for confirmation or correction."

Sample language: "Are you maybe feeling like I'm not taking this seriously?" "I'm wondering if you're worried that I'll react badly if you tell me?"

Priming is the last resort because you're putting words in their mouth. Use only when Ask + Mirror + Paraphrase haven't worked AND only with explicit invitation to correct ("does that match? Or am I off?").

### The escalation logic

Each tool requires MORE of you (more risk, more vulnerability, more potential to be wrong) and FEWER of them (less they have to volunteer). Don't skip to Prime when Ask would have worked. Don't stay at Ask when they need Priming to start.

### What to do AFTER AMPP

Once they've started sharing, you're back in dialogue. From here:
- If you AGREE with their view, use [[cc-abc]] (Agree-Build-Compare) to integrate.
- If you DISAGREE, you'll add your view via [[cc-state]].

## How to apply

When the other person is in silence or violence:

1. **Pause your advocacy.** You can't share your view if they've left the conversation. AMPP first.

2. **Start with Ask.** Genuine, curious tone. If they share, great â€” go to next step (probably ABC or STATE).

3. **If their answer is hollow ("fine," "whatever"), Mirror.** Name the gap between their words and body language.

4. **When they share something but you sense more underneath, Paraphrase.** Reach for the deeper concern.

5. **If they STILL won't share, Prime.** Offer a guess at what might be going on and invite correction.

6. **Stop AMPP-ing when they're genuinely engaged again.** The point is restoring dialogue, not extracting confession.

## Common failure modes

- **Asking with a tone of accusation.** "WELL, what's YOUR view?" Same words; opposite effect. Tone is half the work.
- **Mirroring as ammunition.** "You SAY you're fine but you're CLEARLY angry" weaponized to win. Mirror is gentle observation, not gotcha.
- **Paraphrasing too eagerly.** "So what you're saying is..." used to put words in their mouth that make them sound worse than they did. Paraphrase has to be GENUINE attempts at understanding, not strategic reframing.
- **Priming as projection.** "I bet you're feeling threatened by my success" â€” that's not priming, that's a story. Priming is humble guessing with explicit "or am I wrong?"
- **AMPP without first being SAFE.** If the other person is in violence because they don't trust you, AMPP will sound like manipulation. May need [[cc-crib]] (restoring Mutual Purpose) or apology/Contrasting (restoring Mutual Respect) FIRST.
- **AMPP-ing into a story you've already concluded.** The whole point is to genuinely learn their path. If you've already decided what they think, you're not inquiring; you're confirming.

## Inquiry-integrity check

AMPP is among the highest-leverage NVC-adjacent skills, and among the most-abused. Three guardrails:

- **Genuine curiosity is non-optional.** All four tools assume you actually want to know what they think. Faking it is detectable instantly; the form without the substance trains them to trust you less.
- **AMPP isn't an interrogation method.** "What's REALLY going on" with a leading edge is closer to interrogation than inquiry. The test: are you opening space, or pushing toward a predetermined answer?
- **Stop when invited to.** If someone says "I don't want to talk about it," that's their answer to your Ask. Respecting it builds more trust than pressing for the "deeper" answer.
