# Duhigg: Social Identity Bridge

## The Framework

> All of us have a personal identity and a social identity: how we see ourselves, and how we believe others see us, as members of various tribes. (Chapter 6)

Duhigg's "Who are we?" conversations reveal how identities shape perception. The practical move is not to pretend differences are irrelevant. It is to invite people to describe the identities that matter, reciprocate with your own, avoid one-dimensionality, and find an identity that can be shared.

### Three Sub-Rules

**1. Draw out backgrounds and allegiances.**

Ask about where people come from, what communities shaped them, what roles matter, and what experiences influence their view.

**2. Reciprocate identity, not just information.**

Share how you see yourself in a relevant way: "As a parent..." "As someone who grew up around..." "As a newcomer to this field..."

**3. Avoid one-dimensionality.**

No one is only "lawyer," "anti-vaxxer," "executive," "Gen Z," "rural voter," or "engineer." Evoke multiple identities: "As a lawyer you may see X, but as a parent do you also worry about Y?"

**4. Look for a shared identity that can carry the conversation.**

Shared parent, neighbor, teammate, professional, citizen, learner, or problem-solver identities can make contested facts hearable.

## How To Apply

Build a social-identity bridge map:

1. **Name the active labels.** What identities are currently loud?
2. **List possible hidden identities.** Roles, histories, communities, aspirations.
3. **Identify one-dimensionality traps.**
4. **Write two identity questions.**
5. **Prepare your own reciprocal identity statement.**
6. **Find a shared identity or shared obligation.**
7. **Bridge from identity to topic.**

## Common Failure Modes

- **Color-blind / identity-blind bypass.** "Let's ignore labels" often ignores the very thing shaping the conversation.
- **One-dimensional labeling.** Treating someone as only a member of a disliked group.
- **Weaponized identity questions.** Asking "as a mother, how can you..." is a trap, not bridge-building.
- **No reciprocity.** Asking someone to reveal identity while you remain abstract or neutral.
- **False shared identity.** Claiming commonality that the other person does not recognize.
