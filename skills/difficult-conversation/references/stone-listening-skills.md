# Stone/Patton/Heen â€” Three Listening Skills

## The framework

> "In addition to the stance of curiosity, there are three primary skills that good listeners employ: inquiry, paraphrasing, and acknowledgment." (Ch 9, lines 5607â€“5608)

Curiosity is the stance; these three are the moves. Each addresses a different listening failure.

### 1. Inquiry â€” ask to learn

> "Inquire to Learn." (Ch 9, lines 5610â€“5761)

Genuine open questions, not disguised assertions. Sub-rules:

- **Don't disguise assertions as questions.** "Don't you think you should have...?" is a statement; "How did you decide on that approach?" is a question.
- **Don't cross-examine.** A series of pointed questions designed to corner them isn't inquiry; it's prosecution.
- **Ask open-ended questions.** Not "did you mean to be dismissive?" (yes/no); "What was going on for you in that moment?" (open).
- **Ask for concrete information.** "What did you mean by X?" beats "Can you elaborate?"
- **Ask about the Three Conversations.** Their facts/interpretation; their feelings; what's at stake for them.

### 2. Paraphrasing â€” check and show

> "Paraphrase for Clarity." (Ch 9, lines 5780â€“5864)

State back what you heard, in your own words. Two purposes:
- **For you:** check your understanding before responding.
- **For them:** confirm you actually heard.

Sub-rules:
- Paraphrase the SUBSTANCE, not just the words. "So what you're saying is..." should capture meaning, not parrot.
- Paraphrase FEELINGS when feelings are present. "So you were really hurt by that?" beats just paraphrasing the situation.
- Acknowledge what's UNSAID. Sometimes the meaning is between the lines â€” paraphrase to surface it ("It sounds like you might also be worried that...").

### 3. Acknowledgment â€” name the feeling

> "Acknowledge their feelings." (Ch 9, lines 5865â€“5964)

Specifically acknowledging the feeling, not just the content. Most listeners stop at "I understand what you're saying" â€” which addresses content, not feeling. Acknowledgment goes further: "That sounds really painful." "I can hear how frustrating that's been."

Sub-rules:

- **Acknowledgment â‰  agreement.** "I hear that you're angry" doesn't admit they're right to be. It just names that they ARE.
- **Answer invisible questions.** They're often asking implicitly: "Does this matter? Am I being heard? Do you care?" Acknowledgment answers yes to all three.
- **Order matters** (lines 5909â€“5931). Acknowledge BEFORE responding with your view. The feeling has to land first.
- **The right level of intensity.** A casual "yeah, that sucks" for a deep grief doesn't acknowledge. A grave tone for a small annoyance is overdoing.

## How to apply

In a difficult conversation:

1. **Lead with curiosity (the stance).** Without it, the three skills become technique.

2. **Inquire.** Open question. Specific. About what THEY know/think/feel, not pre-loaded with your conclusion.

3. **Paraphrase.** "So what you're saying is..." Capture substance and (if present) feeling. Confirm.

4. **Acknowledge the feeling.** "That sounds [hard / painful / frustrating / scary]." Use the right intensity.

5. **Cycle.** Often you'll do Inquire â†’ Paraphrase â†’ Acknowledge â†’ Inquire again as the person opens up further.

6. **THEN share your view.** After they've felt heard, your perspective will land. Skipping to your view first means it bounces off.

## Common failure modes

- **Inquiry as cross-examination.** Pointed questions designed to surface contradictions in their view. Detectable; ends conversation.
- **Paraphrase as parrot.** Repeating their words verbatim. Reads as condescension or buying time. The paraphrase needs to capture meaning.
- **Acknowledging content but skipping feeling.** "So you're saying the deadline was tight" without "and I can hear how stressful that was." Listener doesn't feel HEARD, just understood.
- **Acknowledgment as agreement.** "Yes you're right to be upset, I was wrong." Acknowledgment doesn't require taking the blame. You can acknowledge their feeling AND hold your different view.
- **Cycling endlessly without sharing your view.** Stone/Patton/Heen are clear: listen FIRST, then share. Permanent listening = withholding your view = its own form of dishonesty.
- **Acknowledgment delivered with impatience.** "Yes I HEAR you're frustrated" through gritted teeth. The form is right; the substance is gone.

## Integrity check

This framework can be deployed mechanically â€” moves done without curiosity. Three guardrails:

- **All three serve understanding, not management.** If you're using these to "handle" someone rather than understand them, they'll detect it.
- **Acknowledgment isn't an exit ramp.** "I hear you're upset" said to MOVE PAST their upset. Genuine acknowledgment lets the feeling be there.
- **Listen, then express â€” both halves.** Don't use listening as a permanent avoidance of sharing your view. The chapter is paired with [[stone-expressing-with-clarity]] for a reason.
