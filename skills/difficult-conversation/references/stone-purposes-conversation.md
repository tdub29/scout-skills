# Stone/Patton/Heen â€” The Three Purposes That Work

## The framework

> "What's Your Purpose? When to Raise It and When to Let Go" (Ch 7)

Most failed difficult conversations fail because the purpose was wrong from the start. Stone/Patton/Heen identify three purposes that sabotage and three that enable.

### Three purposes that DON'T work

> Three purposes that sabotage learning (Ch 7, lines 4330â€“4500):

**1. Change the other person**

> Entering with the agenda to fix, convert, or reshape them. Triggers defensiveness; closes learning. They'll resist even if you're right.

**2. Get them to admit fault**

> Seeking confession or capitulation. About winning, not understanding. The conversation becomes prosecution; they become defendant. Even if you "win," you lose the relationship.

**3. Vent and get sympathy**

> Using the conversation as emotional outlet. You feel temporarily better; they feel blamed and exhausted. Nothing changes. The pattern repeats.

### Three purposes that DO work

> Three productive purposes (Ch 7, lines 4500â€“4700):

**1. Learn their story**

> Genuine curiosity. What do they see that you don't? What's their reasoning? What constraints are they operating under?

**2. Express your story and perspective**

> Clear, honest communication of how things look from your side â€” without demanding they agree.

**3. Problem-solve together**

> Focus forward. Given that you each see it differently, what works for both of you going forward? How do we prevent recurrence?

### The "when to let go" half

The chapter title includes "When to Let Go" for a reason. Sometimes the right answer is NOT to have the conversation:

- You're not actually willing to learn anything (you've decided).
- The other person isn't safe / available / capable of engaging.
- The relationship is ending anyway and raising it serves no purpose.
- You're using "having the conversation" as a way of staying in conflict instead of moving on.

If your only purpose is to vent, change, or extract admission â€” and you can't shift â€” the most mature move may be: don't raise it.

## How to apply

Before opening:

1. **Write down your purposes honestly.** What do you ACTUALLY want from this conversation? Be honest with yourself.
2. **Check against the don't-work list.** Are any of your purposes really "change them," "get them to admit fault," or "vent"?
3. **If yes, can you reframe?** Can you genuinely shift to learning their story, expressing yours cleanly, and problem-solving together?
4. **If you can't shift â€” let go OR sit with it longer.** Don't open a conversation whose only available purposes are sabotaging ones. Either work on yourself until productive purposes are available, or accept you're not having this conversation.

During the conversation:

1. **Notice drift.** When you find yourself rehearsing "the gotcha" or starting to vent â€” pause.
2. **Re-anchor to one of the three productive purposes.** "My purpose here is to understand your view AND share mine AND figure out where we go from here."
3. **Redirect out loud if needed.** "I'm noticing we're drifting toward who's at fault. I'd rather focus on how we move forward."

## Common failure modes

- **Lying to yourself about purpose.** "I just want to understand" said when you've already decided the verdict. Run the honest check.
- **Multiple purposes muddled.** "I want them to admit they were wrong AND learn their story." The admit-fault purpose poisons the learning purpose. Pick one.
- **Treating purpose-check as one-time.** Purposes drift mid-conversation. Re-check.
- **Refusing to let go.** "But I HAVE to have this conversation." Often you don't. Sometimes the most powerful move is acceptance, not engagement.
- **Letting go as avoidance.** "I'll just let it go" used to dodge the conversation you should have. Honest assessment: are you letting go from peace or from cowardice?
- **Confusing "express your story" with "convince them."** Step 2 is expressing â€” not selling, not converting. They may hear you and disagree. That's allowed.

## Integrity check

This framework can be used to gaslight yourself out of legitimate grievances ("I just need to learn their story, not raise that they hurt me"). Three guardrails:

- **Expressing your story IS valid.** Don't translate every legitimate concern into "learning theirs." Both are equally weighted.
- **Some conversations have to happen.** Letting go isn't always available. If silence has costs, you have to engage, even if your purposes aren't pristine.
- **Productive purposes don't make you nice; they make you effective.** You can pursue learning + expressing + problem-solving while still being firm. They're not soft purposes.
