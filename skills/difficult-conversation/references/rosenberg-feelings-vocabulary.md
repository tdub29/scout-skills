# Rosenberg â€” Feelings Vocabulary (Real Feelings vs. Pseudo-Feelings)

## The framework

A precise feelings vocabulary is the load-bearing skill of NVC's second component. Without it, "I feel..." statements collapse into thoughts, judgments, or interpretations of the other person's behavior â€” and the listener responds to the judgment, not the feeling.

> "Building a vocabulary for feelings." (Ch 4, line 2099)

### Three distinctions you have to make

**1. Feeling vs. thought.** Words like *that, like, as if* following "feel" signal thought, not feeling (lines 2001â€“2004).
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **that** you don't care."
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **like** I'm being ignored."
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **as if** I'm not important."
- Real feeling underneath: lonely, hurt, frustrated, sad.

**2. Feeling vs. what we think we are.** Self-evaluation in feeling clothing (lines 2015â€“2016).
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel inadequate as a guitarist."
- Real feeling underneath: disappointed, impatient, frustrated, anxious.

**3. Feeling vs. how we think others react toward us.** Interpretation of the other person's behavior (lines 2039â€“2040).
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **ignored**." (= "I think you ignored me")
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **misunderstood**." (= "I think you didn't understand me")
- Pseudo-feeling: "I feel **unwanted, used, criticized, manipulated, betrayed, attacked**." (all interpretations)
- Real feeling underneath: lonely, hurt, sad, angry, scared.

## How to apply

When you're about to say "I feel...":

1. **Check the next word.** If it's *that, like, as if,* or a pronoun (you, he, she, they) â€” STOP. You're about to express a thought, not a feeling.

2. **If you used an "interpretation feeling" (ignored, attacked, manipulated, betrayed, abandoned, used) â€” translate.** Ask: "When I felt [interpretation], what was the underneath feeling?" Usually: lonely, scared, hurt, sad, angry.

3. **Reach for the unmet-need list when in distress; the needs-met list when expressing appreciation.** Most people only know 5â€“10 feeling words; both lists give you 75â€“100.

4. **State the feeling as one word.** "I feel hurt." Not "I feel hurt because you...". The cause comes next, in the need.

5. **Link to need, not to the other person's behavior.** "I feel hurt because I have a need for understanding," not "I feel hurt because you said X." Their action is the stimulus; your unmet need is the cause. See [[rosenberg-emotional-liberation-stages]] for the responsibility-for-feelings principle.

## Common failure modes

- **"I feel that you..." stays.** This is the #1 NVC error. Drop "that"; replace with a real feeling word.
- **Interpretation feelings stay.** "I feel rejected" â€” get to the feeling underneath. Lonely? Sad? Hurt?
- **Multi-word "feelings" that are stories.** "I feel like a doormat" â€” that's a self-concept, not a feeling. Hurt, resentful, helpless.
- **Refusing the list.** "All those feeling words seem touchy-feely" is the curse of a thin vocabulary. The list is a vocabulary, not a stylistic choice.
- **Feeling-stacking to manipulate.** "I feel hurt, sad, betrayed, abandoned, devastated..." piling up to amplify guilt. Pick the actual one.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

Feeling-language is powerfully evocative â€” which is exactly why it's tempting to weaponize. Three integrity tests:

- **Feelings reveal, they don't bill.** "I feel hurt" is information about you. "I feel hurt **because of what you did**" is a bill you're sending. The feeling vocabulary is for revealing your state, not for proving someone wronged you.
- **No false amplification.** Saying "devastated" when "disappointed" is accurate distorts the signal and corrodes trust.
- **You stay responsible for the feeling.** Their action is the stimulus, not the cause. The cause is your unmet need.
