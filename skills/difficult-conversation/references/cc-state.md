# Crucial Conversations â€” STATE My Path

## The framework

> "STATE My Path" (Ch 7, line 4820)

The advocacy framework â€” how to put your view into the pool of shared meaning without forcing it. STATE is paired with [[cc-ampp]] (inquiry). Together they're the two halves of dialogue: sharing your path and exploring theirs.

> Sample acronym: "STATE: Share your facts, Tell your story, Ask for others' paths, Talk tentatively, Encourage testing." (Ch 7)

### The five steps

**S â€” Share your facts**

Start with the **least controversial, most persuasive** information: the facts. What you saw, heard, observed. Not your interpretation; the raw observable data.

> "Begin from the facts â€” not your story about the facts." (Ch 7)

Example: "I've noticed the last three reports came in two days after the deadline." Not: "You're missing deadlines again."

Why facts first: they're the foundation both parties can agree on. Starting with your story creates immediate defensiveness because the listener hasn't heard the basis.

**T â€” Tell your story**

NOW share the meaning you've made of those facts â€” your interpretation, your concern.

> "Explain what your story is about the facts."

Example: "...and I've started to wonder if there's a workload issue or whether something else is going on."

The crucial property: this is YOUR story. Not "the truth." It's an inference from the facts, and you label it as such.

**A â€” Ask for others' paths**

After sharing your facts and story, INVITE the other person to share theirs.

> "Invite the other person to share their path."

Example: "I'd really like to hear how you see it." "What am I missing here?"

Not optional and not perfunctory. Their facts and story may completely change the picture.

**T â€” Talk tentatively**

Throughout the above, soften the language. Use tentative phrasing.

> "I'm wondering if..." "It seems to me..." "Could it be that..." "I might be off-base, but..."

Why: tentative language signals you're offering a perspective, not pronouncing a verdict. This is the single most-violated rule. People share strong views with absolute language and trigger defensiveness; the SAME view in tentative language gets a hearing.

**E â€” Encourage testing**

Invite the listener to disagree, push back, or correct you.

> "Make it safe for them to express doubts about your path." "Encourage them to poke holes in your thinking."

Example: "What do you think â€” am I seeing this wrong?" "Tell me where I might be missing context."

Without Step E, the conversation tilts into one-sided advocacy. With it, you've explicitly opened the door for them to add to or correct your picture.

### The order is load-bearing

S before T is the single most-skipped property. People start with their interpretation ("you're being disrespectful") instead of the fact ("when I'd just finished speaking and you turned to your phone"). The interpretation IS what your STATE wants to express â€” but only AFTER the facts that produced it.

## How to apply

Before opening:

1. **Write the facts.** What did you actually see/hear? Strip evaluation. ([[rosenberg-observation-without-evaluation]] is the deep-dive on this.)
2. **Write your story.** What meaning have you made of those facts? Be honest â€” including the possibility that your story is wrong.
3. **Draft tentative language for the story.** "I'm wondering whether..." "It looks to me like..."
4. **Prepare your invitation.** What question will you use to invite their path?
5. **Prepare to genuinely hear them.** Step A only works if you're open to having your story revised.

In the conversation:

1. **Open with facts (S).** Pause to let them land.
2. **Tell your story tentatively (T-T).** "I've been wondering if X..."
3. **Ask for their path (A).** "How do you see it?"
4. **Listen.** Use [[cc-ampp]].
5. **Encourage testing (E).** "Push back on me if I'm getting this wrong."

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping S.** Starting with your story-or-conclusion. "You've been disengaged" without the observable evidence. The listener has nothing to anchor to.
- **Skipping the tentative language.** STATE in absolute tone. "Here are the facts. Here's what they mean. What do you say?" delivered with conviction = subtle violence. The tentative language is structural, not stylistic.
- **Asking but not actually wanting to hear.** Step A done as a courtesy: "What do you think?" with no openness to a story-revising answer. The listener picks up on the lack of openness instantly.
- **Treating Step E as optional.** Adding "any thoughts?" at the end like a footer. Step E is genuine â€” "I might be wrong about this, push back."
- **Stories that disguise as facts.** "The fact is, you don't care" â€” that's a story, not a fact. The fact is "you didn't respond to my emails." The story is "you don't care." Don't smuggle.
- **Tentative language deployed to soften manipulation.** "I'm just wondering if perhaps you could possibly consider whether maybe..." â€” sycophantic over-softening that masks underlying demand. Tentative is honest hedging, not performative weakness.

## Speech-integrity check

STATE can be performed without genuine openness, becoming "concessions theater" â€” sounding tentative while staying locked in. Three guardrails:

- **You have to actually be willing to update.** If your STATE is internally framed as "say it gently so they accept it," you're using the form to package a demand. Real STATE has real openness.
- **Tentative language matches tentative beliefs.** If you're 95% sure of your story, don't say "I'm just wondering." Say "I'm quite sure" â€” and STILL invite testing. Calibrated confidence beats theatrical doubt.
- **The facts-first rule isn't an excuse to bury concerns.** "I just have some facts to share" said for 20 minutes while the actual concern stays unspoken is its own avoidance. The facts are setup for your story, not a replacement for it.
