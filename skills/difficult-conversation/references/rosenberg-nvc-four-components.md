# Rosenberg â€” NVC Four Components (Observation, Feeling, Need, Request)

## The framework

Rosenberg's parent model. Every clear, non-violent statement of impact contains four pieces, and people skip one of them at their peril.

> "The four components of NVC: 1. observations 2. feelings 3. needs 4. requests" (Ch 1, line 804)

The components in operation:

1. **Observation** â€” what you actually see/hear, with NO evaluation mixed in. "Articulate this observation without introducing any judgment or evaluation â€” to simply say what people are doing that we either like or don't like." (line 797)

2. **Feeling** â€” your emotional state when you observe this. "State how we feel when we observe this action: are we hurt, scared, joyful, amused, irritated?" (line 799)

3. **Need** â€” the value/need of yours that's connected to that feeling. "Say what needs of ours are connected to the feelings we have identified." (line 801)

4. **Request** â€” a specific, doable, present-tense ask. "A very specific request: what we are wanting from the other person that would enrich our lives." (line 815)

Rosenberg's canonical example: *"When I see soiled socks on the floor, I feel irritated because I have a need for order in the rooms we share. Would you be willing to put your socks in your room or in the washer?"* (lines 810â€“817)

The model is symmetric. You can EXPRESS using the four components, or you can RECEIVE the same four pieces from the other person: "What are you observing? Feeling? Needing? Requesting?" (line 851 â€” empathic receiving uses the same model in reverse).

## How to apply

When you catch yourself about to say something charged:

1. **Strip the observation.** What did you actually see or hear? Strip out "always," "never," labels ("lazy," "selfish"), and inferences. See [[rosenberg-observation-without-evaluation]] for the 7 traps.

2. **Name the feeling.** Not a thought. "I feel that you don't care" is a thought. "I feel hurt" is a feeling. See [[rosenberg-feelings-vocabulary]] for the pseudo-feelings trap.

3. **Name the need behind it.** Connection, respect, rest, autonomy, contribution. The feeling is the signal; the need is the source. Avoid "you-make-me-feel" framings â€” the other person's action is the stimulus, not the cause.

4. **Make a clear, doable request.** Present tense. Specific action. Open to "no." If you'd react to "no" with punishment, it was a demand. See [[rosenberg-requests-versus-demands]] for the diagnostic.

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping the observation, going straight to feeling+need.** "I feel hurt because I need respect" â€” the listener has no idea what triggered this. Always anchor in the specific observable event.
- **Pseudo-observations dressed as observations.** "When you're being inconsiderate" is a judgment, not an observation. "When I came home and the dishes from last night were still in the sink" is an observation.
- **Feelings that are actually thoughts.** "I feel ignored / manipulated / attacked" â€” these are interpretations of what the OTHER person did. The feeling underneath is usually lonely, frustrated, afraid.
- **Strategy disguised as need.** "I need you to come home by 6" is a strategy. The need is connection, predictability, or family time. Strategies are rigid; needs are flexible (they can be met multiple ways).
- **Demand dressed as request.** Watch what you do if they say no. If you escalate or punish, it was a demand.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

NVC is a compassion practice, not a manipulation technique. Using the four-component grammar **without genuine empathy or honesty** corrupts the practice â€” it becomes "fauxNVC," a politer way to coerce. Three integrity tests before using the model:

- **Connection over compliance:** Is your goal to be heard and to hear the other person, or to win? If it's to win, the form alone won't help.
- **Real openness to no:** Are you actually open to a different answer, or have you decided the outcome and you're just trying to land it gently?
- **No covert evaluation:** "I feel that you're being controlling" is a judgment wearing NVC clothes. The grammar doesn't sanitize the content.
