# Crucial Conversations â€” CRIB (Restoring Mutual Purpose)

## The framework

> "When you find yourself at an impasse with someone, step out of the content and create safety by restoring Mutual Purpose. The acronym CRIB outlines four skills." (Ch 6)

Mutual Purpose is one of the two pre-conditions for dialogue (the other is Mutual Respect). When it's lost, no amount of more-eloquent-arguing helps; the parties literally want different things, and that has to be addressed before anything else can move.

### The four steps

**C â€” Commit to seek Mutual Purpose**

State out loud that you'll stay in dialogue until both parties find a purpose both can support. Not a Fool's Choice between your strategy and theirs â€” a genuine purpose both share.

> Sample language: "I'm committed to staying with this until we find a solution that works for both of us."

This is a verbal commitment, not just an internal one. The other party needs to hear it.

**R â€” Recognize the purpose behind the strategy**

Move from POSITIONS (specific strategies each party is advocating) to PURPOSES (what each party is trying to achieve through their strategy).

> "When you find yourself in a stand-off, pause and ask: What do you really want? What's the goal behind the strategy?"

Example: You want to stay home for the holidays; they want to travel. Position vs. position. The purposes underneath might be: rest (you) and connection with family (them). Once articulated, they're no longer mutually exclusive.

**I â€” Invent a Mutual Purpose**

If the existing purposes still seem incompatible, INVENT a higher-order purpose both can endorse.

> Look for the higher-level goal that encompasses both parties' wants.

Example: Husband wants promotion (more hours), wife wants more family time (fewer hours). Higher purpose both can endorse: "We both want a fulfilling life and a strong family." Now you're working on the same problem.

**B â€” Brainstorm new strategies**

Once you share a purpose, brainstorm strategies neither of you originally considered. Most impasses dissolve here because the original two strategies were never the only options.

> "With a clear Mutual Purpose, you can join forces in searching for a solution that serves everyone."

### When to use CRIB vs. Contrasting

CRIB restores Mutual PURPOSE. When you've lost Mutual RESPECT (the other party thinks you don't care about them as a person), the repair is different â€” usually an apology + Contrasting (a "don't / do" statement clarifying what you DON'T mean and what you DO mean).

## How to apply

When stuck at an impasse:

1. **First, name the moment.** "We seem to want different things, and I'm noticing we're stuck."

2. **C â€” Commit verbally to keep going.** "I want to keep working on this until we find something that works for both of us."

3. **R â€” Ask what they really want.** "Help me understand what you're really trying to accomplish here." Then state yours. Move both parties from positions to purposes.

4. **I â€” If purposes still conflict, look for the higher level.** "Could we both agree that we want [Z]?" where Z is the encompassing goal.

5. **B â€” Brainstorm strategies that serve the mutual purpose.** Generate at least 3 new options before evaluating any.

6. **Pick one to try.** Even an imperfect choice is better than the previous impasse. You can revisit.

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping C and going straight to R.** "Why do you want what you want?" without first committing to keep working. Lands as interrogation.
- **Confusing Mutual Purpose with compromise.** "We both have to give up something" is compromise. Mutual Purpose is a shared goal that makes both parties want to find a solution together.
- **Inventing a fake mutual purpose.** "I'm sure deep down we both want world peace" â€” generic, not actually mutual, doesn't help. The invented purpose has to be specific and genuinely endorsed by both.
- **Brainstorming before establishing mutual purpose.** Generates strategies that one party rejects ("that doesn't solve MY problem").
- **Confusing CRIB with empathy.** CRIB is structural â€” it rebuilds the conversation's foundation. Empathic listening ([[cc-ampp]]) is different and also valuable. Often both are needed.
- **Treating the FIRST mutual purpose found as the answer.** Sometimes you'll need to go a level deeper. "We both want this project to succeed" might be true but too vague; press for what success means to each.

## Purpose-integrity check

CRIB can be misused to extract concession through faux-collaboration. Three guardrails:

- **Real Mutual Purpose, not packaged self-interest.** "Surely you also want me to be happy" is your purpose, dressed as theirs. The mutual purpose has to be genuinely endorsable by both parties on its own merits.
- **Power asymmetries matter.** When one party has structural power (boss, parent, larger institution), "Mutual Purpose" can become "the powerful party's purpose with consent extracted." If the dialogue is between unequals, the powerful party has extra responsibility to surface the other's purpose first.
- **Don't use CRIB to avoid hard truths.** Some impasses are real â€” incompatible values, irreconcilable needs. CRIB helps find shared ground where it exists; it doesn't manufacture ground that isn't there. Sometimes the honest answer is "we want different things."
