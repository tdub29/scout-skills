# Rosenberg â€” Four Options for Hearing a Negative Message

## The framework

When someone criticizes us, blames us, complains about us â€” we typically default to one of two terrible options. Rosenberg names four, two of them productive.

> "Hearing a Negative Message: Four Options" (Ch 5, line 2464)

### The four options

**Option 1: Blame ourselves.** (lines 2482â€“2483)
- "You're right, I'm terrible." "I'm such a failure." Internal anguish, shame, self-attack.
- Buys peace at the cost of self-respect; doesn't address the real issue.

**Option 2: Blame the other person.** (lines 2484â€“2489)
- "You're being unreasonable." "How dare you." "You're projecting."
- Discharges discomfort but creates resentment, defensiveness, and escalation.

**Option 3: Sense our own feelings and needs.** (lines 2490â€“2501)
- Ask: "What am I feeling in response to this feedback? What need of mine is unmet?"
- Example: someone calls you selfish. Instead of self-attack or counter-attack: "I'm feeling hurt because I have a need to be seen for what I do contribute."
- Shifts locus to your own internal experience and unmet needs.

**Option 4: Sense the other person's feelings and needs.** (lines 2502â€“2506)
- Ask: "What is THIS person feeling? What need of theirs is unmet that's coming out as this criticism?"
- Example: someone calls you selfish. "Underneath this, they're feeling lonely and have a need for more time together."
- Opens empathy path; often defuses the criticism by reaching for the underneath.

The two productive options (3 and 4) are both NVC moves. Option 3 is self-empathy; Option 4 is empathic receiving applied to a critic.

## How to apply

When criticized:

1. **Notice the heat.** Tightening in chest, urge to defend or shrink. That's the cue.

2. **Don't respond yet.** Even 2 seconds. The default response is Option 1 or 2.

3. **Pick consciously.** Ask yourself: am I about to blame myself? Blame them? Or can I get to my own feeling/need (3), or theirs (4)?

4. **For Option 3 (self-empathy):**
   - What am I feeling right now? (Use the [[rosenberg-feelings-vocabulary]].)
   - What need of mine is unmet that this criticism stirs? (Recognition? Trust? Fairness? Autonomy?)
   - Say something like: "When I hear that, I notice I'm feeling X because I have a need for Y."

5. **For Option 4 (empathy for the critic):**
   - What might THEY be feeling? (Hurt, scared, frustrated, exhausted?)
   - What need of theirs is unmet that's expressed as this criticism?
   - Reflect: "It sounds like you're feeling X because you'd really like Y." See [[rosenberg-empathic-receiving]].

6. **Either can defuse the moment.** Option 3 keeps you whole; Option 4 reaches across.

## Common failure modes

- **Performing Option 4 to avoid Option 3.** Reflexively going to "they must be hurting" to skip examining your own activated feeling. Both have a place.
- **Doing Option 3 silently and Option 1 out loud.** "I'm feeling hurt" thought internally; "you're right, I'm terrible" said aloud. The work has to surface.
- **Using Option 4 as condescension.** "I can see you're really struggling right now" as a sneer. The listener detects it instantly.
- **Picking the option that wins, not the one that fits.** Sometimes the criticism IS valid and Option 3 might just be self-justification dressed up as self-empathy. Be honest: is there an apology owed?
- **Defaulting to Option 1 when caregiving.** Sensitive people collapse into Option 1 automatically. The discipline is the pause.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

Option 4 has a misuse: psychologizing your critic to avoid hearing them. "You're only saying that because you're feeling insecure" is Option 4 weaponized into a dismissal. Three guardrails:

- **Empathic guess, not diagnosis.** "I'm wondering if you're feeling X" â€” they can correct you. "You're feeling X because Y" â€” closed.
- **Don't skip apology if owed.** Sometimes the right move after Option 3/4 is acknowledgment and a concrete change. Empathy isn't a substitute for accountability.
- **Check whether the criticism has a kernel.** Self-empathy can become self-protection. Ask: even setting aside the form, what's the legitimate signal in their feedback?
