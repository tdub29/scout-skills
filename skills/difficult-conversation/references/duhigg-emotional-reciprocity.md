# Duhigg: Emotional Reciprocity

## The Framework

> Reciprocity isn't about matching vulnerability to vulnerability, or sorrow to sorrow. It is being emotionally available, listening to how someone feels and what they need, and sharing our own emotional reactions. (Guide Part III)

Duhigg distinguishes emotional reciprocity from mere self-disclosure. A supportive response notices mood and energy, proves understanding, identifies the needed response, asks permission when sharing, and gives something back without grabbing the spotlight.

### Three Sub-Rules

**1. Read mood and energy separately.**

Mood: upbeat or glum, angry or relieved, joyful or scared. Energy: high or low, agitated or withdrawn, excited or calm. Matching may mean acknowledging rather than copying: "You seem upset" may align better than becoming upset.

**2. Identify the needed response.**

Comfort, empathy, advice, tough love, celebration, witness, or space? If you do not know, loop more.

**3. Ask permission before adding your story or advice.**

"Would it be okay if I shared how this lands for me?" / "Do you want advice or should I just stay with you in this?"

**4. Give something emotionally true and scaled.**

"I'm sad hearing this." "I'm proud of you." "That sounds scary." The response should support their moment, not compete with it.

## How To Apply

Create an emotional-response plan:

1. **Name the visible mood and energy.**
2. **Loop for the feeling.**
3. **Guess the need tentatively.**
4. **Ask permission if sharing advice or your own story.**
5. **Respond with scaled reciprocity.**
6. **Check whether to stay emotional or move practical.**

## Common Failure Modes

- **Spotlight-grabbing reciprocity.** Their cancer diagnosis becomes your story about a sore back or a distant relative.
- **Mood mimicry.** Becoming angry because they are angry can drive people apart; acknowledgment may match better.
- **Advice without permission.** A practical answer can feel like dismissal when the other person wants emotional witness.
- **Under-response.** "That sucks" to a major disclosure can feel like refusal to enter the emotional layer.
- **Over-response.** Matching a small frustration with dramatic intensity makes the moment about your reaction.
