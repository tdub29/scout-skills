# Rosenberg â€” Four Steps to Expressing Anger Fully

## The framework

Anger is one of Rosenberg's most counter-intuitive teachings: anger is **valuable**, not bad â€” but the standard ways of expressing it (blaming, lashing out) AND the standard ways of suppressing it (swallowing, "being the bigger person") both fail. Anger is information about an unmet need, distorted by judgmental thinking that places the cause outside yourself.

> "Four steps to expressing anger" (Ch 10, lines 6147â€“6151)

### The four steps

**1. Stop and breathe.** (line 6147)

Don't act on the impulse. Don't speak. Don't type. Don't send. Breathe â€” long enough to interrupt the automatic blame-attack sequence.

**2. Identify our judgmental thoughts.** (line 6148)

Become aware of the thoughts running through your head: "He's such an idiot." "She's so selfish." "They're impossible." These judgments are what's CREATING the anger as you currently feel it. Anger plus judgment plus blame = explosion. Anger without judgment is just a strong feeling pointing to a need.

> Rosenberg's core claim: "Anger is the result of life-alienating thinking that is disconnected from needs." Without the judgment, the anger transforms.

**3. Connect with our needs.** (line 6149)

Behind every judgmental thought is an unmet need of yours. Ask: "What need of mine is not being met in this situation?"
- "He's such an idiot" â†’ "I have a need for competence/respect/being heard."
- "She's so selfish" â†’ "I have a need for consideration/fairness/being valued."

The judgment is a distorted way of pointing at the need. Strip the judgment; keep the need.

**4. Express our feelings and unmet needs.** (line 6151)

Now â€” to the other person or to yourself, depending on context â€” express the feelings (using the [[rosenberg-feelings-vocabulary]]) and the unmet needs, WITHOUT the judgment.

- NOT: "You're so disrespectful â€” you never listen to anything I say."
- INSTEAD: "When I shared my idea and you turned to your laptop, I felt hurt and discouraged because I have a need to be heard when I bring something important up."

The same feeling â€” anger â€” surfaces as a clear signal instead of an explosion. The other person can actually receive it.

### The reframe: anger as a gift

Rosenberg's biggest reframe: anger isn't the problem. The judgmental thinking that comes WITH the anger is the problem. Strip the judgment, and what's left is the most accurate diagnostic of an unmet need you have.

> Lines 6050â€“6087: blaming prevents hearing pain. The anger metabolized through judgment can never be heard; the same anger expressed via Steps 3 and 4 reliably is.

## How to apply

When anger flares:

1. **STOP.** Step 1. Whatever you were about to say or send â€” don't. Breathe. Buy yourself 30 seconds, 5 minutes, an hour if needed.

2. **Catalog your judgments out loud (privately) or on paper.** Step 2. "He's an asshole." "She doesn't care about me." "They're completely incompetent." Get them out so you can see them.

3. **For each judgment, find the need underneath.** Step 3. The judgment is a poor proxy for the need. Translate:
   - "He doesn't respect me" â†’ "I have a need to be respected" â€” and specifically: heard, considered, valued.
   - "She's so dismissive" â†’ "I have a need to be taken seriously."
   - "They're impossible" â†’ "I have a need for cooperation/collaboration."

4. **Express it cleanly.** Step 4. Use the [[rosenberg-nvc-four-components]]:
   - Observation: what specifically did they do?
   - Feeling: I felt [angry, hurt, frustrated â€” use a real feeling word].
   - Need: because I have a need for [X].
   - Request (optional): would you be willing to [specific action]?

5. **If you can't get there yet, don't express yet.** If steps 2 and 3 haven't actually landed â€” if you're still mostly in "they're awful" â€” your "expression" will be blame in NVC clothing. Wait. Run the steps again.

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping Step 1.** Sending the text. Saying the thing. The whole protocol fails if you act on the impulse.
- **Skipping Step 2.** Going from anger straight to expression, without auditing the judgmental thoughts feeding it. Result: NVC grammar with judgment baked in. "When you ignored me [judgment dressed as observation], I felt unheard [interpretation dressed as feeling]..."
- **Skipping Step 3.** Identifying the judgments but not finding the need. Result: clarity about what's wrong with the other person, not about what you need.
- **Performing Step 4 without doing Steps 2-3 honestly.** Saying the NVC-shaped sentence with anger and judgment still active underneath. The other person feels it.
- **Using "I'm processing my anger" as withdrawal.** Step 1 is a pause, not silent treatment for three days. If the pause stretches, name it: "I'm taking time to think before I respond â€” I want to come back to this by [day]."
- **Believing the goal is to feel less angry.** Sometimes the anger stays â€” and that's accurate signal. The protocol is about expressing it usefully, not eliminating it.

## Compassion check (manipulation-risk)

Anger-expression is one of the higher-risk NVC applications. Three guardrails:

- **Expression is not entitlement.** You can express anger clearly without the other person being obligated to fix what you raised. They may have needs of their own. See [[rosenberg-empathic-receiving]] for what to do after expressing.
- **Don't weaponize the four steps.** "I'm just expressing my unmet needs in NVC" while raging is still raging. The steps require actual internal work, not just the grammar.
- **Anger about a person vs. anger about a system.** Some anger is at injustice/oppression â€” Rosenberg explicitly says NVC isn't about pacifying that. The four steps still apply, but the "request" and the "need" may be for systemic change, not for individual accommodation.
- **Repair if you blew through it.** If you skipped steps and blasted someone, the cleanup is acknowledgment + apology + describing what you wish you'd said. Not "but I was angry," not "I had a need."
