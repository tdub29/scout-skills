### The three moves

**1. Turn it up, not down** (lines 5501â€“5523)

Counter-intuitive. Don't try to suppress the internal voice â€” that just buries it where it controls you unconsciously. Instead, NOTICE it. Hear it explicitly: "I'm thinking they're wrong." "I'm composing my rebuttal." "I'm feeling defensive."

The act of noticing creates a gap between you and the thought, which is what makes managing it possible.

**2. Negotiate your way to curiosity** (lines 5531â€“5564)

Once you've heard your internal voice, talk to it. Specifically, remind it of three things:

- **Task complexity.** "I haven't fully heard their side yet."
- **Depth of their story.** "There's more here than I'm getting in 30 seconds."
- **There's always more to learn.** "Even if I think I'm right, what would change my mind?"

This is a self-negotiation, not a self-command. You're convincing yourself toward curiosity, not forcing it.

> "If internal voice too strong, express feelings first before attempting to listen."

Example: "I want to hear what you have to say. But I have to tell you something first â€” I'm feeling really hurt by what just happened, and if I don't name that I won't be able to hear you. Can I share that, and then I want to listen?"

## How to apply

When attempting to listen in a charged conversation:

1. **Check the internal voice.** Is it noisy? What's it saying?

2. **If manageable: turn it up to hear it, then negotiate.** "I'm thinking [X]. Let me remind myself there's more to their story than what I'm picking up."

3. **If too loud to manage: stop and express.** "I'm noticing I can't fully listen right now because I'm carrying [feeling]. Can I share that before I try to listen?"

4. **Don't fake-listen.** If you can't manage the voice and can't express, name the impasse out loud: "I want to hear you, AND I'm too flooded right now. Can we take 10 minutes?"

5. **Return.** After expressing or pausing, return to listening with the voice quieter.

## Common failure modes

- **Trying to suppress the voice.** Doesn't work. Suppressed thoughts get LOUDER and control you unconsciously. Turn up before turning down.
- **Negotiating without acknowledging.** "Just be curious!" said to yourself. Doesn't work without first noticing what's NOT curious (the actual reactions you're having).
- **Choosing "don't listen, talk" too quickly.** Most internal voice can be managed without taking over the conversation. Default to managing; reserve TALK for genuinely flooded.
- **Expressing the blocked-ness as accusation.** "I can't listen because YOU made me angry" is the opposite of taking responsibility for your internal state.
- **Permanent talk before listen.** Always expressing first, never getting to listening. The TALK move is a precondition for listening, not a replacement.
- **Listening with the voice still loud.** "I CAN listen, I'm fine" while internally raging. The other person picks up the tension and shuts down.

## Integrity check

This framework can be misused to dominate "in the name of needing to express first." Three guardrails:

- **The TALK move has a purpose.** It's to clear the way to listen. If you use "I need to express first" repeatedly without ever cycling to listening, you're not using the framework â€” you're using its language to dominate.
- **Internal voice management is YOUR work.** Don't make it the other person's responsibility to wait for you to manage your reactions.
- **The skill complements listening, not replaces it.** You manage the voice to enable listening. If you never get to listening, the work is incomplete.
