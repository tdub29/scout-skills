## The framework

### The three core identity questions

### The all-or-nothing trap

> "Denial and Exaggeration." (Ch 6)

Under identity threat, people collapse into binary thinking:
- "I'm either fully competent OR a complete failure."
- "I'm either a good person OR a monster."
- "I'm either loved OR alone."

Both poles produce bad outcomes. Denial ("I didn't make a mistake, this is on them") shuts down learning. Exaggeration ("I'm a disaster") collapses functioning.

### The AND stance toward yourself

> "Ground Your Identity â€” The And Stance" (lines 4150â€“4250)

Move from "I am X" (absolute) to "I am X AND I am not always X" (both true).

Examples:
- "I am a good manager AND I made a mistake on this project."
- "I am a kind person AND I sometimes act badly when I'm tired."
- "I am loved AND not by everyone, and that's OK."

The AND stance inoculates against identity collapse because both truths are held simultaneously.

### Three things to accept about yourself

> Reality-anchors (lines 4280â€“4329):

1. **You are not perfect.** Mistakes are inevitable, not exceptional.
2. **You cannot control others' interpretations of your actions.** People will sometimes misread you.
3. **You will hurt others unintentionally.** Impact happens even when intent is clean.

Accepting these in advance prevents identity quakes from feedback that violates any of them.

## How to apply

Before a difficult conversation where identity is at stake:

1. **Ask: which identity is at stake here?** Competence? Goodness? Worthiness?
2. **Notice the all-or-nothing pull.** Are you tempted into "I'm great" or "I'm awful"? Both are collapses.
3. **Construct the AND.** "I am [positive identity] AND [the specific limitation/mistake/struggle]." Both true.
4. **Accept the three reality-anchors.** Especially: you will sometimes be misread; you will sometimes hurt people unintentionally. Doesn't mean you're bad; means you're human.

During the conversation:

1. **When threat hits, name it (silently or out loud).** "I'm noticing this is hitting my competence/goodness/worthiness."
2. **Don't collapse into denial OR exaggeration.** Stay in AND.
3. **You can voice the identity stake.** "I want to be honest â€” I'm noticing this is hard to hear because part of me is reading it as 'I'm bad at my job.' That's not what you said; that's what it's triggering for me."

After the conversation:

1. **If destabilized, reground using the AND stance.** Not "they were wrong, I'm fine"; not "they were right, I'm terrible." "They have a point AND I have value."

## Common failure modes

- **Performing AND stance.** "I'm great AND I have areas to grow" said as platitude. Doesn't land because it's not honest. The genuine AND includes specific, sometimes uncomfortable, content.
- **Skipping straight to acceptance.** "Yeah I make mistakes, it's fine" without genuinely processing the threat. The processing is needed; the bypass is fake.
- **Diagnosing the other person's identity stakes.** "You're just feeling threatened in your competence" â€” invasive, often inaccurate, and patronizing.
- **Treating identity work as conversation-derailing.** "Let's stay focused on the issue, not on feelings about myself." But the identity stake is WHY the issue is loaded. Address it (silently if needed) or it'll derail anyway.
- **Identity threat as license for defensiveness.** "I'm shaken so I can't engage" used to dodge accountability. The framework asks you to ground, not retreat.

## Integrity check

This framework can be misused to relocate accountability into the realm of identity ("you hurt my feelings about myself, so we can't discuss what I did"). Three guardrails:

- **Identity work is YOUR work.** Don't make others responsible for managing your identity stakes.
- **Identity threat â‰  valid criticism is wrong.** Feedback that destabilizes you can still be accurate. The work is to receive it without collapsing.
- **Don't use the framework to weaponize fragility.** "This is triggering my identity issues" said to shut down legitimate feedback is misuse. Identity stakes deserve respect; they don't trump accuracy.
