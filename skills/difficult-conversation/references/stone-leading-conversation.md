## The framework

> "There are a set of powerful 'moves' you can make during the conversation â€” reframing, listening, and naming the dynamic â€” that can help keep the conversation on track." (Ch 11, lines 6500â€“6502)

Difficult conversations veer. The other person blames; you start to defend; the loop closes. Three moves let you redirect without escalating.

### Move 1: Reframe

> "Reframe, Reframe, Reframe." (Ch 11, lines 6516â€“6622)

Translate destructive moves into productive ones, in real time, by what you say back. The other person's language stays as-is; your translation creates space.

Common reframes:

| When they say... | You translate to... |
|---|---|
| "I'm right" (truth claim) | "I want to share my perspective" |
| "You hurt me" (impact claim with hidden intent assumption) | "I feel angry; let's understand what happened" |
| "It's your fault" (blame) | "Both of us contributed; let's map it" |
| "You're being nasty" (character judgment) | "You're hurting and reacting" |

Don't argue with their framing; quietly REFRAME and respond from the new frame.

Example:
- Them: "You're being completely unreasonable."
- You (not arguing the label): "It sounds like you really see this differently than I do. Help me understand what feels unreasonable from your side."

You didn't accept "unreasonable"; you didn't fight "unreasonable"; you translated it.

### Move 2: The You-Me AND

> "The 'You-Me' And." (Ch 11, lines 6623â€“6655)

Reject the false choice between your view and theirs. Both can be true; both can be heard; both can be present.

- "I can listen to you AND you can listen to me."
- "I can hear your view AND share mine."
- "You can be hurt AND I didn't mean to hurt you AND we can still work this out."

The AND construction prevents the conversation from collapsing into either/or. Most stuck conversations are stuck on a false either/or.

### Move 3: Name the dynamic

> "Name the Dynamic." (Ch 11, lines 6732â€“6780)

When the conversation pattern itself is the problem, make IT the topic. Step outside the content and describe what's happening.

- "We keep running out of time before we get to my piece."
- "I notice every time I share something, you go to disagreeing before acknowledging it."
- "You say you're not upset, AND you're speaking with a lot of intensity. Can we talk about that?"

Naming the dynamic surfaces what's been operating below the surface. Often it's been the actual problem all along.

### Persistent listening

> "It's Always the Right Time to Listen." (lines 6656â€“6671)

Sometimes the right redirect is just to stay listening â€” even when the other person becomes emotional, repeats, or accuses. Persistent listening pulls the conversation back to dialogue.

## How to apply

In a conversation that's drifting:

1. **Notice the drift.** Blame? Domination? Pattern that's repeating?

2. **Pick the redirect:**
   - If they're using destructive framing (judgment, blame, attribution): **Reframe** silently and respond from the new frame.
   - If you're stuck in either/or thinking: **You-Me AND** out loud.
   - If the conversation pattern itself is the problem: **Name the Dynamic** out loud.
   - If you're tempted to defend or escalate: **Persistent Listening**.

3. **Don't announce the move.** Reframing only works if it's seamless. "I'm reframing what you said as..." breaks the spell.

4. **Cycle.** Often you'll do all four in one conversation. Reframe blame â†’ name the dynamic â†’ AND-construct â†’ return to listening.

## Common failure modes

- **Reframing as correction.** "When you say 'you always do X,' I think what you really mean is..." That's correction, not reframe. Reframe is invisible to the speaker; correction is confrontation.
- **You-Me AND as both-sidesing.** "We both have a point" used to avoid the harder work of either party. The AND construction names that both are present, not that both are equally weighted.
- **Naming the dynamic combatively.** "You always interrupt!" That's not naming the dynamic; it's accusation. Naming: "I notice we keep running into this â€” when one of us shares something, the other goes to disagreement. Can we change the pattern?"
- **Persistent listening as silent treatment.** Listening with body language of contempt isn't listening. Real persistent listening keeps engagement.
- **Picking the wrong move.** Reframing when the dynamic needs naming; naming the dynamic when reframing would have sufficed. Match the move to the failure.
- **Over-leading.** Constantly steering can come across as managing the other person. Sometimes the conversation just needs to be a conversation.

## Integrity check

These moves can be used to control conversation flow at the expense of the other party. Three guardrails:

- **Lead, don't direct.** The goal is to keep the conversation on track for BOTH parties, not to steer to your preferred outcome.
- **Reframing isn't gaslighting.** Don't reframe to erase what the other person actually meant. Reframe to translate the destructive form into a learning form, while honoring the underlying content.
- **Don't use "naming the dynamic" to deflect.** "I notice you keep bringing up the same complaint" said to dodge addressing a legitimate, repeated grievance. Sometimes the dynamic IS the legitimate grievance.
