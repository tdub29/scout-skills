# The Anti (Trend-Termination Pullback)

## What this decides

Whether a bounce following a sharp reversal move qualifies as a tradable Anti setup, and if so: entry trigger, stop placement (conservative vs. aggressive), first profit-taking split, and the hold-vs-protect call at the measured-move objective (MMO). Output is a go/no-go plus a fully specified trade (entry, stop, target, MMO checkpoint).

## When to use

- A trend (up or down) shows signs of terminating: loss of momentum on successive thrusts, an overextended reading on a higher time frame, a double top/bottom, or a failed continuation / failure test.
- That is followed by a sharp countertrend thrust with a distinct change of character â€” bigger and faster than prior countertrend moves, and typically registering a new momentum-indicator extreme against the old trend.
- The market is now bouncing weakly off that thrust and you're deciding whether to fade the bounce as a reversal trade.
- Not for: an ordinary pullback inside a healthy, established trend (use `grimes-pullback` â€” no prior termination signal, no change-of-character thrust required, and the trade is with-trend, not countertrend).

## Method

1. **Confirm the two-condition setup (go/no-go gate).** Both are required â€” if either is missing, this is not an Anti, it's noise or a normal pullback:
   - **Condition 1 â€” termination context.** The trend shows a pattern suggesting it could be ending: momentum loss on successive thrusts, an extremely overextended higher-time-frame reading, a double top/bottom that fails to continue, or a failure test.
   - **Condition 2 â€” change-of-character thrust.** The market then makes a countertrend move distinctly stronger (in price and/or time) than prior countertrend moves in that trend, ideally confirmed by a new momentum-indicator extreme against the old trend. This is the thrust that "breaks" the trend's rhythm.
2. **Wait for the tell: a reluctant bounce.** After the thrust, the market should bounce â€” but weakly. A slow, grinding, low-conviction bounce is the signature that buyers (in a downside Anti) or sellers (in an upside Anti) have lost control. A sharp, clean bounce back toward the old trend extreme argues the countertrend attempt is failing â€” stand aside.
3. **Trigger the entry on the bounce, exactly like a pullback.** Use either standard pullback entry: (a) a breakout trigger â€” enter on a break of the bounce's own support/resistance (e.g., a break of the prior bar's low to sell, prior bar's high to buy) â€” this often has a slightly better edge here than in a normal pullback; or (b) enter directly at the bounce's support/resistance without waiting for a break. Avoid the third generic pullback entry (buying strength/selling weakness mid-bounce) â€” it is not ideal for this countertrend context.
4. **Set the stop â€” pick conservative or aggressive, never in between.**
   - **Conservative (farther) stop:** beyond the extreme of the original trend (below the low for a buy Anti, above the high for a sell Anti). Anything short of that level is still consolidation compatible with trend change. Use this by default, and always when the setup thrust was not decisively strong.
   - **Aggressive (nearer) stop:** just beyond the extreme of the setup bounce itself. Smaller loss if wrong, but these zones are volatile and more prone to a noise stop-out; pairs with a larger position size.
   - Never place the stop between the two levels â€” no probability edge compensates for that added loss.
5. **Take partial profits at the MMO; this is not optional.** Book 33-66% of the position at the measured-move objective (MMO) projected from the setup thrust. Countertrend trades need closer targets and tighter management than with-trend trades because a failed reversal can snap back hard into the old trend.
6. **MMO checkpoint â€” hold or protect the runner.** Watch price action right at the MMO closely:
   - If price **breaks through** the MMO with continued countertrend momentum: probabilities favor a genuine new trend has begun; hold the runner, trail it like a new with-trend position.
   - If price **stalls or reverses at/near** the MMO: the pattern is evolving into a complex consolidation and reversion to the *original* trend becomes more likely. Get very protective of remaining profits â€” tighten the stop or exit the balance.
7. **Classify the outcome against the three known failure modes** (governs what "acceptable" looks like and what to do next):
   - **Failure through consolidation:** price goes dead / chops instead of extending. Usually the setup thrust wasn't a strong enough change of character. Reduce exposure, tighten stops, or exit â€” don't wait for the loss to grow.
   - **Failure near the previous swing:** price stalls right at the first (conservative) target â€” the extreme of the setup thrust â€” and can't get through. Often still salvageable if partials were already taken at/near that level; treat remaining size defensively.
   - **Failure by strong momentum against:** the old trend reasserts with force, running through both the near and far stops. This is the ugly one â€” can produce a full loss or worse on a gap. This is why the stop discipline in step 4 is non-negotiable and why position size should already be sized for a full loss at entry.

## Sub-rules / failure modes

- **Location, not shape, defines the Anti.** An Anti looks identical to a plain pullback on the chart â€” the only distinguishing feature is *where it sits in market structure* (first pullback after a potential trend termination) versus an ordinary pullback (continuation inside an established trend). If you can't articulate the termination context from step 1, you're trading a generic pullback and should size/manage it as one, not as an Anti.
- **Weak setup thrust â†’ expect a weak trade.** A textbook Anti isn't required for the trade to work, but a two-bar countertrend move with no real change of character (a common cause of the "failure through consolidation" mode) is a warning to size down or skip, not proof the pattern is broken.
- **Works on all time frames â€” and stacks across them.** A weekly-chart Anti can supply bullish/bearish context that upgrades an otherwise mediocre daily pattern, and a lower-time-frame Anti nested inside a higher-time-frame range is a legitimate way to enter that range early. When trading a lower-time-frame Anti inside a higher-time-frame structure, consider anchoring the stop to the higher-time-frame boundary rather than the lower-time-frame extreme â€” smaller size, but respects the pattern that actually matters.
- **Countertrend sizing discipline.** Because this is explicitly a countertrend trade against a trend that may not actually be over, position size and stop distance must assume the trade can be a full loser (see failure mode 3) â€” this is not a place to under-size the stop to "improve" R multiple.
- **Don't confuse this with Raschke's Anti.** Raschke's original Anti (Street Smarts, 1996) is defined mechanically via a dual-timeframe stochastic crossing (fast line against slow line). Grimes borrows the name for a price-structure pattern with the same location-based logic but no indicator requirement â€” an indicator crossing can support the read but is not the trigger.

## Worked mini-example

Sell Anti, First Majestic Silver (AG), daily chart (book Anti 1):
- **Termination context:** parabolic uptrend showing signs of exhaustion.
- **Change-of-character thrust:** a sharp downswing breaks the rhythm of the uptrend â€” clearly stronger than prior pullback swings.
- **The tell:** the bounce following that downswing is slow and reluctant â€” "the final nail in the coffin." A sharp, clean bounce here would have argued for trend continuation instead.
- **Entry:** breakdown of the prior bar's low (the bar at the top of the reluctant bounce did not itself trigger).
- **Stop:** two valid choices â€” conservative just above the high of the trend, or aggressive just above the extreme of the setup bounce. Nothing in between.
- **Management:** multiple small pauses on the way down; partials taken per plan, no adding except on a fresh pattern (e.g., a nested pullback). A small failure test later was the exit signal for remaining short exposure â€” a orderly finish, not a failure mode.
