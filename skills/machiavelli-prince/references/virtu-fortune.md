## The framework

### Core thesis

Chapter XXV (*Quanto possa nelle umane cose la fortuna, e in che modo se gli possa ostare*) is Machiavelli's most explicit metaphysical pause â€” yet it ends in **practice**. Many believe the world is governed by fortune and God such that prudence cannot correct it; Machiavelli partly inclines to this when he sees daily shocks beyond human conjecture.

Still, **our free will is not extinguished**. Fortune is **arbiter of half our actions**, but leaves **the other half, or a little less, in our governance**. He compares fortune to a **ruinous river**: when wrathful it floods plains, uproots trees and buildings, washes soil away; everyone flees, none can resist. Yet when times are quiet, men can build **dikes and embankments** so that when waters rise again, they go in a channel or their force is neither so licentious nor so damaging.

**So with fortune:** she shows power where **virtÃ¹ is not ordered to resist**; she turns her impetus where **no embankments and repairs** were made. Italy, Machiavelli says, is the plain without dikes â€” hence great variations.

Parallel claim: the prince who **relies wholly on fortune** is ruined as she varies. He is **happy** whose **mode of proceeding** (*modo del procedere*) **encounters** the **qualities of the times** (*qualitÃ  dei tempi*); **unhappy** he whose conduct **discord with them**.

VirtÃ¹ here is not Christian virtue alone â€” it is **capacity, boldness, prudence, adaptation**, the quality that lets you **match method to season** and **prepare before crisis**.

### The half-and-half model

Use Machiavelli's split as a **working attribution**, not precise physics:

| Domain | Tends toward fortune | Tends toward virtÃ¹ |
|---|---|---|
| Market/regulatory shock | Macro cycle, geopolitics | Prepared balance sheet, diversified supply |
| Career rise/fall | Patron dies, war begins | Skills, reputation, adaptable style |
| Battle/outcome | Weather, chance encounters | Training, logistics, discipline |
| Organizational crisis | Sudden scandal external | Governance, embankments, candid reserves |

The error Machiavelli attacks: Italian rulers who **want to stay with fortune, not with their virtÃ¹**, because they see little virtÃ¹ locally and let fortune rule â€” **refusing to build embankments**.

### The river metaphor â€” preparedness vs panic

Two phases:

1. **Quiet times:** invest in embankments â€” reserves, alliances, redundant systems, legal structures, loyal troops (Ch. XIIâ€“XIV theme), reputation that survives shock
2. **Flood times:** channel the force â€” rapid adaptation, decisive moves, sometimes cruelty or boldness **if season demands** (cross-link realpolitik/feared-loved only as context)

Failure: building nothing in calm, then praying when the river rises. Also failure: building embankments but **refusing to change style** when the season shifts (happy/unhappy times-fit).

### Times-fit â€” the procedural match

Machiavelli observes men pursue glory and riches by **different paths**; outcomes depend on whether **your proceeding matches the time**:

- Caution succeeds in cautious seasons; boldness succeeds in bold seasons
- Two may act differently yet reach the same end if each matches their contrast
- Men **do not change their fantasies or modes** while **times and things change universally and particularly** â€” hence one has good fortune then bad

The wisest man who **knows the times and order of things** and **accommodates himself** would always have good fortune â€” Machiavelli cites the maxim *vir sapiens dominabitur astris* not as astrology endorsement but as **adaptation beats fatalism**.

Practical diagnostic: name the **season** (expansion, contraction, moralistic backlash, war, consolidation) and the **style** (bold/cautious, merciful/severe, fox/lion). Mismatch â†’ infelicity even with talent.

### Opposing fortune "in general" vs particular

Machiavelli says his embankment discussion suffices for **opposing fortune in general**; in particular, princes rise and fall without visible cause because of **fortune/times-fit** hidden variables.

Do not promise to eliminate fortune. Promise to:

- Expand the governed half
- Reduce damage when floods come
- Adjust style when season turns

### Burd's editorial note on the chapter

Burd flags Ch. XXV as one of Machiavelli's most remarkable â€” where politics forces a wider question (*free will vs fortune*). Machiavelli cannot abolish fortune; he argues within limits a man can **alter himself**: if shrewd enough to **know times and order of things** and **accommodate**, he would always have good fortune â€” hence the ironic use of *vir sapiens dominabitur astris* (the wise man commands the stars) as **anti-fatalist adaptation**, not astrology.

When fortune remains **arbiter of half**, the practical prince still builds **embankments** and **matches style to season** â€” the two levers that do not require predicting every shock.

### France, Spain, Germany vs Italy (embankment contrast)

Machiavelli contrasts polities with **conveniente virtÃ¹** (ordered defenses â€” laws, arms, institutions) vs Italy's **plain without dikes**. Translation for organizations:

- **Embanked:** cash runway, diversified revenue, documented succession, owned capability (not mercenary dependency per Ch. XIIâ€“XIV), reputational reserves
- **Plain:** single client, hero-founder dependency, outsourced core function, no scenario planning

Attribution rule: when an embanked org survives a sector flood and a plain org does not, raise **virtÃ¹ share** for the survivor's preparedness even if fortune triggered the event.

### Two operators, same goal (times-fit nuance)

Machiavelli notes two can operate differently yet reach the same end if each **matches their contrast** â€” many orders of things as there are provinces. Implication: benchmarking a rival's boldness when your season requires consolidation is **discord with times**, not lack of talent.

Ask: **Is the user's failure "wrong virtue" or "right virtue, wrong season"?**

## Common failure modes

- **Fatalism.** "Fortune rules everything" â€” Machiavelli explicitly rejects total fatalism; half (or little less) is governable.

- **Heroic denial of fortune.** Ignoring half that is variable produces hubris; when season turns, the same virtÃ¹ becomes "wrong" method.

- **Building embankments once.** Dikes require maintenance; quiet times are when you prepare, not when you rest on laurels.

- **Style identity over times-fit.** "I'm always bold" or "I'm always prudent" â€” Machiavelli's unhappy prince discord with times.

- **Confusing virtÃ¹ with moral virtue only.** Scipio's humanity and Hannibal's terror both express virtÃ¹ as **effective capacity** in context â€” moral judgment is separate.

- **Astrology cosplay.** *Vir sapiens dominabitur astris* in Machiavelli means shrewd adaptation, not horoscopes.

- **Blaming fortune for missing embankments.** Italy's plain floods because unembanked â€” organizational analog is underinvestment in resilience.

- **Copying another era's season.** What worked in 2021 liquidity may fail in 2026 constraint â€” times-fit is dynamic, not a one-time label.

- **Treating virtÃ¹ as brute force only.** Prudence, accommodation, and knowing when not to act are virtÃ¹ â€” not only aggression.
