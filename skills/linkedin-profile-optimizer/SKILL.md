# LinkedIn Profile Optimizer

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Optimize their LinkedIn profile for job searching
- Improve LinkedIn visibility and searchability
- Sync their resume with their LinkedIn profile
- Attract recruiters and job opportunities
- Mentions: "LinkedIn", "LinkedIn profile", "optimize LinkedIn", "LinkedIn headline", "recruiter"

## Core Capabilities

- Optimize headline for searchability
- Write compelling About/Summary sections
- Structure Experience section for impact
- Improve profile completeness score
- Add relevant keywords for recruiter searches
- Align LinkedIn with resume while leveraging platform differences

## LinkedIn vs. Resume: Key Differences

| Aspect | Resume | LinkedIn |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Length | 1-2 pages | Unlimited |
| Tone | Formal | More conversational |
| Keywords | Job-specific | Industry-wide |
| Audience | One specific employer | All recruiters |
| Updates | Per application | Always current |
| Personality | Minimal | Show more |

## Recruiter-scan field notes (practitioner)

Distilled from a finance-career creator's "How To Get A Job" (Your Finance Brother, YouTube). Finance-flavored; the principles generalize.

- **Profile photo â€” the three failure modes recruiters actually flag:** (1) *underdressed* for the field; (2) *ambiguous social-setting* photo that makes the viewer wonder "is that from a wedding?"; (3) a cropped *frat/sorority* picture (common, always wrong). Fix is trivial: a plain-backdrop headshot from roughly chest-up, dressed one notch above your field's norm. (Suit-and-tie is the finance bar; match *your* industry â€” for tech/DS, clean business-casual is fine.)
- **Banner:** don't overthink it â€” a clean city / nature / school image sized correctly beats a blank one. It's a completeness signal, not a branding project.
- **About section â€” concrete accolades, not aspirational fluff.** 2â€“3 succinct sentences of *specific, verifiable* things (named awards, GPA/honors, the institutions and numbers). Cut "I love leadership" / "I love the cross-disciplinary nature of X" â€” junior-level readers don't believe it and don't care; they care whether you can do the actual work. (This is the `filler-vs-differentiator` test: if the line would still be true with someone else's name on it, delete it.)
- **Experience â€” quantify and recency-weight.** Lead each role with numbers (10x improvement, 45% fewer errors, 3 hours/week saved), and put the most detail on the most recent role, less on older ones.
- **Interests â€” make them conversation starters, not categories.** "13th-century French poetry" or "1950s six-speed manual transmissions" beats "travel" and "cooking" (everyone travels and eats). Specific interests differentiate you and give an interviewer something to open with. Same logic applies to the resume's interests line.

## Resume-to-LinkedIn Sync

### What to Keep the Same:
- Core achievements and metrics
- Job titles and dates
- Key skills and qualifications
- Overall career narrative

### What to Expand:
- More detail in descriptions
- Additional context
- More bullets per role
- Personality and voice

### What to Adjust:
- Tone (more conversational)
- Length (can be longer)
- Keywords (broader than job-specific)
- Call to action (add contact info)
