## The framework

> "The cue triggers a craving, which motivates a response, which provides a reward, which satisfies the craving and, ultimately, becomes associated with the cue." (*Atomic Habits*, Chapter 3)

This reference is the **spine** of the Clear material: it explains the four-step habit loop and the Four Laws built on top of it, then routes to the four law references for the tactics. A habit is "a behavior that has been repeated enough times to become automatic" â€” a reliable solution to a recurring problem, which reduces cognitive load so the conscious mind is freed for harder tasks (habits "do not restrict freedom; they create it").

## The four-step habit loop

Drawing on Thorndike's puzzle-box cats ("behaviors followed by satisfying consequences tend to be repeated"), every habit runs through four stages **in the same order**:

1. **Cue** â€” the trigger that predicts a reward; a bit of information your prediction machine notices. The two most common cues are **time and location**.
2. **Craving** â€” the motivational force; the *desire to change your internal state*. "What you crave is not the habit itself but the change in state it delivers." (You don't crave the cigarette; you crave relief.) Cravings are meaningless until a cue is interpreted â€” the observer's thoughts and feelings transform a cue into a craving.
3. **Response** â€” the actual habit (a thought or action). Whether it happens depends on motivation, friction, and ability.
4. **Reward** â€” the end goal. Rewards (1) **satisfy** the craving and (2) **teach** â€” they tell the brain which actions are worth remembering. Rewards close the loop.

The four stages split into a **problem phase** (cue + craving â€” you realize something needs to change) and a **solution phase** (response + reward â€” you act and get the change). "Without the first three steps, a behavior will not occur. Without all four, a behavior will not be repeated."

## The Four Laws of Behavior Change

> Each law is "a lever that influences human behavior." (Ch 3)

Each stage of the loop maps to one law for *creating* a good habit, with an **inversion** for *breaking* a bad one:

| Stage | Create a good habit (the Law) | Break a bad habit (the inversion) |
|---|---|---|
| Cue | 1st Law â€” **Make it obvious** | Make it invisible |
| Craving | 2nd Law â€” **Make it attractive** | Make it unattractive |
| Response | 3rd Law â€” **Make it easy** | Make it difficult |
| Reward | 4th Law â€” **Make it satisfying** | Make it unsatisfying |

Whenever you want to change a behavior, ask: How can I make it obvious / attractive / easy / satisfying? Clear notes the laws aren't a guaranteed-exhaustive theory of all behavior "but I think they're close" â€” they apply across sport, politics, art, medicine, and management without needing a different strategy per habit.

The first three laws (obvious, attractive, easy) increase the odds a behavior is **performed this time**; the fourth (satisfying) increases the odds it is **repeated next time**, completing the loop.

## How to apply

**To build a habit:** route through all four laws in order. Make the cue obvious (`clear-make-it-obvious.md`), the craving attractive (`clear-make-it-attractive.md`), the response easy (`clear-make-it-easy.md`), and the reward satisfying (`clear-make-it-satisfying.md`).

**To break a habit:** run the inversions â€” make the cue invisible, the craving unattractive, the response difficult, the reward unsatisfying.

**To diagnose why a habit isn't working:** walk the four stages and find the broken one. No cue â†’ it never starts. Weak craving â†’ not enough motivation. Too much friction â†’ can't do it. Unsatisfying reward â†’ no reason to repeat. "If a behavior is insufficient in any of the four stages, it will not become a habit."

## Common failure modes

- **Fixing the wrong stage.** Trying to manufacture motivation (craving) when the real break is a missing cue or excessive friction.
- **Stopping at three laws.** Making a habit obvious, attractive, and easy but leaving it unsatisfying, so it's done once and never repeated.
- **Treating the loop as conscious.** The loop runs in a split second, automatically; awareness (the Habits Scorecard) is the prerequisite to changing it.
- **Ignoring the inversions for bad habits.** Applying "make it obvious/easy" logic to a habit you want to *eliminate* instead of inverting each law.
