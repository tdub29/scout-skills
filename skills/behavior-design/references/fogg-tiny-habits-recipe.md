## The framework

> "After I [ANCHOR], I will [NEW TINY BEHAVIOR]." (*Tiny Habits*, recipe card; Chapter 4 on Anchors)

The Tiny Habits recipe is the concrete format for installing a new habit. It has three parts, executed in immediate sequence:

1. **Anchor Moment** â€” an existing routine (brushing your teeth) or reliable event (the phone ringing) that already happens in your life. The Anchor is the *prompt*; it reminds you to do the new behavior. "Find the right Anchor within your current routine to serve as the prompt."
2. **New Tiny Behavior** â€” a deliberately *shrunk* version of the habit you want: floss **one** tooth, do **two** push-ups. You do it **immediately after** the Anchor.
3. **Instant Celebration** â€” something that creates a positive emotion right after the behavior, to wire it in (covered in depth in `fogg-celebration-emotion.md`).

The canonical recipe-card phrasing:

> **After I** [brush my teeth], **I will** [floss one tooth]. **To wire the habit into my brain, I will immediately:** [celebrate].

This recipe is just B=MAP applied: the **Anchor supplies the Prompt**, **tiny supplies the Ability**, and **celebration supplies the wiring** so it doesn't depend on Motivation.

## Anchoring: borrow a prompt you already have

You don't need to remember to do a new habit â€” you attach it to something that **already happens reliably**. Fogg's flossing breakthrough came "like a bolt of lightning" when he realized he could ride an existing routine (brushing) as the cue.

Rules for a good Anchor:
- **Reliability.** The Anchor must happen consistently in your life. A fuzzy anchor produces a fuzzy habit; precise events ("after I pour my morning coffee") work, vague ones ("in the morning") don't. Fogg calls these **Anchor Moments** â€” precise events.
- **Location/context match.** The Anchor should occur in the same place and context as the new behavior. Avoid an Anchor in one room and a behavior in another. If the new habit lives in the kitchen, anchor it to an existing kitchen routine.
- **Frequency match.** A daily habit needs a daily Anchor; a once-a-week behavior anchors to a once-a-week event.
- **Trailing edge.** Anchor to the *end* of the existing routine (the moment it finishes) so there's a clean handoff into the new behavior â€” "after I set down my toothbrush," not "while brushing."

## Start absurdly small

> Floss **one** tooth. Do **two** push-ups. Read **one** sentence.

The "tiny" in Tiny Habits is not a motivational softener â€” it is the mechanism. A behavior small enough to do even on your worst, busiest, most demoralized day reliably clears the Action Line, so it actually happens and gets repeated. Fogg's flossing rule: floss one tooth was *all* he had to do; flossing more was "extra credit." Within two weeks he was flossing all his teeth twice a day â€” because the *habit* (the automaticity), not the *size*, was what he was installing first.

This is the "small roots, then deep roots" logic: a big behavior with shallow roots topples in the first storm (a cold, a crisis, a bad week); a tiny behavior weathers the storm and then grows. **Go tiny to create consistency; stay tiny to get firmly rooted.** You can always do *more* once the behavior is wired â€” but the minimum stays tiny so the habit survives bad days.

Tiny also defuses the "Motivation Monkey": you never need much motivation to do something this small, so the habit doesn't collapse when motivation dips.

## How to apply

**To design a new habit:**
1. Write the recipe template: "After I ____, I will ____."
2. **Shrink the behavior** until it's something you could do even on the worst day (one tooth, one push-up, one deep breath). If you'd skip it when rushed, it's not tiny enough.
3. **Pick the Anchor** from your existing routines â€” reliable, same context, matching frequency, attached to the trailing edge.
4. **Add the celebration** immediately after (see `fogg-celebration-emotion.md`).
5. Run it; if it's not happening, the Anchor is probably unreliable/mismatched, or the behavior isn't tiny enough.

Worked example from the book: "After I take my last vitamin in the morning, I will eat a small bit of healthy [dark chocolate]." Anchor = taking vitamins (reliable, daily, kitchen); tiny behavior = one small bit; celebration follows.

**To detect it in habit advice:** any durable "stack this onto that" habit advice (habit stacking, trigger-action plans) is this recipe â€” the load-bearing parts are *reliable anchor*, *genuinely tiny*, and *immediate positive emotion*. Advice missing any of the three tends to fail.

## Common failure modes

- **Not tiny enough.** "Do a 7-minute workout" feels small but isn't on a bad day. If you'd skip it when busy or sick, shrink it (one wall push-up). The most common reason a Tiny Habit fails is that it was never actually tiny.
- **A weak or fuzzy Anchor.** "In the morning" is not an event. The Anchor must be a precise, reliable, already-existing action.
- **Context mismatch.** Anchoring in one place and doing the behavior somewhere else breaks the handoff.
- **Skipping the celebration.** Without the immediate positive emotion, you're relying on repetition alone, which is slow and fragile (see `fogg-celebration-emotion.md`).
- **Scaling up the minimum too fast.** Doing more is fine as "extra credit," but if you redefine the *required* behavior as big, you lose the bad-day resilience that tiny buys you.
