## Is in-person even necessary? (segment fit first)

Digital scales efficiently; in-person builds trust that digital can't. In-person earns its cost mainly for high-trust, high-consideration motions. Prioritize events when your ICP looks like:

- **Enterprise / multi-stakeholder** â€” high ACV, several people must build trust before a big commitment
- **Regulated buyers** â€” healthcare, finance, government have strict vendor-evaluation norms
- **High-touch / heavy customization** â€” significant integration or configuration work
- **Conservative industries** â€” manufacturing, utilities still run on traditional relationship-building
- **Long cycles** â€” 6+ month sales cycles get disproportionate acceleration from face time

Reality check: a cybersecurity company found $500k+ ACV deals almost never closed without at least one in-person meeting â€” the trust to switch security vendors couldn't be built over Zoom. If your ICP is *not* in these buckets, spend on digital first and treat events as a small experiment.

## The 80/20 of event selection

A small number of events generate the majority of event-attributed pipeline (one B2B SaaS program found **3 conferences drove ~70%** of it). The job is to find those and concentrate:

- Increase presence at the winners â€” secure **speaking slots**, host **larger side events**, send **more of the right people**, buy **better placement**
- Cut or minimize the long tail of low-yield events
- Re-rank yearly; the 20% shifts as your ICP and market move

## Bigger isn't better (size â†” ROI is often inverse)

Major conferences look can't-miss and frequently deliver the *worst* returns:

- **Big events = more noise** â€” higher cost on everything (booth, hotels, travel), more competing vendors, attendees spread thin across tracks, endless competing side events
- **Audience dilution** â€” you're paying to reach a crowd padded with students, investors, press, other vendors, consultants, and industry tourists; your ICP is a thin slice, so effective cost-per-qualified-lead balloons
- **Small-event advantage** â€” a 50-person niche meetup can out-produce a 5,000-person conference; highest ROI is often **regional events of 100â€“200** where you can reach every qualified prospect in the room

## The three event types (three risk profiles)

### 1. Owned events â€” maximum control, maximum risk

- **User conferences** turn customers into a community and a product into a movement (Dreamforce). Don't attempt before you have an audience that would come unbegged.

- **Industry summits** build thought leadership by tackling category problems, not product pitches â€” they pull in partners and influencers who amplify.
- **Workshops / certifications** tie the event directly to customer success and can pay for themselves via fees.

### 2. Trade shows & conferences â€” someone else's arena
Less control, less risk â€” you rent instant access to an audience but work inside their format. **Success is 120 days of prep, not the 4 days on the floor.**
- **Pre-show (starts ~120 days out):** mine the attendee list for *stories*, not just names (recent funding, press, job posts) â†’ hooks far better than "want a demo?"; **book ~70% of meeting slots before anyone flies out** ("saw you opened a Singapore office â€” we helped 3 companies with APAC expansion last quarter, coffee at the show?")

- **Turn customers into advocates** â€” support customer-run user groups but let them stay independent; they become a reference network prospects trust *because* they're not on your payroll
