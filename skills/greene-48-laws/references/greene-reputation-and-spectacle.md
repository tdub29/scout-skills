## The framework

### The three sub-mechanisms

**1. Build before you need.** Reputation accumulates slowly; it's deployed instantly. Build in advance.

**2. Defend asymmetrically.** Attacks on reputation are cheap; defenses are expensive. The first move advantage is real. Don't let attacks compound; respond quickly.

**3. Calibrate visibility.** Constant presence devalues. Strategic absence increases anticipation. The cycle: appear, achieve, withdraw, return larger.

### The shadow-of-the-predecessor problem (Law 41)

The most underrated of the seven. Taking over from a famous predecessor is a trap. You inherit the reputation, but every action is compared. You can't outshine them in their own genre; you can only build a new genre.

The move: deliberately DIFFERENTIATE. Change the focus, the tone, the metric. Establish that you're playing a different game.

## How to apply

When building reputation:

1. **Identify your one consistent signal.** What do you want to be known for? Pick one thing; reinforce it for years.
2. **Make your spectacle proportionate to your substance.** Over-spectacle without substance collapses; under-spectacle wastes substance.
3. **Watch the visibility cycle.** Are you over-circulated? Time for strategic absence. Under-circulated? Time for a deliberate appearance.
4. **Never apologize for ambition.** Apologizing for wanting recognition signals you don't deserve it. Be royal in your fashion.

When following a predecessor (Law 41):
1. **Don't compete on their terms.** You'll lose; their legacy is calibrated to themselves.
2. **Identify the new dimension.** What can YOU bring that the predecessor didn't?
3. **Differentiate explicitly.** Subtle differentiation is mistaken for inferior similarity.

When defending against reputation attacks:
1. **Speed matters.** Unanswered attacks become facts.
2. **Don't dignify minor attacks.** Some attacks should be ignored; engagement amplifies them.
3. **For serious attacks: open holes in the attacker's reputation.** Greene's Law 5: deflect attention to the attacker's vulnerabilities.

## Common failure modes

- **Substance without spectacle.** Excellent work, no audience. The world is too noisy for substance alone.
- **Spectacle without substance.** Flash without deliverables. Reputation collapses on first inspection.
- **Continuous presence.** Over-circulation makes you mundane. Strategic absence increases value.
- **Defensive crouching after attacks.** Becoming smaller in response to attacks confirms the attack. Greene's response: counter-attack the attacker's reputation, or stand tall.
- **Stepping into giant predecessor shoes without differentiation.** Three years of unfavorable comparison.

## Ethical guardrail

Reputation is built on substance OR appearance. Genuine reputation rests on actual delivery. Manufactured reputation (Greene's Law 37 in extreme application) erodes when reality intrudes.

- **Substance-backed spectacle:** ethically clean. Show what you can do; make it visible.
- **Spectacle-only spectacle:** manipulative. Eventually exposed.
- **Reputation attacks (Law 5 reversal):** Greene endorses opening "holes in their reputation." Doing this CYNICALLY (with no truth to the attack) is character assassination. Doing this HONESTLY (surfacing real failings) is competitive but defensible.
