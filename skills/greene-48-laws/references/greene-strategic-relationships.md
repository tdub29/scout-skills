## The framework

Nine laws on **how to position yourself in a network of allies, enemies, and dependents.**

### The nine laws

**Law 2 â€” Don't trust friends; use enemies.** Friends become envious; enemies-turned-allies have more to prove.

**Law 10 â€” Infection: avoid the unhappy and unlucky.** Emotional and reputational states are contagious.

**Law 11 â€” Keep people dependent on you.** The more you're needed, the more freedom you have.

**Law 14 â€” Pose as friend, work as spy.** Polite social encounters are intelligence-gathering opportunities.

**Law 18 â€” Don't isolate.** Fortresses make you a target. Circulate; be shielded by the crowd.

**Law 19 â€” Don't offend the wrong person.** Some people will spend their lives seeking revenge. Choose targets carefully.

**Law 20 â€” Don't commit to anyone.** Independence makes you valuable. Sides constrain.

### The unifying logic

Your network is not neutral background. It's the medium of your power. Designed deliberately, it supports you. Accumulated accidentally, it constrains you.

- **Who you're near matters** (Laws 2, 10, 19).
- **Who needs you matters** (Law 11, 23).
- **Who you're committed to matters** (Law 20).
- **What you know about them matters** (Law 14).
- **Who you isolate or fail to isolate matters** (Law 18, 42).

### The dependency calculus (Law 11)

> "The more you are relied on, the more freedom you have."

Dependency is mutual coin. The mistake: making yourself dependent without making them dependent. Healthy positions are MUTUAL dependency where you can credibly walk; unhealthy positions are asymmetric where they can.

The dependency-audit:
- Who depends on me for what?
- Whom do I depend on for what?
- Are these symmetric? If not, what's my risk?

### The Concentration principle (Law 23)

Spreading thin is the most common career mistake. Two patrons at 50% each is weaker than one at 100%. Multiple half-investments produce no flag-bearer for any of them.

The skilled operator picks ONE big patron / project / domain and invests deeply. The reputation and influence compounds.

### The shepherd rule (Law 42)

When a team or organization has chronic dysfunction, look for the single individual whose influence radiates the problem. Trying to fix the dysfunction without addressing the shepherd is futile.

The move: identify the source, isolate or remove them. The sheep usually scatter.

## How to apply

When auditing your network:

1. **List your top 10 relationships by time-investment.**
2. **For each: are they ascending (their power growing), stable, or descending?** Greene's Law 10 â†’ avoid the descending unless you have specific reason.
3. **Are they grateful to you, or do you need them?** Asymmetric dependency in your favor is durable. The reverse isn't.
4. **Who depends on you? For what?** Cultivate this. Don't teach everyone everything (Law 11).

When choosing where to commit (Law 20 vs. Law 23):
- **Law 20 (don't commit) for politically charged situations.** Stay neutral; let others fight; emerge as the option.
- **Law 23 (concentrate) for career building.** One deep mine beats five shallow ones.

When dealing with troublemakers (Law 42):
1. **Identify the single source.** It's usually one person.
2. **Don't try to negotiate.** Some people are "irredeemable" in Greene's frame.
3. **Isolate or banish.** Remove their influence from the system.

When you've already offended someone (Law 19):
- **Assess.** Are they a "wolf in lamb's clothing"? Someone who'll spend years seeking revenge?
- **If yes:** repair urgently or expect ongoing damage.
- **If no:** absorb and move on.

## Common failure modes

- **Loyalty as default.** Greene's Law 2 is provocative because it inverts the default. Friends DO often betray more readily than would-be enemies. But sometimes friends are exactly that. Don't apply mechanically.
- **Isolating in the name of focus.** Law 18 vs. Law 23 in tension. Concentrate forces, yes; but stay in circulation. Both apply.
- **Wrongly identifying the shepherd.** Sometimes the difficult person is a symptom, not a source. Removing them doesn't solve the underlying issue. Diagnose carefully.
- **Over-concentration (Law 23).** A single patron creates single-point-of-failure risk. Concentrate, but maintain optionality.
- **Failing to maintain ascending networks.** Your network ages with you. Continuously add younger / different / lateral relationships.

## Ethical guardrail

This cluster has a cynical edge but contains genuine wisdom:

- **Law 10** (avoid the unhappy) sounds heartless. Steelman: emotional states ARE contagious; close proximity to chronic negativity does affect you. The compassionate version: support the unhappy from caring distance, not enmeshment.
- **Law 14** (pose as friend, work as spy) is straightforwardly manipulative. Detect when applied to you; deploy only in clearly adversarial contexts.
- **Law 23** (concentrate forces) is mostly just good strategic advice with no ethical issue.

The cluster overall: useful for diagnosis, applied carefully.
