## The framework

Ten of Greene's 48 Laws cluster around a single principle: **the less others can read you, the less they can control you.**

### The unifying logic

All ten laws answer one question: **how do I reduce my legibility to others without becoming invisible?**

- **Conceal what you want** (Law 3) â†’ they can't block it.
- **Conceal your effort** (Law 30) â†’ they can't predict your capacity.
- **Conceal your faults' absence** (Law 46) â†’ they don't unite against your perfection.
- **Conceal your superiority over the master** (Law 1) â†’ they don't feel threatened.
- **Conceal your intelligence** (Law 21) â†’ they don't guard against you.

### When this is wisdom vs. when it's pathology

**Wisdom contexts:**
- Adversarial environments (negotiation against bad-faith counterparties).
- Court politics, organizational power struggles, competitive industries.
- Situations where others are clearly playing these laws against you.

**Pathology contexts:**
- Intimate relationships (concealing intentions = betrayal).
- High-trust teams (concealing capacity = sabotaging collective capability).
- Long-term collaborations where transparency compounds trust.

Greene's laws describe what works in zero-sum power contests. They corrode positive-sum relationships when misapplied there.

## How to apply

When entering a power-laden context:

1. **Identify the game.** Is this zero-sum? Adversarial? Coalition-formed-against-coalition? If yes â€” Greene's laws are operationally relevant.
2. **Audit your own legibility.** What signals are you broadcasting that an opponent could read? Plans? Frustrations? Ambitions?
3. **Reduce signal where it doesn't serve you.** Don't broadcast plans. Don't overshow effort. Don't flaunt the master.
4. **For detected manipulation: counter-read.** When someone deploys these against you, name them (privately) and adjust.

## Common failure modes

- **Applying in trust-based contexts.** Concealment in intimate or high-trust relationships destroys what you're trying to protect.
- **Becoming chronically opaque.** Some operators internalize concealment so deeply they can't be read by allies either. Coalitions become impossible.
- **Mistaking concealment for absence of plan.** "I'm being formless" = I have no strategy. Greene's formlessness assumes a CLEAR underlying intent, just unreadable surface.
- **Over-conformity (Law 38).** Pure conformity is just servility. Greene's law is conform OUTWARDLY while preserving inner judgment.

## Ethical guardrail

This entire cluster is high-risk. Three hard checks:

- **Is this context actually adversarial?** Greene's laws presume zero-sum. Misapplied in cooperative contexts they create the adversarial reality they assume.
- **Does deployment serve genuine self-protection or unprovoked predation?** Defending against bad-faith actors is one thing. Initiating concealment in good-faith relationships is another.
- **Would your network endorse this deployment if seen?** If concealment is structurally required to function in your environment, the environment itself may be the problem worth changing.

The laws describe what's instrumentally effective in power contests. They don't endorse the power contests.
