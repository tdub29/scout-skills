## The framework

Ten of Greene's laws cluster around: **don't try to push people; pull them via what they already want.**

### The ten laws

**Law 8 â€” Make people come to you.** Force the other to act. Bait if necessary. Whoever moves first cedes initiative.

**Law 9 â€” Win through actions, never through argument.** Demonstrated truth crushes argued truth. Bypass debate.

**Law 12 â€” Selective honesty as Trojan horse.** One sincere gesture opens defenses dozens of dishonest ones couldn't.

**Law 13 â€” Appeal to self-interest, not gratitude.** "Remember when I helped you" produces resistance. "Here's what's in it for you" produces movement.

**Law 27 â€” Create a cult-like following.** Vague promises, enthusiasm over rationality, rituals, sacrifices. People want to believe; offer them something.

**Law 31 â€” Control the options.** Don't argue your position; design the choice menu so all paths favor you.

**Law 32 â€” Play to people's fantasies.** Truth is harsh; fantasy is sought. People who manufacture romance/escape become magnetic.

**Law 33 â€” Discover each man's thumbscrew.** Everyone has an insecurity or hidden need. Find it; turn it.

**Law 39 â€” Stir up waters to catch fish.** Provoke emotion in opponents while staying calm yourself. Their anger destabilizes them; your calm magnifies.

**Law 43 â€” Work on hearts and minds.** Coercion creates resistance. Seduction creates loyalty. Operate on emotions and weaknesses, not arguments.

### The unifying logic

People don't move because they're argued into it. They move because something inside them is activated. Greene's laws identify the activation points: self-interest, fantasy, insecurity, emotion, opportunity-presented-as-discovered.

The skilled operator doesn't argue with the resistance â€” they reach around it via what the target already wants.

### Three named techniques

**1. The trap that they walk into.**
> Law 8 + Law 31. Don't push them toward what you want; design the situation so they reach it on their own. The trap is set; you wait.

**2. The Trojan horse.**
> Law 12. One genuine gesture (a real favor, a real kindness, a real revealed truth) opens defenses. The defenses don't open under attack; they open under perceived authenticity.

**3. The mirror of their fantasy.**
> Law 32 + Law 33. Find what they wish were true. Confirm it carefully. They'll do almost anything to keep the mirror facing them.

### When this works vs. fails

**Works:**
- Adversarial negotiations (opponent is already strategizing against you).
- Sales contexts where the counterparty is sophisticated.
- Political maneuvering where coalitions form around interest.

**Fails:**
- Long-term collaborative relationships (these laws corrode trust over time).
- Sophisticated audiences who recognize the moves (counter-reading destroys the technique).
- High-stakes contexts where post-hoc analysis is likely.

## How to apply

When operating in adversarial influence:

1. **Don't argue.** Almost all of Greene's persuasion laws bypass argument entirely.
2. **Identify their wants.** Self-interest? Fantasy? Insecurity? Different targets respond to different levers.
3. **Construct the situation.** They come to your conclusion through their own apparent reasoning.
4. **Resist the urge to claim credit.** Once they own the conclusion, you've won. Don't undo it.

When recognizing these moves on you:
- An unusually sincere gesture from someone who normally doesn't bother â†’ Law 12 in action.
- An option-set where every choice favors them â†’ Law 31.
- Someone consistently calm while you're agitated â†’ Law 39.
- A flatterer who's identified exactly what you wish were true â†’ Law 32/33.

The defense: name what's happening privately, slow down, and check if you'd endorse the move if you'd seen it from outside.

## Common failure modes

- **Crude deployment.** Obvious Trojan horses or thumbscrew-finding triggers detection and counter-attack.
- **Applying to allies.** These laws corrode trust. Once an ally recognizes deployment, the relationship is poisoned.
- **Believing your own manipulation.** Operators who deploy these too long start to believe their own framing. Reality intrudes harshly.
- **Failing to deliver after the seduction.** Law 43's seduction creates expectations. Failing to satisfy them produces durable enemies.
- **Cult-like-following (Law 27) without delivering value.** Eventually fails spectacularly.

## Ethical guardrail

This cluster is the most ethically fraught in the book. It's a manipulation manual barely disguised.

Three hard tests before deploying:

- **Are you delivering proportionate value?** Seduction (Law 43) and Trojan horses (Law 12) are exploitative if the underlying offer is hollow.
- **Would the target retrospectively endorse this?** If they discovered the move was made, would they thank you or sue you?
- **What's your alternative?** If you're in a context where these laws are routinely deployed against you, refusing to learn them is unilateral disarmament. Learning to recognize them is defensive; learning to deploy them is offensive.
