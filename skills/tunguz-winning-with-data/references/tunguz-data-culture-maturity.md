
# Tunguz: Data Culture Maturity

## When to use

Trigger when the user asks about building a data-driven culture, reducing management by opinion, improving analytics adoption, or diagnosing why data is not shaping decisions. Cues: "data culture," "too many opinions," "HiPPO," "nobody trusts data," "analytics isn't changing decisions," or "how mature is our data org."

## The framework

Tunguz frames data culture as an operating discipline, not a tooling purchase. A data-driven company uses accessible evidence to challenge opinion, learn faster, and change operations. A weak data culture shows up as breadlines, obscurity, fragmentation, and brawls.

### Maturity dimensions

1. **Access.** Can decision-makers get useful data quickly, or do they wait in breadlines for analysts/engineers?

2. **Findability.** Do people know what data exists, where it lives, and what it means, or is the catalog obscure?

3. **Consistency.** Do teams share definitions, or do rogue spreadsheets and databases fragment truth?

4. **Decision norms.** Are debates settled by evidence and experiments, or by rank, confidence, and opinion?

5. **Curiosity.** Do people ask testable questions and seek surprise, or defend inherited narratives?

## How to apply

1. **Collect symptoms.** List recent decisions, data requests, metric disputes, and analysis bottlenecks.

2. **Score the five dimensions.** Use 1-5 for access, findability, consistency, decision norms, and curiosity.

3. **Identify the dominant failure.** Breadline, obscurity, fragmentation, brawl, or HiPPO/opinion dominance.

4. **Choose operating moves.** Recommend no more than three: metric glossary, self-serve model, embedded analyst, decision-meeting rules, literacy training, experiment brief, or data ownership cleanup.

5. **Return a maturity diagnosis.** Include current stage, evidence, risks, and a 30-day culture-change plan.

## Common failure modes

- **Buying tools before changing meetings.** Self-serve BI does not matter if decisions still reward the loudest opinion.

- **Treating data culture as analyst responsibility.** Culture changes when operators and leaders use data differently.

- **Mistaking dashboards for decisions.** A dashboard viewed weekly but ignored in planning is theater.

- **Punishing inconvenient findings.** People stop bringing evidence when data that contradicts leadership is socially costly.

- **Skipping definitions.** Culture collapses into brawls when revenue, active user, churn, or pipeline mean different things by team.
