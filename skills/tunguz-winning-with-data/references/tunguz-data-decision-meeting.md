
# Tunguz: Data Decision Meeting

## When to use

Trigger for decision meetings, exec reviews, strategy debates, product councils, sales pipeline reviews, or planning sessions where data should influence action but opinion dominates. Cues: "speaking data to power," "HiPPO," "brutal intellectual honesty," "settle this with data," or "our metrics meetings are arguments."

## The framework

Tunguz's brutal intellectual honesty is a meeting norm: evidence must be allowed to contradict status, and opinions must become testable claims. The goal is not data theater; it is a decision that records what evidence changed and what action follows.

### Meeting rules

1. **Separate evidence from interpretation.** Facts, metric movement, and analysis assumptions should be visible before recommendations.

2. **Make opinions testable.** Strong views become hypotheses, predictions, or decision criteria.

3. **Lower rank pressure.** The highest-paid person's opinion should not decide by default. Use pre-reads, silent reads, or structured rounds where needed.

4. **Record decisions and evidence.** A data-backed culture remembers what was believed, what was decided, and what would change the decision.

5. **Define action thresholds.** Data should lead to keep/stop/change/test decisions, not endless analysis.

## How to apply

1. **Frame the decision.** State the choice, options, owner, deadline, and stakes.

2. **Set the evidence packet.** Include metrics, definitions, caveats, counterevidence, and open questions.

3. **Run the meeting.** Start with evidence review, then interpretations, then decision criteria, then action.

4. **Convert disagreement.** Turn unresolved opinion into a metric, experiment, customer research question, or explicit risk.

5. **Return a decision record.** Include decision, evidence used, dissent, next action, owner, review date, and kill/change conditions.

## Common failure modes

- **Data as decoration.** Slides full of metrics that do not affect the decision preserve opinion rule.

- **Weaponized analysis.** Selective metrics used to win a political argument violate brutal intellectual honesty.

- **Rank decides before evidence is heard.** If the decision is effectively made before the data review, the meeting is performative.

- **Endless calls for more data.** More analysis can be a way to avoid commitment; define enough-to-decide thresholds.

- **No decision memory.** Without a record, teams relitigate the same arguments and forget prediction errors.
