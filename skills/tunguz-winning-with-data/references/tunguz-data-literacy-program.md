
# Tunguz: Data Literacy Program

## When to use

Trigger when the goal is raising the organization's ability to use data: training, enablement, office hours, analyst embedding, data camps, data gemba, or self-service adoption. Cues: "data literacy," "teach teams to use data," "analysts are overloaded with basic questions," or "business users misinterpret dashboards."

## The framework

Tunguz argues data literacy is a core responsibility of the data team, but not a classroom-only effort. It comes from tooling, education, walking the data gemba, and embedding analysts close to business questions.

### Program pillars

1. **Question literacy.** Teach people to turn vague curiosity into answerable business questions.

2. **Metric literacy.** Teach definitions, grains, time windows, caveats, and correlation-vs-causation limits.

3. **Tool literacy.** Teach safe self-service paths and when to ask the data team.

4. **Embedded practice.** Analysts join team rituals to observe real decisions and coach in context.

5. **Feedback loops.** Track repeated questions and confusion to improve models, docs, and dashboards.

## How to apply

1. **Segment learners.** Executives, managers, operators, PMs, sales, marketing, finance, and analysts need different practice.

2. **Identify recurring failure modes.** Misread dashboards, wrong grain, metric disputes, tool avoidance, causation errors, or stale exports.

3. **Design the curriculum.** Include concepts, tool workflows, case studies, office hours, and data-gemba attendance.

4. **Embed in rituals.** Put analysts in weekly operating meetings long enough to learn questions and coach usage.

5. **Return a literacy plan.** Include modules, audiences, cadence, owners, and success metrics such as reduced basic requests or faster decisions.

## Common failure modes

- **Training without tool access.** People forget abstract lessons if they cannot practice on real data.

- **Tool rollout without concepts.** Self-service tools can spread wrong answers faster when users do not understand grain or definitions.

- **Analysts as ticket takers.** Literacy improves when analysts observe business context, not just receive requests.

- **One-size-fits-all curriculum.** Executives and frontline operators need different depth and examples.

- **No measurement of enablement.** Track whether training reduces queues, improves question quality, and changes decisions.
