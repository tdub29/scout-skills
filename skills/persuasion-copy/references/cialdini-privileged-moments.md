## The framework

> "Privileged moments: identifiable points in time when an individual is particularly receptive to a communicator's message." (Ch 2)

The core insight of *Pre-Suasion*: the moment BEFORE your ask determines compliance more than the ask itself. Activate the right self-concept in the seconds before the request, and the request lands on prepared ground.

### The Bolkan & Andersen demonstration

Cold-callers asked passersby to fill out a marketing survey.
- Direct ask: 29% compliance.
- "Do you consider yourself adventurous?" â†’ wait for yes â†’ then the ask: 76% compliance.

The pre-suasion question activated an identity. The subsequent request was processed through that identity's filter.

### Three named sub-mechanisms

**1. Positive Test Strategy**
> "Individuals are more likely to search for confirmations of their existing hypotheses than disconfirmations." (Ch 2)

When asked "are you helpful?" most people search memory for evidence of helpfulness and conclude yes. That conclusion is then a public, retrievable commitment that biases the next decision.

**2. Single-Chute Question**
> "The question put them in a particular mind-set. They'd already made a public commitment on the issue." (Ch 2)

A single well-placed question creates an internal commitment to a frame before the real ask arrives. The commitment doesn't have to be elaborate â€” one yes is enough.

**3. The Identity-Activation Window**
The privileged moment is brief â€” minutes, sometimes seconds. The activated self-concept fades. Make the request immediately after the activation, not later.

### What makes a question "privileged"

- **Self-descriptive** (asks about who you are, not what you'll do).
- **Asks for a positive answer** ("Do you consider yourself X?" where X is favorable).
- **Concept-aligned with the upcoming request** (adventurous â†’ try new product; charitable â†’ donate; concerned â†’ sign petition).

A privileged question that asks about an unrelated concept doesn't pre-suade. The alignment is load-bearing.

## How to apply

When designing a request or sequence:

1. **Identify the self-concept you need activated.** What identity, if affirmed, makes saying yes to your request feel natural? (Helpful, generous, adventurous, curious, fair, prudent...)

2. **Craft a self-descriptive question that activates it.** "Do you consider yourself...?" or "Would you describe yourself as...?" Phrased so the natural answer is yes.

3. **Wait for the affirmation.** Don't move on until you have the yes. The verbal commitment is the pre-suasion.

4. **Make your request immediately after.** Don't fill the gap with unrelated talk; the window closes fast.

5. **For high-stakes asks: layer.** "Do you care about X? ... Have you ever Y? ... Then would you Z?"

When detecting pre-suasion being used on you:

1. **Notice the opening question.** Was it a self-descriptive question that primed your identity?
2. **Separate the question's answer from the request's merits.** Just because you ARE adventurous doesn't mean the specific product offer is good for you.

## Common failure modes

- **Wrong concept activated.** Asking "do you consider yourself thrifty?" before pitching a luxury upgrade. The activated identity fights the request.
- **Filler between question and ask.** "Would you say you're charitable? ... So, anyway, the weather... oh and also could you donate?" The window closed.
- **Manipulative question that backfires.** "Don't you agree that..." with a leading frame. People notice; trust drops.
- **Activation that's too generic.** "Are you a good person?" Too vague to bias a specific decision.

## Ethical guardrail

Privileged moments are powerful enough that the ethical edge matters. Three integrity tests:

- **Truth-aligned.** The pre-suasion question should activate a self-concept that's actually relevant to the merits of the request. Activating "adventurous" to pitch a fraudulent investment is misuse.
- **Material disclosure intact.** Pre-suasion that depends on hiding material facts to work crosses into manipulation.
- **Reversibility.** If the target understood the pre-suasion was being used, would they still endorse the resulting decision? If yes, ethical. If no, manipulation.
