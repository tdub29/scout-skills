# Ogilvy: B2B Advertising

## When to Use

Use this when planning or auditing ads for products people buy for their companies: industrial, technical, software, professional services, trade journals, B2B TV, or lead-generation campaigns.

Trigger when the user asks how to advertise to engineers, specifiers, purchasing agents, top management, or business buyers; how to make a technical product interesting; how to generate and analyze inquiries; or how to differentiate a commodity B2B product.

Do not trigger for full market/ICP platform work; route that to `brand-positioning`. Do not trigger for final ad copy execution; route to `copywriting` or `ad-creative`. Do not trigger for selective headline audits, claim substantiation, or test pilots; route to `hopkins-headline-audit`, `hopkins-scientific-claim-check`, or `hopkins-test-campaign`.

## Framework

Ogilvy's B2B premise is practical: advertising rarely closes the sale alone, but it can pave the way for salespeople by pre-selling the product and attracting leads.

1. Reach buying influences: industrial purchases often involve several influencers, including specifiers the sales force may not know.
2. Use proven ad techniques: benefit promises, news, testimonials, helpful information, and demonstrations still work.
3. Make the promise important to the customer, not to the supplier. Responsiveness, support, service, and product performance may outrank company size.
4. Be specific: use percentages, time elapsed, dollars saved, and concrete performance because the reader may be an engineer.
5. Use credible proof: expert testimonials from reputable companies, demonstrations readers can perform themselves, and comparison demonstrations when clear.
6. Play it straight: business buyers care about the product. Do not lure them with irrelevant babies, beagles, or bosoms.
7. Make complex products readable: simple editorial layouts, long copy where warranted, captions as mini-ads, color when economical, and clear calls for more information.
8. Analyze inquiries: survey inquirers, ask salespeople what happened, and relate inquiries to media sources.
9. Segment top management: executives may need broad cost-savings benefits while specialists need technical detail.

## How to Apply

1. Define the buying committee: user, specifier, technical evaluator, purchasing, finance, and top management.
2. Choose the primary reader for this ad and the decision stage it should influence.
3. Translate product features into job-relevant outcomes: savings, uptime, speed, support, compliance, quality, service, or risk reduction.
4. Replace generalities with proof: numbers, timelines, demonstrations, expert testimonials, case evidence, and useful calculations.
5. Audit the creative for straight product relevance, editorial readability, long-copy adequacy, captions, response devices, and contact ease.
6. Define inquiry handling: toll-free number or equivalent CTA, reply card/form, address in body copy, sales follow-up, and media attribution.
7. Decide whether specialists and top management need separate campaigns.

## Output Format

Return a B2B advertising brief:

- Buying influences: who the ad must reach and what each cares about.
- Main promise: job-relevant benefit in specific terms.
- Proof stack: numbers, expert testimonial, demo, useful information, or calculation.
- Creative audit: headline, layout, body copy, captions, CTA, and product relevance.
- Inquiry plan: response path, follow-up owner, quality questions, and media attribution.
- Segmentation note: whether top management and specialists need different messages.

## Common Failure Modes

- Writing for the supplier's pride instead of the buyer's job.
- Assuming technical buyers are bored by the product they are paid to evaluate.
- Using consumer-style borrowed interest that repels serious prospects.
- Omitting specifics because the topic feels complex.
- Counting inquiries without learning intent, sales outcome, or source quality.
- Writing one ad for both specifiers and executives when their decision needs differ.
- Treating this as a full ICP platform or final copywriting task; route those out.
