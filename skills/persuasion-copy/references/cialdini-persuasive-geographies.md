### External geography â€” engineer the physical setting

> Cialdini's own discovery: his university desk surrounded by academic cues produced specialist prose; his home desk surrounded by everyday-life cues produced general-audience writing. Same author; different cues; different output (lines 4018â€“4041).

**3+ moves:**

1. **Match the cue-environment to the task.** Writing for academics? Sit among academic cues. Pitching to entrepreneurs? Surround yourself with entrepreneurial signals. The environment will quietly shape what comes out.

2. **Use photos/representations as cues.** Some consultants display employee photos on conference room walls so meetings stay anchored on the target audience (lines 4100â€“4109). Looking at a photo of "who this is for" primes that audience's frame.

3. **Curate cue-density.** Strong, consistent cues > scattered or contradictory ones. A workspace half-set-up for deep work and half-set-up for collaboration produces neither.

### Internal geography â€” manage where attention goes within yourself

> Cialdini's insight: attention direction activates dormant capacities. Medical students perceive symptoms of diseases they're studying; focused attention on a body location makes sensations there salient (lines 4195â€“4223).

**3+ moves:**

1. **Lyubomirsky's happiness-boost activities** (lines 4333â€“4339):
   - **Count blessings (in writing).** The act of writing focuses attention on what's good.
   - **Cultivate optimism (beforehand).** Direct attention to positive futures before they arrive.
   - **Negate the negative (time-limit dwelling).** When stuck in rumination, time-box: 10 minutes to think about it, then move attention.

2. **Self-affirmation against identity threat.** When facing a stereotype-threat situation, write briefly about a personal value (lines 4533â€“4548). 3. **Order-effect attention.** Recording your year-in-school (achievement) before a test outperforms recording your gender (potential stereotype). Whichever identity is foregrounded shapes the performance (lines 4550â€“4562).

### When the two combine

Best pre-suasion stacks BOTH:
- Curate the physical room (external).
- Direct internal attention before entering (a brief writing exercise, an affirmation, a visualization).

The environment primes one set of associations; internal attention directs them.

## How to apply

When you need to do specific cognitive work:

1. **Name the kind of work.** Specialist vs. general audience? Creative vs. analytical? Sales vs. support?

2. **Engineer the external setting.** Photos, papers, music, lighting, lo-fi vs. hi-fi vibe. Match cues to task.

3. **Direct internal attention before starting.** Brief warm-up writing, a 30-second focusing on the audience, a mental rehearsal.

4. **For threatening situations (high-stakes pitch, evaluation):**
   - Self-affirm beforehand (3-min writing on a core value).
   - Foreground an achievement identity, not a threatened one.
   - Choose the environment that reinforces the desired identity.

5. **For team meetings:** Design the room. Whiteboard on wall = collaboration. Quotes on wall = inspiration. Empty wall = focus on the conversation.

## Common failure modes

- **Treating environment as decoration.** "It's just a meeting room." It's shaping outcomes whether you designed it deliberately or not.
- **Cue-contradiction.** Trying to do creative work in a sterile room with motivational platitudes on the wall â€” cues fight each other.
- **Internal geography ignored.** Walking into a high-stakes moment with no pre-attention work. Default attention will be on the threat.
- **Self-affirmation as positive-thinking.** Affirming "I'm great at everything" doesn't work. Affirming a specific core value DOES (because it's about character continuity, not skill claims).
- **Engineering only the START.** Maintain the geography through the work. A creative room that gets cluttered with distractions degrades back to baseline.

## Ethical guardrail

Persuasive geographies can be used on others (sales rooms designed for purchase) or on yourself (workspace for deep work). Other-directed use needs the same disclosure checks as other pre-suasion. Self-directed use is generally fine ethically.

- **For self-direction:** Engineer freely; you're the beneficiary.
- **For other-direction in negotiation/sales:** If the room is designed to bias the other party against their interests, that's manipulation. Disclosure ("I've laid out the room this way to highlight X") restores integrity.
