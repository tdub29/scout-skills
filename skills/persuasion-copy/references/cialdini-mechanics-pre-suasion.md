## How to apply

**When designing pre-suasion:**

1. **Check link strength** between the activator and the target concept. If weak, the transfer won't happen. Pick a closer link.

2. **Predict audience cognitive capacity** at the moment of decision. Well-rested + time = pre-suasion weak; tired + time-pressured = pre-suasion strong.

3. **Design for "below awareness."** Over-conspicuous priming triggers correction. Subtle wins.

4. **For persistence: install if/when-then plans.** Don't rely on willpower; create automatic cue-action links.

**When defending against pre-suasion:**

1. **Notice the activator.** What concept was foregrounded before the ask?

2. **Trigger your own corrective.** Out loud or silently: "Wait â€” is this question/image/word trying to prime me?" The recognition is most of the defense.

3. **Slow down.** Pre-suasion's leverage shrinks with deliberation time. If a decision feels rushed, take longer.

4. **Pre-commit decisions.** Before facing the pre-suasion (sales meeting, fundraiser), decide your limits. If/when-then for yourself: "If they offer the upgrade, I'll say no."

**When diagnosing a pre-suasion that didn't work:**

1. Was the link from activator to target strong? If no â†’ that's the cause.
2. Was the audience well-rested / sophisticated / time-rich? If yes â†’ cognitive correction fired.
3. Was the priming too conspicuous? If yes â†’ recognition blocked it.

## Common failure modes

- **Weak-link priming.** Trying to prime "achievement" via a peripheral cue (faint background music) instead of a strong cue (visible runner-winning-race image).
- **Conspicuous priming.** Heavy-handed product placement; clumsy "are you helpful?" questions. Detection triggers correction.
- **Pre-suading the wrong moment.** Audience is well-rested and analytical â€” pre-suasion is suppressed. Save it for tired/rushed moments OR switch to deliberative persuasion.
- **No persistence mechanism.** Pre-suasion shifts the moment; without if/when-then, the effect fades.

## Ethical guardrail

The Causes/Constraints/Correctives framework is also a manipulation diagnostic. If your design specifically exploits low cognitive capacity (tired audiences, rushed moments) to bypass deliberation that would lead to a different choice â€” you're manipulating.

The transparency test: would your audience endorse the pre-suasion if they could see it? Subtle isn't the same as deceptive â€” but subtle PLUS exploiting depletion to bypass refusal IS deceptive.
