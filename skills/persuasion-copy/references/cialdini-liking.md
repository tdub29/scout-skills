## The framework

We comply far more readily with people we like â€” and liking is manufacturable through a small set of reliable factors. The Tupperware party is the canonical case: the real persuader isn't the demonstrator, it's the friend who invited you, so liking does the selling.

### Five drivers of liking

1. **Physical attractiveness.** A halo effect: attractive people are automatically rated as more talented, honest, and persuasive.
2. **Similarity.** We like those similar to us â€” background, interests, dress, opinions. Even trivial similarities (shared name, birthday) raise compliance.
3. **Compliments.** Praise â€” even when we suspect it's strategic, even when it's inaccurate â€” increases liking for the flatterer.
4. **Contact and cooperation.** Familiarity through repeated positive contact, and especially **working toward a common goal**, builds liking (Sherif's Robbers Cave: shared superordinate goals dissolved hostility between groups).
5. **Conditioning and association.** We like people and things linked to good experiences and dislike those linked to bad ones (the "blame the weatherman" effect). Hence the luncheon technique (persuasion over good food), celebrity endorsement, and attaching products to attractive models or admired events.

## How to apply

To invoke it ethically:

1. **Find and surface genuine similarities** early â€” real common ground, not invented rapport.
2. **Give honest, specific compliments** for things you actually admire.
3. **Frame the relationship as cooperative** â€” "we're solving this together" â€” and pursue real shared goals.
4. **Associate yourself with genuinely positive context** (be the bearer of good news and useful help, not bad news and friction).

To detect it on yourself:

1. **Run Cialdini's check:** "Have I come to like this person *more than I would expect* in the time we've known each other?" An unusually fast liking spike is the warning light.
2. **Mentally separate the dealer from the deal.** Decide on the offer's merits as if you disliked the person presenting it.
