# Ogilvy: Direct Response Lens

## When to Use

Use this when an ad, mailing, landing offer, or TV spot should generate measurable orders, inquiries, or leads rather than only awareness.

Trigger when the user asks to make an ad more response-oriented, review a direct-mail piece, improve coupon or CTA mechanics, choose variables to test, compare media by orders, or translate general advertising into direct-response discipline.

Do not trigger for broad A/B test program design or market pilots; route structured campaign tests to `hopkins-test-campaign`. Do not trigger for final copy execution; route to `copywriting` or `ad-creative`. Do not trigger for headline selection alone; route to `hopkins-headline-audit`. Do not trigger for claim substantiation alone; route to `hopkins-scientific-claim-check`.

## Framework

Ogilvy treats direct response as advertising's most accountable laboratory.

1. Measure to the dollar: direct mail can isolate results and test variables precisely.
2. Keep a control: once a mailing is profitable, treat it as the control and test ways to beat it.
3. Test few high-leverage variables: positioning, price, payment terms, premiums, and mailing format matter most.
4. Prefer selling substance: strong benefit headlines, good product photographs, long copy, testimonials, short words, black type on white, and organized layouts.
5. Make response devices sell: coupons should be mini-ads with brand, promise, and product image.
6. Add urgency ethically: limited edition, limited supply, last time at this price, or special price for promptness.
7. Judge media by orders: direct-response TV often works in long formats, off-peak times, and dull programs; audience size is not orders.

## How to Apply

1. Define the desired response: order, inquiry, lead, appointment, donation, trial, or renewal.
2. Identify the control or closest historical baseline.
3. Audit the offer: promise, price, terms, premium, guarantee, urgency, and ease of response.
4. Audit the creative: headline, product image, long copy, testimonials, typography, coupon, and CTA.
5. Choose one variable to test next and explain why it is worth testing.
6. Define the measurement: response count, order count, net revenue, conversion quality, retention, or media source.
7. Recommend what to keep, test, cut, or make cheaper without reducing orders.

## Output Format

Return a direct-response audit:

- Response goal: the action and economic measure.
- Control: current baseline or missing-control warning.
- Offer diagnosis: promise, price, terms, premium, guarantee, urgency.
- Creative diagnosis: headline, copy, image, coupon, CTA, legibility.
- Test priority: one variable to test next and expected learning.
- Media measurement: how to attribute orders or inquiries by insertion, station, time, or list.
- Decision: keep, test, revise, cut cost, or rebuild.

## Common Failure Modes

- Measuring CPM, recall, or taste when orders and inquiries are available.
- Testing several variables at once and learning nothing.
- Replacing a profitable control because the team is bored with it.
- Treating coupons, forms, toll-free numbers, and ordering instructions as clerical details rather than sales devices.
- Using short copy for expensive or complex offers.
- Confusing this lens with full pilot planning; use `hopkins-test-campaign` for geography, budget, and scale/no-scale design.
