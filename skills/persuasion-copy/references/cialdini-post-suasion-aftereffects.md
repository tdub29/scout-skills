## The framework

> "Aftereffects." (Ch 14)

Pre-suasion shifts the moment. Post-suasion makes the shift PERSIST. Without post-suasion, the persuasive moment evaporates within hours or days.

### Three persistence mechanisms

**1. Active behavioral commitment (identity-affecting)**

A passive "yes" forgets fast. An ACTIVE commitment â€” written, signed, public â€” sticks.

Evidence:
- Medical no-show rate: 18% when staff filled in the appointment card. 3.5% when patients filled in their OWN appointment card (Ch 14, lines 7700â€“7750). The act of writing the appointment made the patient a co-author of the commitment.
- Voter behavior: people flagged as "early voters" in their community voted at higher rates in FUTURE elections, even years later (Ch 14, lines 7750â€“7800). Public commitment installed an identity that persisted.

**Move:** Don't accept passive agreement. Require active participation in the commitment â€” signature, written promise, public declaration, filled-in date. Frame the commitment as IDENTITY: "You're the kind of person who [follows through / shows up / votes]."

**2. Environmental cues at point-of-decision**

Pre-suasion at the original moment is undone by environmental cues that contradict it later. Conversely, post-suasion cues at the point-of-decision REINFORCE the prior commitment.

- Rodin's *Thinker* statue displayed near an analytical task: subjects solved more problems correctly (Ch 14, lines 7850â€“7900). Visible cue â†’ activated analytical mindset â†’ better performance.
- Hand-washing signage: 45% increase when framed as "protecting patients from infection" vs. 38% when framed as "protecting your own health" (Ch 14, lines 7950â€“8000). The identity-aligned cue (caregiver protecting others, for healthcare workers) outperformed the self-interest cue.

**Move:** Audit where the target physically encounters the moment-of-decision. Place reinforcing cues there. Signs, reminders, visuals â€” using identity-aligned language wherever possible.

**3. If/when-then implementation intentions**

The strongest persistence mechanism. Pre-construct cue-action links so the desired behavior happens automatically without willpower.

Evidence:
- "If [cue], then [action]" â€” medication adherence rose from 55% in control to 79% in the if/when-then group.

**Move:** After a commitment, work with the target to construct 3 if/when-then statements. Write them down. Place one copy where the cue appears.

### Why this matters

Most persuasion fails not at the moment of agreement but in the gap between agreement and action. Three things close that gap:
- **Active commitment** (identity buy-in at moment of decision)
- **Environmental cues** (re-activation at point of execution)
- **If/when-then plans** (automation of execution)

Each on its own helps. All three combined produce the highest persistence rates Cialdini documents.

## How to apply

When a persuasion attempt has reached "yes":

1. **Convert passive yes to active commitment.**
   - Get a signature, written commitment, or public declaration.
   - Frame as identity: "I'm the kind of person who..."

2. **Design point-of-decision cues.**
   - Where will the action happen? Place reinforcing cues there.
   - Identity-aligned language ("protecting patients" not "protecting yourself" for caregivers).

3. **Build if/when-then plans.**
   - "When [cue], then [action]." Specific cue, specific action.
   - Write them down. Give the target a copy.

4. **Combine all three for high-stakes commitments.**
   - Sign-the-pledge moment (active commitment).
   - Visible reminder where the action happens (cue).
   - Written if/when-then statements (automation).

When detecting post-suasion being applied to you:

1. **Notice the active-commitment ask.** "Could you write that down?" "Can you sign here?" â€” locking in your commitment beyond mere agreement.
2. **Notice cues placed strategically.** The fitness app's notification, the gym poster, the printed pledge on your desk.
3. **Notice if/when-then framing.** "When you get home tonight, what time will you start?" â€” that's a pre-execution implementation intention.

## Common failure modes

- **Accepting passive yes and walking away.** No active commitment, no cues, no if/when-then. Commitment evaporates by next week.
- **Cues in wrong location.** The reminder is on your phone; the decision happens at the fridge. Wrong geography.
- **Vague if/when-then.** "When I have time, I'll try to..." That's not an implementation intention. Specific cue, specific action.
- **Identity-misaligned framing.** Telling a self-focused person "this is for others" or a caregiver-identity person "this is for you." Mismatch reduces effect.
- **Over-engineering.** A 12-step post-suasion plan for a small commitment is overkill and breeds resentment.

## Ethical guardrail

Post-suasion makes prior persuasion persist. If the prior persuasion was manipulative, post-suasion compounds the manipulation. Three checks:

- **Did the original persuasion pass ethical tests?** ([references/cialdini-ethical-pre-suasion.md](references/cialdini-ethical-pre-suasion.md)) If not, don't engineer persistence for it.
- **Does the persistence serve the target?** Persistence on a genuinely beneficial behavior change (medication, exercise) is helpful. Persistence on a manipulated commitment (subscription locked in past the reasonable cancel window) is exploitative.
- **Is the target free to revoke?** Active commitments shouldn't trap. Real persistence comes from genuine endorsement, not contractual lock-in.
