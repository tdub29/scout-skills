## The framework

> "I link, therefore I think." (Ch 7)
> "In the family of ideas, there are no orphans. Each notion exists within a network of relatives linked through a shared system of associations." (lines 3440â€“3441)

Cognition is associative. When you activate one concept, the linked network becomes more accessible: words, images, judgments, behaviors. Pre-suasion deploys this by selecting WHICH network to activate before the request.

### 4 named sub-mechanisms

**1. Word-based priming**
Subtle exposure to achievement words (win, attain, succeed, master) before a task improves performance and doubles persistence (lines 3593â€“3596).

Example: Fundraisers given materials on paper with a "runner winning race" image raised 60% more money than control (lines 3609â€“3619). The runner primed achievement, then donation was processed through the achievement frame.

**2. Metaphor as associative gateway**
> "If you want to change the world, change the metaphor." (line 3630)

Crime described as "wild beast" primes catch-and-cage solutions; same crime described as "spreading virus" primes remove-the-conditions solutions. Single-word metaphor swap drove a 22% shift in policy preference â€” larger than gender (9%) or political affiliation (8%) effects (lines 3711â€“3714).

**3. Implicit egoism â€” the self-link**
People overvalue things linked to themselves. Birthdays, names, initials, hometown.

Coca-Cola's "Share a Coke" campaign (150 common first names printed on 100M UK bottles) produced the first sales increase in a decade (lines 3827â€“3848). The link wasn't even strong â€” just a printed first name â€” but the self-association ran through associative networks reliably.

**4. Fluency = liking = perceived truth**
Easy-to-process associations feel better and feel truer.

- Rhyming statements are rated more insightful than identical non-rhyming versions.
- Stocks with easy-to-pronounce ticker codes (KAR) outperform hard-to-pronounce ones (RDO) in early-trading periods.
- Names that are phonetically fluent are trusted more.

## How to apply

When designing pre-suasion through associations:

1. **Identify the target concept.** What concept do you want activated BEFORE your message?

2. **Find a word/image/metaphor in that concept's network.** Not random â€” strongly associated.

3. **Insert it pre-suasively.** In the heading, the imagery, the room, the paper, the opening lines. Before the main message.

4. **For metaphors specifically: try alternatives.** "Crime is a beast" vs. "Crime is a virus" vs. "Crime is a habit" â€” all primed differently. The metaphor isn't decoration; it IS the frame.

5. **For naming: optimize fluency.** Brand names, product names, ticker codes â€” easier-to-pronounce wins on liking, trust, and recall.

6. **For self-links: incorporate the audience.** Their name, hometown, profession, identity. Even shallow links matter.

When detecting associative priming:

1. **Notice the imagery and word choice.** Are you being asked about charitable giving in a room with "runner winning race" photos? Associative.
2. **Notice the metaphor.** Whoever picks the metaphor picks the policy direction.
3. **Notice fluency manipulation.** If the brand name is suspiciously easy or the rhyming slogan unusually slick, fluency is doing work that the substance might not.

## Common failure modes

- **Random word seeding without coherence.** Throwing achievement words into copy doesn't help if the rest of the copy is misaligned. The associations have to converge.
- **Off-target associations.** Activating "luxury" when the audience values "value" â€” wrong network primed.
- **Over-relying on metaphor without substance.** Metaphor sets the frame; the actual argument has to land within the frame.
- **Self-link that feels manufactured.** "Hi [FIRST_NAME], have we got a deal for you" â€” visibly templated, triggers cynicism instead of self-link.
- **Fluency optimization that contradicts the brand.** A complex technical product with a cartoonish brand name reads as untrustworthy.

## Ethical guardrail

Associative priming runs entirely beneath awareness. Three checks:

- **Truth-aligned priming.** Activating networks that ACCURATELY characterize the offer (achievement words for a genuinely achievement-oriented product) is fair. Activating networks that misrepresent (luxury words for a low-quality offer) is deception.
- **Disclosure on the rare occasions it's relevant.** Most associative priming is too small-scale to disclose; egregious manipulation (subliminal threat priming) warrants regulation.
- **Reversibility test.** If the audience saw the priming choice, would they still endorse the outcome? If yes, ethical.
