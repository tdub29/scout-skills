# Heinrichs: Concession Jujitsu

## The Framework

> Every point has its flip side. That's the trick to concession. (Chapter 4)

Heinrichs treats concession as a logos tool and a social lubricant. Rather than refuting the other person's point, you find the part you can grant, use its flip side, and pivot toward your desired choice. It turns fighting into arguing.

### Three Sub-Rules

**1. Concede something real.**

The concession must be recognizable, not fake: "You're right that privacy matters" lands better than "I understand your concern" if you do not actually engage it.

**2. Find the flip side.**

Every point has a reverse: "We hardly go out anymore" can become "that's because I want you to myself" before pivoting to a plan.

**3. Pivot to future choice.**

Concession without direction becomes surrender. After the concession, move to what should happen next.

**4. Maintain agreeability.**

The tone matters. The goal is to argue without appearing to argue, reducing confrontation while still steering.

## How To Apply

Draft a concession-jujitsu script:

1. **State their objection fairly.**
2. **Identify what you can honestly concede.**
3. **Name the flip side or shared value.**
4. **Bridge to a future choice.**
5. **Ask for the next step.**

## Common Failure Modes

- **Fake concession.** "You're right, but..." where everything after "but" cancels the concession.
- **Over-conceding.** Giving away the decision rather than the usable premise.
- **No pivot.** Agreement creates warmth but no movement.
- **Sarcastic concession.** "Sure, if you want the company to collapse..." is contempt wearing a concession mask.
- **Conceding values you cannot stand behind.** Agreement is not worth losing ethical footing.
