# Claude Hopkins: Test Campaigns

## When to use

Trigger when the decision is *whether or how to scale* â€” and opinions outrun evidence. Concrete cues:

- New product, package, or message slated for national spend without buyer proof
- Meeting deadlock: "I like it" vs "I don't" with no keyed data
- Agency or creative refresh proposed but current ads are "fairly well"

- Need to compare cost per customer and payback before financing rollout

Don't trigger for: rewriting headlines (`hopkins-headline-audit`), strengthening claims (`hopkins-scientific-claim-check`), body copy discipline (`hopkins-reason-why-copy`), or service-first offer copy (`hopkins-offer-service`). This skill owns *experiment design and scale gates*.

Additional trigger contexts:

- Board wants national rollout; ops wants proof
- Two agencies pitching â€” pick by bake-off not deck quality
- Product team sure new flavor wins; marketing wants verification
- User references bookstoskill `offer-test-plan`
- Startup "blitz scaling" without unit economics

## The framework

### Core thesis

Almost any question can be answered cheaply, quickly, and finally by a test campaign. That's the way to answer â€” not arguments around a table. Go to the court of last resort: **the buyers of your product.**

On every new project: will it sell profitably? You and your friends may like it; the majority may not. Rival products may be entrenched. Users may buy once and not repeat. The article may last too long. Most advertising may waste on small percentages. Tastes differ â€” none of us know enough people's desires to average a viewpoint.

In old days advertisers ventured on opinion â€” few guessed right, many wrong, advertising disasters common. Now **let the thousands decide what the millions will do.** Small venture, watch cost and result. Learn what a thousand customers cost â†’ know almost exactly what a million will cost. Learn what they buy â†’ know what a million will buy. Averages on small scale always hold. Prove the undertaking safe before spreading out.

### The standard test loop

1. Try project in **four or five towns** (representative averages)
2. Use **sample offer or free package** to start users quickly
3. Measure **cost per customer started**
4. Wait: do users buy samples? Will they **continue**? How much? How long until **profit returns selling cost**?
5. Cost **$3,000â€“$5,000** â€” not all lost even if unpopular; some sales made; nearly every test eventually returns entire cost
6. Sometimes ad cost comes back **before bills are due** â€” advertise without investment
7. Sometimes **three months** to profit payback â€” still sure; finance scale accordingly
8. Branch out only when playing the **safe side of a hundred-to-one shot**

### Questions tests settle

- **Product/form:** Food advertiser thought new form would win â€” coupon test in few towns, users wrote disapproval â€” costly mistake avoided ($1,000 test). Another form: 91% of thousands of women voted yes â€” unique product launched.
- **Creative/agency:** Mediocre advertising continues because change risks alliances. Skilled agent tests few towns without disturbing general campaign â€” compares results, proves skill at small cost. "Go out for a week and prove yourself."
- **Method improvement:** Five years, fifty separate plans on one food advertiser â€” constant cost reduction; best plan cut selling cost **75 percent** (four times more effective). Mail order advertisers try plan after plan â€” general advertisers should be equally business-like.

### Scientific advertising as chemistry

Suppose a chemist stated arbitrarily which compound is best. You'd little respect his opinion â€” he makes hundreds of tests to know. How long before advertisers apply that exactness?

Coupons and keyed returns (Chapter 1) are the instrumentation â€” test campaigns are the **controlled experiment**.

### Twenty-six winners pattern

Hopkins notes the largest advertiser in the world makes a business of starting projects one by one, finding winners â€” **twenty-six** earning many millions yearly. Test campaigns aren't modesty; they're **how scale empires get built** without gambling the company on each launch.

### Opinion purge (old vs new advertising)

Old: advertisers ventured on opinion â€” few guessed right, many wrong, disasters common, cost per customer unknown, payback maybe never. New: let thousands decide for millions â€” **ventures may fail but failures are not disasters** â€” losses trifling when tests are small and traced.

Your plan should quantify **maximum acceptable test loss** vs **cost of national mistake**.

### When bills come due (cash timing)

Sometimes ad cost returns **before bills are due** â€” ideal, advertise without investment. Sometimes **three months** to profit payback â€” still sure if you finance scale accordingly. Test plans must output **cash timing**, not only boolean success.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **Write the business question as a buyer verdict.** Not "Do we like the headline?" but "Will enough buyers start, repeat, and pay back acquisition cost in X months?" List failure modes Hopkins names: no repeat, too narrow appeal, entrenched rival, wrong form factor.

2. **Define the test cell:**
   - *Geography:* 4â€“5 average towns (or digital geo equivalents with clean read)
   - *Duration:* long enough for repeat behavior signal
   - *Offer mechanic:* sample, free package, coupon â€” start users quickly (link `hopkins-offer-service`)
   - *Creative:* variant(s) to test â€” hold body constant when testing headlines (link `hopkins-headline-audit`)

3. **Set primary metrics (Hopkins order):**
   - Cost per customer **started**
   - Conversion from sample to purchase
   - **Repeat** purchase rate / volume
   - Time to **profit payback** of selling cost
   - Final verdict on **cost per dollar of sale** â€” not raw replies (worthless replies poison averages)

4. **Budget and downside.** Plan $3â€“5K class spend (adjust for era/channel). Acknowledge partial recovery via sales even on failure â€” tests are learning investments, not pure loss.

5. **Define scale gates before running:**
   - *Scale:* payback within acceptable window, repeat confirms model
   - *Iterate:* promising but wrong form/headline â€” retest
   - *Kill:* no path to payback â€” national disaster avoided

6. **Optional bake-off.** New agent or creative: parallel towns, same product, keyed comparison â€” no commitment until figures decide.

7. **Produce the deliverable â€” test campaign plan:**
   - Hypothesis and buyer question
   - Markets / audience / channels
   - Offer and creative variants
   - Metrics, tracking (coupon/key/code)
   - Timeline and budget
   - Decision rules (scale / iterate / kill)
   - What national rollout finances if scale gate passes

8. **Sanity-check with the opinion purge.** List every opinion-based reason to skip testing. Hopkins: arguments around a table are worthless vs. buyers.

## Common failure modes

- **Testing without keyed measurement.** Unkeyed national ads are "merely presumed to pay" â€” Hopkins's Chapter 21 warning. Every test must trace cost to customer and sale.

- **Counting replies instead of customers.** Too many worthless replies vs. valuable ones â€” final conclusions on cost per customer or cost per dollar of sale.

- **Friends-and-team taste tests.** "You and your friends may like it" â€” irrelevant. Thousands must decide for millions.

- **National launch to learn what a town would teach.** Reverse of Hopkins â€” maximum risk instead of hundred-to-one safety.

- **Stopping at first purchase.** Repeat and duration matter â€” product may "last too long" or fail after trial.

- **Skipping sample mechanics.** Tests use samples/free packages to accelerate user starts â€” without them, read is too slow and inconclusive.

- **One headline tweak without hold constant.** Change one variable per test when possible â€” Hopkins compares plans systematically.

- **Accepting mediocre because change is uncomfortable.** Test lets new agent prove skill in few towns â€” alliances shouldn't block measurement.

- **Confusing with `ab-testing`.** AB infrastructure is tooling. Hopkins test campaigns are **strategic buyer courts** with geography, offer economics, and scale gates â€” compose both for implementation.

### Scale math (thousand â†’ million)

Hopkins's extrapolation logic:

- Learn what **1,000 customers cost** â†’ know almost exactly what **1,000,000** will cost
- Learn what they **buy** â†’ know what a million will buy
- Averages on small scale **always hold** when towns are average and methods stable

Your plan must define the **unit of extrapolation** (geo, channel, cohort) and refuse to scale if the test cell isn't representative.

### Metric hierarchy (strict order)

1. **Cost per customer started** (not raw impressions)
2. **Quality of start** â€” worthless replies vs valuable (Hopkins Ch 1)
3. **Repeat / continue rate** â€” one-time curiosity vs product fit
4. **Volume per buyer**
5. **Time to profit payback** of selling cost
6. **Cost per dollar of sale** â€” final verdict

Skipping straight to ROAS on day three violates Hopkins's wait-for-repeat instruction.

### Scale / iterate / kill rules (template)

**SCALE when:**  
- Payback â‰¤ your finance threshold  
- Repeat confirms ongoing demand  
- Cost per dollar of sale beats incumbent or baseline  

**ITERATE when:**  
- Starts strong, repeat weak â†’ product or message mismatch  
- Headline chord wrong but offer viable â†’ `hopkins-headline-audit` retest  

**KILL when:**  
- No path to payback at realistic scale  
- Buyer verdict negative on form (food package rejection story)  

Document which branch before spending â€” prevents moving goalposts.

### Digital adaptation

Hopkins used towns; modern equivalents:

- **Geo-lift tests** (Meta/Google geo experiments)
- **Holdout markets** for DTC brands
- **Email 10% sends** before full list (cheap opinion purge)
- **Landing page split** with keyed checkout codes

Principles unchanged: **buyers decide, small scale, traced dollars.**

### Agency bake-off protocol

When mediocre ads continue because change feels risky:

1. Incumbent runs in control geos  
2. Challenger runs in matched test geos  
3. Same product, same offer economics  
4. Compare cost per customer and payback â€” not opinions  
5. "Go out for a week and prove yourself" â€” Hopkins's salesman analogy  

No commitment until figures decide. Large percentage of advertising would change hands if applied.

## Extended failure modes (agent checklist)

- **Test too small to extrapolate.** 50 starts â€” noise, not Hopkins thousand-scale logic.
- **Stopping at vanity metric.** Impressions, likes, clicks â€” not cost per customer started.
- **Changing offer mid-test.** Invalidates comparison â€” hold offer constant when testing creative.
- **National rollout during test.** Contaminates control â€” finish test cell first.
- **Ignoring repeat signal.** One-time spike declared victory â€” Hopkins waits for continue/buy again.
- **No kill discipline.** Sunk cost fallacy after failed test â€” national disaster follows.
