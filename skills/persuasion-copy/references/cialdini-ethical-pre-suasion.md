## The framework

> "A Pre-Pre-Suasive Consideration." (Ch 13)

Pre-suasion is powerful, which is why Cialdini devotes an explicit chapter to ethics. His argument isn't moral handwaving â€” he documents specific MEASURABLE COSTS to organizations that deploy unethical pre-suasion.

### The three integrity tests

**1. Reputational integrity test**

> SEC study of 585 firms: 41% average market-value loss following revelation of unethical conduct. (Ch 13)

Pre-suasion that masks true intent or hides material facts produces large, durable reputation damage if exposed.

**Test:** "If the target discovers we directed their attention toward X to bias them toward Y, does our reputation survive?" If no, the tactic is too risky to deploy even on pure self-interest grounds.

**2. Material-disclosure integrity test**

Legitimate pre-suasion directs attention to TRUE features of the offer ("note the craftsmanship") without hiding contradictory facts. Manipulation hides material facts or creates false scarcity/urgency.

**Test:** "Are we omitting material information that would reverse the target's decision?" If yes, the tactic is manipulation, not persuasion. Pre-suasion + concealment of material facts = deception.

**3. Organizational health test â€” the "Triple-Tumor"**

> Organizations that deploy manipulative persuasion internally (with employees) develop three measurable pathologies (Ch 13):

a) **Poor employee performance** â€” moral stress reduces output.
b) **High turnover among ethical employees** â€” they leave the manipulative environment.
c) **Cheater retention** â€” dishonest performers fit in and stay; honest ones leave. Composition of the workforce shifts toward manipulation-tolerance.

Net effect: organizations that use manipulation become STAFFED by people for whom manipulation is normal. The institution decays.

**Test:** "Would deploying this tactic, repeatedly, on employees produce stress + turnover + cheater-retention?" If yes, the cost will hit the organization regardless of whether external customers notice.

### What ethical pre-suasion looks like

Cialdini doesn't argue against pre-suasion; he argues for its ethical deployment. Ethical pre-suasion:

- **Directs attention to true features** of an offer that genuinely matter to the target's decision.
- **Doesn't conceal material facts** that would change the decision.
- **Can survive transparency** â€” if disclosed, the audience would still endorse it.
- **Aligns with the target's interest**, not against it.

## How to apply

Before deploying pre-suasion:

1. **Run the reputational test.** What's the worst-case if this becomes public knowledge? Could you defend it in writing?

2. **Run the material-disclosure test.** List 3 facts about the offer that, if known, might cause the target to refuse. Are any of them being hidden? If yes, redesign or disclose.

3. **Run the triple-tumor test.** If your team had to deploy this tactic repeatedly, would it create moral stress? Would ethical employees quit over it? If yes, the institution will pay.

4. **Apply the transparency standard.** If the target saw the pre-suasion choice, would they endorse the resulting decision? If yes â†’ ethical. If no â†’ manipulation.

5. **For gray cases: disclose.** Adding a brief disclosure ("we've designed this to highlight X") usually preserves the pre-suasion's effectiveness while restoring integrity.

## Common failure modes

- **Reasoning ethics from outcomes.** "This worked, so it was fine." But the triple-tumor and reputation costs accumulate over time, not in the immediate result.
- **Confusing legal with ethical.** Many manipulative tactics are legal. The framework asks a different question.
- **Treating attention-direction as inherently neutral.** Most attention-direction is fine; specific patterns (hiding material disclosures, manufacturing fake urgency) cross lines.
- **Single-actor ethics.** "I'd never do that" vs. "but the team does it." Organizations adopt the practices of their dominant influencers; if you don't push back on tactics you'd personally reject, you've consented institutionally.
- **Ignoring the triple-tumor signal.** Treating employee turnover and morale issues as separate problems from sales tactics. They're often the same problem.

## Integrity check (meta)

This framework can itself be misused as a checklist for laundering manipulation. The actual integrity work is harder than checking boxes:

- **Honest self-assessment** about whether you're complying with the SPIRIT of the tests or just gaming them.
- **External review** when stakes are high. Get someone who isn't invested in the tactic to evaluate it.
- **Track institutional indicators** (turnover, employee surveys, customer complaints) â€” these reveal what individual self-assessment misses.
