# Translate Expert Truths

Convert what an expert can prove into what a specific reader can understand, care about, and act on. Preserve the underlying truth while changing its frame.

Read [references/source-method.md](references/source-method.md) when provenance or the original example matters. For PRTL positioning or white-paper work, also read [references/prtl-white-paper-application.md](references/prtl-white-paper-application.md).

## Workflow

### 1. Lock the intention

State:

- Primary reader and decision context
- Desired reader action
- Current belief
- Required belief
- Evidence needed for that shift
- Emotional tension
- Reason to care now

Do not translate truths until these are explicit. Relevance depends on the reader and intended decision.

### 2. Build the truth inventory

List 8-12 statements the source owner believes they know. Rewrite each as one falsifiable sentence.

For each statement, attach:

- Direct evidence
- Classification: verified fact, supported inference, assumption, aspiration, or contradiction
- Confidence
- Invalidation condition

Exclude advice, benefits, slogans, and desired positioning. This step captures territory, not messaging.

### 3. Run the truth-to-stakes ladder

For every verified fact or supported inference, answer in order:

1. **Truth:** What exactly is true?
2. **Mechanism:** Why is it true? What causes or enables it?
3. **Situation:** When does this become visible in the reader's life or decision?
4. **Stakes:** What recognized problem, desire, risk, cost, identity, or tradeoff does it change?
5. **Belief shift:** What should the reader believe or reconsider?
6. **Action:** What reasonable next action follows?

Ask "why does that matter to this reader?" again when an answer remains technical. Stop when the chain reaches a concrete situation the reader already recognizes. Do not force an emotional payoff where evidence supports only functional utility.

### 4. Translate, then preserve the receipt

Write an audience-facing claim beside the original expert truth. Keep the evidence link visible so simplification remains reversible.

Useful transformations:

- Technical definition -> familiar situation
- Feature -> changed experience or decision
- Mechanism -> reason to believe
- Abstract benefit -> observable outcome
- Broad category claim -> specific contrast
- Expert correction -> consequential misconception

Prefer: `[familiar situation]: [accurate consequence], because [verified mechanism].`

Use contrarian framing only when the contradiction is material, evidence-backed, and more useful than a plain statement. Never strengthen a claim merely to make it interesting.

### 5. Apply fidelity and ethics gates

Reject or revise a translated claim when any answer is "no":

- Does evidence support every material word?
- Is the claim no stronger than its source truth?
- Does it distinguish current capability from roadmap?
- Would the intended reader recognize the situation?
- Does it avoid jargon or define unavoidable jargon concretely?
- Does it create relevance without manufactured fear, urgency, authority, or social proof?
- Can a skeptical reader trace the claim back to evidence?

Preserve uncertainty. A faithful modest claim beats a memorable false one.

### 6. Rank and return

Score each ladder from 1-5 on evidence strength, reader relevance, specificity, novelty, and decision usefulness. Subtract risk for overclaiming, ambiguity, or dependence on missing evidence.

Return this table:

| ID | Expert truth | Evidence/class | Mechanism | Reader situation | Stakes | Belief shift | Audience-facing claim | Risk/gap | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then return:

- Three strongest message foundations
- Truths that should remain internal or technical
- Missing evidence that blocks stronger claims
- Claims to avoid
- Questions the source owner must answer

## White-paper sequence

Use this skill after the evidence and positioning audit, before outlining or drafting:

1. Audit reality and produce classified truths.
2. Translate truths into reader stakes.
3. Select the governing thought and supporting argument.
4. Draft only from selected, evidenced ladders.
5. Copy-edit without deleting qualifications that carry truth.

Do not use this method to discover market facts, choose a category by itself, or turn aspirations into present-tense claims.

## Composition

- Use `deep-research` and repository inspection to establish truths.
- Use `grill-for-unknowns` to expose assumptions and contradictions.
- Use `customer-research` to validate situations and language.
- Use `product-marketing` to decide category, audience, alternatives, and differentiation.
- Use `marketing-psychology`, `behavioral-economics`, and `behavior-design` to test relevance and ethical influence.
- Use `persuasion-copy` for credible, concrete, memorable expression.
- Use `minto-pyramid-principle` to assemble the argument after claims survive validation.
- Use `llm-council` to challenge consequential message choices, never to manufacture evidence.
