## The framework

### Core thesis ("Think Small, Act Big")
A **mission** â€” a unifying focus that spans jobs and answers "what should I do with my life?" â€” is one of the most powerful traits you can buy with career capital (Pardis Sabeti's "rid the world of ancient diseases" energizes her whole life). But it's the *ficklest* trait, and it requires two things people usually get wrong: (1) you must have **career capital first**, and (2) capital alone isn't enough â€” you need tactics to convert a vague mission into a success. The title captures the order: **think small** (patiently master a narrow niche to the cutting edge) then **act big** (pursue the mission you find there with zeal).

### Missions live in the adjacent possible (so capital comes first)
Borrowing Steven Johnson's "adjacent possible": breakthroughs sit just beyond the current cutting edge, visible only to those who've reached it (which is why discoveries happen as simultaneous "multiples"). A good career mission is an innovation waiting in the adjacent possible of *your* field â€” so you can only see it after you've gotten to the cutting edge, i.e., after you've built capital. **Sarah and Jane failed by reaching for a mission before reaching the cutting edge** â€” big thinking matched only by small, ineffectual acts. Pardis took *years* (math â†’ medicine â†’ infectious disease â†’ the Nature paper) before her mission crystallized. Mission is career-capital theory in action: skip the capital and you get "lots of enthusiasm but very little to show for it."

### Missions require little bets (capital â†’ idea â†’ execution)
Even with capital, a mission idea doesn't execute itself (Newport had notebooks of missions and acted on none). The bridge is **little bets**: small, concrete projects (Peter Sims) â€” completable in under a month, forcing you to create new value, returning concrete feedback. Chris Rock tests jokes in tiny clubs; Kirk French tried a DVD, then exploratory documentary footage, then the Armchair Archaeologist film â€” and *that* unplanned little bet became the Discovery Channel show. Pardis bet on three labs before computational genetics hit. **Tentativeness, not boldness, converts a general mission into a specific success.** Little bets also double as deliberate practice.

### Missions require marketing â€” the Law of Remarkability
A mission-driven project succeeds when it's remarkable in **two** ways:
1. **It compels people to remark** about it (Seth Godin's "purple cow" vs. "brown cow"). Giles's package of Ruby command-line tools = useful brown cow; Archaeopteryx, code that composes its own techno = purple cow. Newport's own purple cow: a book arguing "follow your passion" is *bad* advice.
2. **It's launched in a venue that supports remarking** â€” infrastructure built to spread word: peer-reviewed journals (Pardis), open-source communities + a conference tour (Giles), television (Kirk), blogging (Newport). A purple cow released into a closed/quiet venue never gets seen.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **Mission-readiness check.** Is the user at (or near) the cutting edge of a defined field â€” enough capital to *see* the adjacent possible? If not: verdict = not ready; go build capital (`career-capital-theory.md`), keep a mission notebook, revisit later. This prevents the Sarah/Jane failure.
2. **Survey the adjacent possible.** From the cutting edge, list the innovations/directions newly visible â€” the candidate missions. Keep them tentative.
3. **State a tentative mission** as a rough guideline (Newport's: "apply distributed algorithm theory to interesting new places"). It will evolve; don't over-commit.
4. **Design 2â€“3 little bets.** Each: â‰¤ ~1 month, forces new value creation, returns concrete feedback. Cap them so each gets real attention; add deadlines.
5. **Run the bets; read the feedback.** Abort losers, double down on the bet that catches traction â€” don't pre-decide which will win (Kirk couldn't have).
6. **Apply the Law of Remarkability to the promising project.** (a) Would someone who sees it be compelled to tell others? If not, it's a brown cow â€” reshape it. (b) Are you launching it into a venue with spreading infrastructure? If not, change the venue.
7. **Sanity-check ordering:** confirm you thought small (patient niche mastery) *before* acting big (zealous pursuit). Reversed order = enthusiasm with nothing to show.

## Common failure modes

- **Mission before capital.** Reaching for a world-changing focus from far below the cutting edge. Produces vague, un-fundable visions (Jane's nonprofit, Sarah's panic).
- **Big bet instead of little bets.** Committing years to one bold plan up front instead of probing with cheap experiments. You lose the feedback that finds the unexpected winner.
- **Brown-cow projects.** Doing solid, useful, *forgettable* work and wondering why the mission isn't lifting your career. Useful â‰  remarkable.
- **Right cow, wrong venue.** A genuinely remarkable project hidden in a venue with no spreading infrastructure (closed-source, paid-only, a drawer). Striking but unseen.
- **Forcing it.** Treating mission as a moment of inspiration plus courage. Newport: it's a *lifestyle* (patient capital + ceaseless scanning of the adjacent possible), not a step you complete.
- **Mistaking a hobby-interest for a mission.** Many interests â‰  a mission; the mission emerges from the cutting edge, not from introspection about what you like.

## Apply to Trevor's search

- **Readiness first.** At 23 you may be at the cutting edge of a *narrow* DS niche but not the field â€” so expect your durable mission to crystallize *later* (Pardis-style), and don't let "I don't have a grand mission yet" become a reason to thrash. A tentative mission ("apply [your specialty] to [domain] to produce results others can't") is enough to steer; it underpins the same moats as `strategy/leveling-framework.md`.
- **Little bets are your portfolio/reputation engine.** In a field where AI commoditizes artifacts, the AI-proof asset is *reputation for judgment*. Build it with sub-month bets that create new value and return concrete feedback: a sharp internal analysis that changes a decision, a write-up, a small OSS contribution, a talk, a benchmark. Cap at 2â€“3 active, deadline them, track the hours.
- **Make the signature work a purple cow, not a brown cow.** A competent dashboard is a brown cow; a result/method/take that makes a hiring manager or peer say "you have to see this" is a purple cow. When choosing what to be known for, optimize for *remarkable*, not merely *useful* â€” this is what shows up in interviews and referrals.
- **Pick a remarking venue.** A purple cow in a private repo or an unshared deck dies quietly. Launch where DS reputation spreads: a public writeup, OSS, a conference talk, the right internal audience. The venue is half the law.
- **For the higher-comp pivot specifically:** the mission/remarkability layer is what differentiates "a senior DS" from "*the* DS known for X." That differentiation is the leverage in negotiation and the reason the next role chases you rather than the reverse.
