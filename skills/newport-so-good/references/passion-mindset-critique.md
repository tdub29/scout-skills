## The claim
The **passion hypothesis** â€” "the key to occupational happiness is to figure out what you're passionate about, then find a job that matches it" â€” is bad, and often dangerous, advice. Three reasons:

1. **Passion is rare.** In Vallerand's study, 84% of students had a passion but <4% of those passions were work-related (mostly dance, hockey, sports). Most people have no pre-existing *career* passion to match. (Jobs himself wasn't passionate about tech before Apple; Apple was a small-time scheme that took off.)
2. **Passion takes time / is a side effect of mastery.** Wrzesniewski: the strongest predictor of seeing work as a "calling" was *years on the job*, not the type of work. Self-Determination Theory says motivation comes from **competence + autonomy + relatedness** â€” not from matching work to a pre-existing passion. Passion follows getting good; it doesn't precede it.
3. **The passion mindset is dangerous.** Believing a magical "right job" exists breeds chronic job-hopping and self-doubt when reality (any reality) falls short â€” the Thomas-at-the-monastery and "quarterlife crisis" pattern. Since "follow your passion" became ubiquitous (post-1970, *What Color Is Your Parachute?*), job satisfaction has *fallen*.

Caveat Newport grants: for a rare few (some athletes, the occasional prodigy) passion came first. These are exceptions; their rareness proves the rule. The takeaway is the book's spine: **working right trumps finding the right work.**

## How to apply
1. **Detect passion-framing.** User says "follow my passion," "find my calling," "what am I meant to do," "I'm not passionate about this so it must be wrong."
2. **Reframe in one move.** Replace "what work matches my passion?" with "what rare & valuable skill will I build, and how?" Passion is the *output* of a working life well lived, not the input you search for.
3. **Defuse the danger.** If the user is using passion-logic to justify a leap (quit to do what they love), flag the dangerous-mindset risk and route to `the-control-traps-and-financial-viability.md`.
4. **Route forward.** Send them to `career-capital-theory.md` (what to build) â†’ `deliberate-practice-for-knowledge-work.md` (how) â†’ control/mission references (how to invest it).

## Common failure modes
- **Re-litigating passion philosophically.** The point is pragmatic, not a debate about whether passion exists. Don't argue metaphysics; redirect to capital.
- **Over-correcting into nihilism.** "Passion is fake" is wrong. Passion is real and worth wanting â€” it just *follows* mastery. Disqualifiers #2/#3 (useless work, bad people) still mean some jobs should be left.
- **Letting "I'm not passionate yet" justify a plateau or a job-hop.** That feeling is expected early-career; it's not a leave signal by itself.
