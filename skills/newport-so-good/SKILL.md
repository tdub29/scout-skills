## The 4-Rules logic at a glance

The book is one argument in four moves. The spine: **working right trumps finding the right work.**

1. **Rule #1 â€” Don't follow your passion.** "Match a job to a pre-existing passion" is rare-to-impossible and dangerous advice. Passion is a *side effect* of mastery, not a prerequisite you discover. â†’ clears the ground for everything else.
2. **Rule #2 â€” Be so good they can't ignore you.** The traits that make great work great (creativity, impact, control) are rare and valuable, so you must offer something rare and valuable in return: **career capital** (rare & valuable skills), built via the **craftsman mindset** and **deliberate practice**.
3. **Rule #3 â€” Turn down a promotion (control).** Invest capital in **control** â€” the dream-job elixir â€” but mind the two **control traps** (no capital â†’ unsustainable; enough capital â†’ employer resists) and use the **law of financial viability** (only pursue control people will pay for) to tell them apart.
4. **Rule #4 â€” Think small, act big (mission).** Invest capital in a **mission**, found in the **adjacent possible** (so capital first), executed via **little bets**, and made to land via the **law of remarkability** (purple cow + a venue that spreads it).

**Everything is gated by capital.** Control and mission are *purchases*; career capital is the currency. Skip the capital step and you get Jane (broke), Feuer (food stamps), Sarah (paralyzed) â€” enthusiasm with nothing to show.

## Common failure modes (hub-level)

- **Summarizing instead of deciding.** This skill exists to drive a choice. If you produce a book summary instead of a diagnosis + decision + plan, you've failed the brief.
- **Skipping the capital gate.** Routing straight to control or mission when the user has no rare-&-valuable skill yet. Capital first, always.
- **Confusing the two control decisions.** "What to build" (`career-capital-theory`) is not "should I leave / negotiate" (`the-control-traps-and-financial-viability`). Match the reference to the actual decision.
- **Letting "I'm not passionate" be a leave signal.** It isn't, by itself â€” run the real disqualifier/plateau tests.
- **Treating mission as inspiration + courage.** Mission is a lifestyle of patient capital + scanning the adjacent possible + little bets, not a eureka moment.
