
# Sun Tzu: Terrain & Nine Situations

## When to use

Trigger when the question is **what ground am I standing on, and what does that ground permit?** Concrete cues:

- Committed deep in a competitor's core market with fortified positions behind you
- Home-turf advantage vs. fighting on someone else's territory (regulatory, geographic, platform)
- Stalemate where neither side benefits from moving first (pricing truce, patent gridlock)
- Bottleneck position â€” single channel, single standard, single enterprise buyer
- High ground already occupied by rival (incumbent platform, category king)
- Remote market where provoking battle is expensive and timing is wrong
- Team dysfunction that looks like "bad strategy" but is actually leadership calamity
- User mentions Sun Tzu terrain, nine grounds, desperate ground, narrow passes, or six calamities

Don't trigger for: macro factor comparison before entry (use `sun-tzu-five-factors-audit`), intelligence/stratagem choice (use `sun-tzu-know-enemy-self`), disaster response geography (use `dr-map-creation`), org stakeholder power maps (use `stakeholder-map`), or military/violent contexts.

## The framework

### Core thesis

Sun Tzu treats **ground** as a variable independent of courage or spend. Two classification systems layer:

**Chapter X â€” Six kinds of terrain (physical/strategic ground):** the shape of the field â€” how movement, supply, and position work.

**Chapter XI â€” Nine situations (commitment ground):** how far you are from home, how trapped you are, and what psychology that creates.

The same organization can sit on favorable terrain but wrong situation (strong product in a dispersive home market during internal politics) â€” or desperate situation on good terrain (burning boats after correct entry). The skill diagnoses both layers and applies Sun Tzu's paired doctrine: *On dispersive ground, fight not. On desperate ground, fight.*

Business translation preserves Sun Tzu's structure: **terrain = market structure; nine situations = commitment and exit options.**

### Six kinds of terrain (Chapter X)

**1. Accessible ground**

Both sides can move freely. Doctrine: occupy raised sunny spots first; guard supply lines. Business: open competitive market, low switching costs â€” speed to scale and logistics (customer success, fulfillment, cash) decide advantage.

**2. Entangling ground**

Can abandon but hard to re-occupy. Doctrine: if rival unprepared, sally and win; if rival prepared and you fail, return impossible â€” disaster. Business: positions that are easy to enter but hard to rebuild if lost (SEO rank, app store featuring, sole integrator relationship). Caution on opportunistic raids.

**3. Temporizing ground**

Neither side gains by moving first â€” deadlock. Doctrine: even if enemy offers bait, do not stir; retreat to lure enemy out, then attack detached force. Business: mutual hold-out games â€” wait for rival to break cover first (capacity expansion, price break, feature launch).

**4. Narrow passes**

Choke points. Doctrine: if first, fortify and wait; if enemy holds fortified pass, do not attack â€” lure away. Business: standards bodies, app store gates, regulated licenses, single procurement portal, API with rate limits.

**5. Precipitous heights**

High ground. Doctrine: occupy sunny heights first; if enemy holds, do not follow â€” entice away. Business: category definition, brand premium, data scale advantages, network effects at ceiling.

**6. Distant ground**

Strength equal but great distance â€” fighting disadvantages you. Doctrine: do not provoke battle; fighting will be to your disadvantage. Business: far geography, wrong timezone coverage, cultural mismatch, late entrant without local allies.

### Six calamities (leadership faults â€” Chapter X, ss. 14â€“20)

Not natural causes â€” faults for which the leader is responsible:

| Calamity | Cause | Business symptom |
|---|---|---|
| Flight | Hurl small force against tenfold | Startup challenges incumbent on all fronts simultaneously |
| Insubordination | Soldiers strong, officers weak | Talented ICs ignore weak manager; chaos |
| Collapse | Officers strong, soldiers weak | Aggressive VPs, exhausted junior staff; burnout collapse |
| Ruin | Angry officers fight on own account before HQ decides | Rogue regional P&L wars, silo deals that wreck strategy |
| Disorganization | Weak general, unclear orders, no fixed duties | Reorg without decision rights; everyone busy, nothing ships |
| Rout | Underestimate enemy, weak detachment vs strong | Under-resourced enterprise push; picked team loses |

The general who attains a responsible post must study these. Natural formation of country is soldier's best ally â€” but estimating adversary, controlling victory, calculating distances constitutes the test of great general.

### Halfway to victory (Chapter X, ss. 27â€“29)

Three incomplete knowledge states â€” each only halfway:

- Know you can attack, but not that enemy is not open to attack
- Know enemy open, but not that your men are not ready
- Know both, but not that ground makes fighting impracticable

Experienced soldier once in motion is never bewildered. Full victory requires knowing enemy, self, **and** ground (Heaven/Earth from Chapter I).

### Leadership on terrain (Chapter X, ss. 21â€“26)

Sun Tzu adds commander conduct on difficult ground:

- **Natural formation is soldier's best ally** â€” market structure helps more than heroics; do not fight physics of the category
- **Fight when victory certain even if ruler forbids; do not fight when defeat certain even if ruler commands** â€” professional judgment overrides bad exec orders when ground dictates
- **Advance without coveting fame; retreat without fearing disgrace** â€” protect org, not ego
- **Regard soldiers as children** â€” loyalty under hardship; balance with strictness (ss. 26) â€” indulgent leaders without authority create spoiled troops useless for practical purpose

Translate ss. 26: teams given autonomy without accountability collapse on serious ground.

### Nine situations â€” expanded playbooks

**Dispersive (home territory):** Teams protect local KPIs when headquarters declares war â€” do not launch major competitive push until local incentives align. Fix: unified scorecard before engagement.

**Facile (shallow penetration):** Easy retreat breeds hesitation. Either commit deeper or do not enter. Burning boats = public commitment customers can rely on (hard migration path, announced sunset of old SKU).

**Contentious (high-value choke):** Thermopylae logic â€” narrow advantage worth fighting for *if you get there first*. If rival holds, Sun Tzu recommends lure: feign retreat, attack elsewhere they cannot afford to lose, ambush detached force. Not frontal enterprise sales assault.

**Open:** Do not try to block enemy's freedom â€” futile. Compete on execution speed and segment choice instead of illegal blocking tactics.

**Intersecting highways:** Coalition mandatory â€” platform partnerships, integrators, industry associations. Solo player cannot hold hub ground.

**Serious (deep with fortresses behind):** Consolidate supply â€” cash, talent, reference customers in new geography. Forage = acquire local assets (acqui-hire, local brand) rather than shipping everything from HQ.

**Difficult (operational friction):** Keep moving â€” do not encamp in legacy integration hell. Simplify stack or exit.

**Hemmed-in:** Stratagem required â€” Hannibal's oxen fire trick equivalent: unconventional partnership, regulatory appeal, creative deal structure. Not brute force.

**Desperate:** Fight â€” but Sun Tzu means unified maximum effort when no alternative exists, not unethical desperation. Runway zero without pivot is desperate; fraud is not doctrine.

### Chapter XI â€” psychology of desperate ground

Sun Tzu: throw soldiers into positions whence there is no escape, they prefer death to flight â€” officers and men put forth uttermost strength. Business ethics translation:

- **Acceptable:** All-hands survival sprint with transparent stakes; executive pay cuts before layoffs; single-product bet with informed board consent
- **Unacceptable:** Deceiving employees about runway; trapping customers in contracts without exit; coercing partners illegally

Prohibit omens and superstitious doubts (ss. 26) â€” in business: kill analytics theater and rumor-driven panic; replace with clear metrics.

### Rapidity (Chapter XI, ss. 19â€“20)

"Rapidity is the essence of war." Examples in Giles commentary: Ssu-ma I reaching Hsin-ch'eng in eight days; Li Ching striking before flood season expected. Translation: first-mover on regulatory window, fast follow on competitor outage, speed to close before rival mobilizes sales force.

Further you penetrate with solidarity, greater troop cohesion â€” but only if serious ground was chosen deliberately, not stumbled into.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **Name the competitive theater.** Market, deal, internal initiative, or regulatory contest. Identify primary rival or opposing force.

2. **Classify six-terrain type.** Pick the dominant terrain (may be hybrid â€” note secondary). Quote evidence: channels, switching costs, chokepoints, distance.

3. **Apply terrain doctrine.** State Sun Tzu's rule for that terrain in one sentence, then translate to 2â€“3 concrete organizational actions.

4. **Classify nine-situation type.** Pick dominant situation based on commitment depth and exit options â€” not mood.

5. **Apply nine-situation doctrine.** Produce do / do-not table: fight, halt, ally, stratagem, etc.

6. **Check for layer conflict.** If terrain says "occupy heights" but situation says "dispersive â€” fight not," explain which layer dominates and why.

7. **Run six calamities audit.** Score each calamity Present / At risk / Absent for the user's side. Tie to named leadership or org evidence.

8. **Halfway-to-victory gap analysis.** Which of the three knowledge gaps is active? Recommend intelligence or readiness fix before motion.

9. **Produce integrated posture recommendation.** One paragraph: where you stand, what ground permits, what ground forbids, calamity blockers to fix first.

10. **Manipulation-risk check.** Flag coercive "desperate ground" tactics that harm employees, customers, or illegal (e.g., trapping people without consent). Burning boats is a **voluntary org commitment**, not trapping stakeholders.

11. **Deliver the output artifact** below.

### Worked example (abbreviated)

**Situation:** Regional retailer expanding into national e-commerce against Amazon-class incumbent.

- **Terrain:** Accessible + narrow passes (open market but platform logistics choke)
- **Terrain doctrine:** Guard supply lines (fulfillment, returns); do not fight on pass without alliance
- **Situation:** Serious ground (deep investment, legacy stores as rear fortresses)
- **Situation doctrine:** Gather plunder â€” monetize local brand assets; do not plunder customer trust (unjust treatment alienates â€” Sun Tzu via commentary)
- **Calamity at risk:** Rout â€” underestimating incumbent ad auction strength
- **Halfway gap:** Know enemy open to price war, unaware own fulfillment not ready
- **Recommendation:** Pause geographic expansion; intersecting-highways alliance with marketplace operator before continuing serious ground

## Common failure modes

- **Treating all hard markets as desperate ground.** Desperate means no escape without fight â€” most SaaS categories are serious or difficult, not desperate.

- **Dispersive ground denial.** Remote teams protecting local metrics during a global launch â€” Sun Tzu says fight not on dispersive ground; fix cohesion first.

- **Contentious ground frontal assault.** Enterprise account owned by incumbent â€” do not siege; lure or take alternate high ground.

- **Ignoring intersecting highways.** Platform wars require allies; solo assault on AWS/Google/Apple gate fails doctrine.

- **Calamity misdiagnosis.** Rout looks like bad product but is weak detachment vs strong incumbent sales force â€” wrong team, not wrong feature.

- **Confusing terrain with situation.** Narrow pass (terrain) vs hemmed-in (situation): pass is structural choke; hemmed-in is your retreat cut off.

- **Permanent desperate posture.** Living quarter-to-quarter is desperate; normalize it and you destroy planning â€” fix runway or exit.

- **Literal military mapping.** This skill is structural metaphor â€” not GIS, not troop movements, not violence.

- **Terrain skill without situation layer.** Six terrains describe field shape; nine situations describe your commitment â€” always classify both.

- **Desperate ground glorification.** Sun Tzu's "fight" on desperate ground is survival unity, not suicide missions â€” if pivot exists, you are not on desperate ground.

- **Ignoring intersecting highways in platform markets.** Hub ground without allies is automatic loss â€” doctrine requires coalition.

- **Calamity blame on soldiers.** All six calamities are leader-responsible â€” do not blame "bad hires" without leader process failure.

- **Hemmed-in without stratagem creativity.** If legal contracts trap you, doctrine is negotiation/stratagem, not denial.

- **Confusing with `stakeholder-map`.** Stakeholder map is power/interest grid for people; nine situations is strategic commitment topology.

- **Confusing with `dr-map-creation`.** Disaster maps are geographic operational pictures for DR â€” not Sun Tzu competitive metaphor.
