## The framework

### Core thesis

Chapter III reframes victory. Fighting and conquering in every battle is not supreme excellence. Supreme excellence consists in **breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting**. The hierarchy of generalship:

1. **Best:** Balk the enemy's plans â€” frustrate strategy before resources collide
2. **Next:** Prevent junction of enemy forces â€” isolate allies, split coalitions, deny partnerships
3. **Next:** Attack the enemy's army in the field â€” engage when they are at full strength (expensive)
4. **Worst:** Besiege walled cities â€” attrition warfare; months of preparation, one-third losses, town still untaken

Sun Tzu's business translation: the best companies win by making the competitor's strategy irrelevant â€” category definition, standard-setting, regulatory capture, exclusive distribution â€” not by out-spending them in a siege (endless discounting, feature parity arms races, talent bidding wars with no margin endgame).

The chapter ends with the famous intelligence matrix and five essentials for victory â€” operational complements to the stratagem hierarchy.

### Supreme excellence without fighting

Skillful leaders subdue opponents without fighting, capture positions without prolonged siege, overthrow kingdoms without lengthy field operations â€” forces intact, triumph complete.

Modern instances Sun Tzu would approve:
- M&A that removes the competitor as a separate actor
- Standard or platform adoption that makes rival products technically compatible but strategically marginal
- Regulatory or certification moat that raises cost of entry above rival's willingness
- Partnership that cuts competitor off from a critical channel
- Talent or IP acquisition that hollows rival capability without public war

Modern sieges Sun Tzu would condemn:
- Multi-year price wars with no path to margin recovery
- Feature-checklist parity chasing a better-funded incumbent
- Litigation as default competitive tool without settlement math
- "Land grab" burn rates with no unit economics story

### The stratagem hierarchy (operationalized)

When advising on a competitive move, rank options top-down:

| Level | Military term | Business translation | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Balk plans | Pre-empt strategy â€” launch first, define category, exclusive deal, patent wall | Rival pivots before major spend |
| 2 | Prevent junction | Isolate â€” poach key partner, split coalition, deny integration | Rival's alliance fails to form |
| 3 | Attack in field | Direct competition â€” market share battle, head-to-head sales | Rival loses share with margin intact for you |
| 4 | Besiege | Attrition â€” price war, endless POC, legal grind | Avoid unless exit of rival is certain and cost bounded |

If the user is already at level 4, ask what level 1â€“2 moves were skipped.

### Force ratios and posture

Sun Tzu's rules (translated to relative resource strength):

- **Ten to one:** surround â€” dominate the niche; make rival irrelevant in segment
- **Five to one:** attack â€” you may strike straightway
- **Two to one:** divide â€” split forces; frontal plus flanking (e.g., enterprise plus SMB)
- **Equal:** offer battle only if generalship (execution) is superior
- **Slightly inferior:** avoid â€” do not engage on their terms
- **Greatly inferior:** flee â€” exit, pivot, or seek alliance

Critical nuance from commentators: numbers are never the only variable â€” energy, discipline, and ground modify the rule. A "two to one" resource edge wasted on siege still loses.

### The know-yourself / know-enemy matrix

Sun Tzu's four postures:

| | Know enemy | Don't know enemy |
|---|---|---|
| **Know self** | Secure â€” need not fear result of a hundred battles | One victory, one defeat for each victory â€” inconsistent outcomes |
| **Don't know self** | (partial) | Succumb in every battle |

Extended for business:

- **Know self:** honest capability map â€” product, cost structure, sales cycle, culture, runway, leadership limits
- **Know enemy:** their strategy, constraints, coalition, cash, decision-maker incentives, likely reactions
- **Know neither:** default state of most startups post-pivot â€” dangerous

The matrix drives a blunt recommendation: gather intelligence, fix self-deception, or disengage.

### Five essentials for victory

1. Know when to fight and when not to fight
2. Know how to handle both superior and inferior forces (art, not just arithmetic)
3. Army animated by same spirit throughout ranks â€” aligned incentives top to bottom
4. Prepared self waits for unprepared enemy
5. Military capacity without sovereign interference â€” team has decision authority

### Three ways a ruler destroys the army

Translate to executive/board failure modes:

1. **Hobbling:** ordering advance/retreat when the organization cannot obey â€” unrealistic timelines, contradictory goals
2. **Civil administration of competitive units:** HR policies, approval chains, or "nice culture" that prevents competitive action
3. **Wrong officers without adaptation:** misfit leaders, no permission to adjust to circumstances â€” confidence collapses

When the army is restless and distrustful, trouble comes from other feudal princes â€” competitors exploit internal dysfunction. This is bringing anarchy into the army and flinging victory away.

### Balking plans â€” business patterns

What "balk the enemy's plans" looks like in practice:

- **Pre-emptive category creation** â€” define the buying criteria before rival's product ships
- **Exclusive distribution or OEM** â€” remove their junction option (stratagem level 2)
- **Regulatory comment period** â€” shape rules so their model is non-compliant
- **Talent acquisition of rival's unique capability** â€” not poaching randomly, but hollowing a planned launch
- **Open-source or standards release** â€” commoditize the wedge they planned to monetize

Each pattern should be evaluated against know-self: can you execute the balk, or will you trigger siege anyway?

### Siege warning signs

Sun Tzu's siege math: three months mantlets, three months mounds, one-third casualties, town untaken. Business siege equivalents:

- Discounting with no floor for 4+ quarters
- Enterprise POC cycles exceeding average sales cycle without conversion rate
- Legal discovery consuming executive attention with no settlement band
- Feature parity roadmap with no differentiation thesis
- Hiring war in single skill pool (ML, security) with no retention plan

If two or more signs present, force stratagem level review â€” user is likely at level 4.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **Define the contested outcome.** What would "victory" mean without unnecessary destruction? (Profitable share, deal signed, standard adopted, rival exited category â€” be specific.)

2. **Score know-self.** List capabilities, constraints, and blind spots. Require evidence: metrics, lost deals, churn reasons, internal dissent. Rate confidence High / Medium / Low.

3. **Score know-enemy.** Same structure for the primary rival or counterparty. Flag explicit unknowns â€” do not fabricate competitor intelligence.

4. **Place on the four-cell matrix.** Name the posture and plain-language consequence (secure, inconsistent, doomed).

5. **Recommend stratagem level.** Start at level 1; only descend if upper levels are infeasible. Document why lower levels are blocked.

6. **Apply force-ratio posture.** Estimate relative strength (resources, share, attention, legal position). Recommend surround / attack / divide / defend / evade / exit. Note if execution quality modifies the arithmetic.

7. **Run five essentials checklist.** Pass/fail each with one sentence of evidence. Any fail on essentials 1, 4, or 5 is a stop sign for escalation.

8. **Scan ruler-interference risks.** Is leadership/board forcing siege when team recommends balk? Flag three hobbling patterns if present.

9. **Produce the engagement recommendation:** Engage (at which stratagem level), delay (gather intelligence), or disengage (evade/exit) â€” with conditions that would change the call.

10. **Manipulation-risk check.** Flag if user seeks "Sun Tzu justification" for predatory, illegal, or harmful action. Winning without fighting in business excludes fraud, violence, and sanctions evasion.

11. **Deliver the output artifact** using the template below.

## Common failure modes

- **Confusing "without fighting" with "without effort."** Balking plans requires superior intelligence, speed, and positioning â€” not passivity.

- **Siege addiction.** Incumbents often default to attrition because they can afford it; startups must not mirror that mistake against better-funded rivals.

- **Know-self theater.** OKRs that nobody believes are not self-knowledge. Include what the sales team actually tells customers vs. what the deck says.

- **Enemy as caricature.** "They are slow/stupid" is not know-enemy. Model their constraints â€” why they might rationally outspend you.

- **Ignoring junction prevention.** Modern wars are coalition wars â€” ecosystems, integrators, regulators. Level 2 stratagem is often underused.

- **Force-ratio machismo.** Equal resources with worse ground or discipline still means avoid â€” route to terrain skill.

- **Sovereign interference denial.** Founders blame team when the board won't let them cut a product line or fire a blocker â€” essential #5 failure.

- **Applying military ratios to single deals.** One enterprise negotiation is not "ten to one surround" â€” adjust metaphor to BATNA strength and alternatives count.

- **Intelligence theater.** Scraping a competitor's website is not know-enemy. Model decision-makers, incentives, constraints, and likely reactions to your move.

- **Self-knowledge without numbers.** Know-self requires honest runway, churn, sales conversion, and delivery capacity â€” not vision statements.

- **Skipping stratagem level 1 because it feels slow.** Regulatory, standards, and partnership pre-emption take quarters; still faster than siege.

- **Essential #3 confusion.** "Same spirit" is aligned incentives â€” not forced cheer. Sales compensated on revenue while product on quality guarantees insubordination (see terrain calamities).

- **Using know-enemy for violent scenarios.** Do not apply to physical conflict, insurgency, or harm â€” skill refuses and redirects to business frame.
