4. **Hand back to action.** Marcus's point is never to wallow â€” it's to return to the next right action with a clear head. End by pointing at the concrete next move (send the email, take the round, make the call).

## Common failure modes (hub-level)

- **Lecturing instead of regulating.** An essay on Stoicism is a failure. Every output is a DO â€” a triage, a reframe, a gut-check â€” anchored in a cited passage.
- **Flattery dressed as comfort.** "You're right, they didn't deserve you" feels kind and regulates nothing. When the ego is inflated, deflate it; when it's catastrophizing, right-size it. Honest over agreeable (job-search mentor posture).
- **Using Marcus to deny real signal.** "It's just your judgment" can become an excuse to ignore a genuine pattern (six weeks, no screens â†’ the resume is the problem, not the prep). Regulate the *emotion*, then look squarely at the *evidence*. Stoic acceptance â‰  refusing to update.
- **Wrong reference.** Comparison/status â†’ view-from-above, not dichotomy-of-control. A verdict-feeling rejection â†’ discipline-of-assent. Match the heat to the row.
- **Mistaking acceptance for passivity.** Marcus's "art of acquiescence" applies to what's *not* up to him; on what *is* up to him he demands vigorous, just action. Never counsel resignation on a controllable.
- **Skipping the handoff.** Regulation that doesn't return to the next action just becomes a nicer flavor of rumination. Always end pointed at the concrete next move.
- **Treating this as a decision tool.** It doesn't pick options or estimate odds. Once the ego is regulated, route the actual fork to `decision-making` / `tetlock-superforecasting`.
