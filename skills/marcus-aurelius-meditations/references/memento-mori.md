## When to use

Trigger when Trevor has **over-identified with a single outcome** and it's become load-bearing for his whole sense of the plan or himself:

- "If I don't land *this* one, the whole pivot is dead."
- One role / one company / one offer has become *the* thing, and its risk is generating outsized dread.
- He's pouring weeks of emotional weight into a result that is, at altitude, one move among many.
- A loss is being mourned as if a chapter of his life ended, not as one bet that didn't hit.
- Burnout/tempo-loss over a long search â€” the grind has flattened into "this is forever and it's all riding on the next yes."

This is Marcus's most pervasive theme (Hays, Intro): **transience**. All things change, pass, are forgotten â€” which sounds bleak but functions as *relief*: it strips the false permanence off the one outcome and shrinks it back to its real weight. Use it to reset priorities, not to induce gloom.

## The exercise (run this with Trevor)

1. **Name the one outcome he's fused to.** ("This Anthropic FDE role is the whole pivot.")
2. **Run it down the river.** Both branches: "If it's a yes â€” great, and in six months it's just where you work. If it's a no â€” it stings, and in six months it's one rejection in a funnel of thirty. Neither branch is permanent; neither *is* you."
3. **Collapse to the present rep.** "You don't own the verdict. You own *this* application, *this* prep block, done well. Give yourself the present moment (8.44) â€” the next clean rep â€” and let the river take the rest."
4. **Three-line gut-check back to Trevor:**
   - The fused outcome, named.
   - The impermanence read: this is one move, not the plan; the plan is the *funnel*, and the funnel survives any single no.
   - The present action to return to right now.

## Where this earns its candor

- **De-loading a single outcome:** directly serves the job-search rule that no single rejection should rewrite the strategy ("decisions are bets, not identities"). Memento mori is the emotional version of that discipline â€” and pairs with `decision-making` (resulting) for the analytic version.
- **Priority reset under burnout:** "Observing life for forty years is as good as a thousand â€” you'd see nothing new" (*7.49*). The grind feels infinite; from altitude it's a finite, survivable stretch. Re-anchor on *why* the pivot exists (the wealth lever, the life it funds), not on the next single yes.
- **Honest counterweight:** impermanence cuts the inflation off a *win* too. A strong offer is real and worth pursuing hard â€” and also not a coronation. Right-size both directions.

## Common failure modes

- **Tipping into "nothing matters, why try."** Wrong landing. Marcus's transience *frees action*: because the outcome is impermanent and not yours, throw yourself fully at the present rep. End on the rep, never on the void.
- **Using mortality-talk to excuse avoidance.** "It won't matter anyway" can become a dodge of a round he's scared of. If fear is the real driver, route to `premeditatio-malorum.md`.
- **Flattening real stakes to zero.** The pivot *does* matter (it funds the plan). Memento mori right-sizes the *single outcome*, not the *mission*. Keep the mission; de-load the one bet.
- **Gloom for gloom's sake.** Hays warns the book runs melancholy. Deliver the relief, not the dirge â€” and hand back to the next action.
