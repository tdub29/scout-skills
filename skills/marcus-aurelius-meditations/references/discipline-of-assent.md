## When to use

Trigger when a **specific event has landed as a verdict** and the pain is in the *interpretation*, not the fact:

- A rejection email reads as "you're not good enough" rather than "this role, this week, was a no."
- A lowball offer feels like an insult to his worth instead of an opening number.
- A recruiter's silence becomes "I embarrassed myself" instead of "their process is slow."
- A rude or dismissive interviewer is occupying his head hours later.
- A peer's win lands as his own loss.
- He's saying "this is humiliating / this is a disaster / they disrespected me" â€” those are *judgments*, not events.

This is Marcus's **discipline of perception** (Hays, Intro): the raw impression (*phantasia*) is just a report from the senses; the suffering comes from the *hypolepsis*, the interpretation the mind stamps on top of it â€” "and I have thereby suffered a terrible tragedy." That second layer is optional, and it's where the work happens.

## The two-column strip (run this with Trevor)

Split what happened into the **fact** and the **story**:

| THE FACT (first impression â€” true) | THE STORY (extrapolation â€” optional, usually false) |
|---|---|
| They sent a rejection for this role | "I'm not good enough for this whole track" |
| The offer was $X, below my number | "They think I'm worth less than I am / I should be grateful" |
| The recruiter hasn't replied in 9 days | "I blew the round / I'm being ghosted because I'm a fraud" |
| The interviewer was curt | "He could see right through me" |

Then deliver three lines:
1. **The fact, stated flat.** No adjectives. ("You got a no on one role.")
2. **The story you bolted on.** Name it as a judgment, not a truth. ("The story is that this is a verdict on you. It isn't â€” it's one data point.")
3. **The strip.** "Drop the story and look at just the fact. What does the *fact alone* require you to do?" Usually: log it, extract the one real signal, run the next app.

## Where this earns its candor

The discipline cuts both ways, and the mentor's job is honest-over-agreeable:
- **Deflation:** strip the catastrophe off a rejection. One no is not a verdict.
- **The genuine-signal exception:** if the *fact* is a pattern (six weeks, zero screens), the discipline isn't "ignore it" â€” it's "feel the plain fact without the shame spiral, *then* fix the resume." Don't let assent-discipline become denial. Regulate the sting; keep the signal.
- **Best revenge:** if someone treated him badly, "the best revenge is not to be like that" (*6.6*) â€” i.e., don't let their conduct author his. He controls his response, not their behavior (hand to `dichotomy-of-control.md`).

## Common failure modes

- **Comforting the story instead of stripping it.** "They didn't deserve you" validates the extrapolation. Strip it; don't feed it.
- **Stripping a real signal.** If the fact itself is a warning, regulate the emotion and *then* act on the evidence. Acceptance â‰  refusing to update.
- **Over-extrapolating from one event** (the exact thing 8.49 forbids) â€” "one rejection" becoming "the whole track is closed."
- **Mistaking suppression for the discipline.** The point isn't "don't feel it" â€” it's "see the impression for what it is, then decline the false interpretation."
