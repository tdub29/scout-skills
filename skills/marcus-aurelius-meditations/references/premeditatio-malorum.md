## When to use

- The morning of (or night before) a hard interview round, panel, or negotiation call.
- Anxiety that "they'll rattle me / I'll freeze / someone will be dismissive and I'll lose my footing."
- He's bracing for a difficult counterpart (a cold technical interviewer, a hardball recruiter) and the anticipation is the problem.

This is the **emotional** pre-mortem: Marcus's practice of pre-living the difficulty so it can't ambush the ego in the moment. It is distinct from the project pre-mortem.

## The exercise (run this with Trevor, ~5 minutes pre-event)

1. **Name the three things he's dreading.** Be specific: "a hostile system-design grilling," "a comp question I'll fumble," "an interviewer who clearly thinks I'm under-leveled."
2. **Pre-live each â€” say it plainly, in advance.** "Some of them will be curt, skeptical, or dismissive. That's expected (2.1). It says nothing about my worth and changes nothing about my prep."
3. **Pre-decide the response of his best self.** For each dreaded item, one line: *if X happens, I do Y.* ("If I blank on a question, I say 'let me think out loud' and reason from first principles." "If they lowball, I stay warm and ask what's driving the band.") This is the emotional analogue of an if-then plan.
4. **Reframe the obstacle as fuel (5.20 / 4.1).** "The hard question isn't the threat â€” it's the chance to show how you think. The obstacle *is* the way."
5. **Three-line gut-check to walk in with:**
   - "The friction is expected, not a verdict." (2.1)
   - "My job is my conduct and my reasoning â€” not their reaction." (hand to `dichotomy-of-control.md`)
   - "Whatever they throw is raw material. Use it." (5.20)

## Where this earns its candor

- **It immunizes, it doesn't catastrophize.** The line between premeditatio and anxiety-spiral is the *reframe*: every dreaded item is paired, in advance, with "and here's how I meet it." Bare worst-casing with no reframe is just rumination â€” don't leave Trevor there.
- **Pairs with the analytic prep, doesn't replace it.** This regulates the *nerves*; the actual technical readiness still comes from the track-mapped prep skills. Compose the person; let the prep skills arm him.

## Common failure modes

- **Sliding into a spiral.** Pre-living the bad WITHOUT the pre-decided response is anxiety, not premeditatio. Always pair each dread with its if-then and its reframe.
- **Confusing it with the project pre-mortem.** Emotional composure â‰  plan risk. If the real worry is the strategy, route to `premortem`. Keep the lanes clean.
- **Over-rehearsing into rigidity.** The goal is composure and a few if-thens, not a memorized script. Marcus pre-loads an *attitude*, not a screenplay.
- **Pre-living only the worst, never the win.** Also pre-decide how he stays grounded if it goes *great* â€” easy praise can inflate the ego mid-round (route to `view-from-above.md`).
- **Skipping the handoff to action.** End by sending him in â€” pointed at the round, composed, not still rehearsing dread.
