
# McGilvray DQ Project Charter

Use this when a data quality issue needs to become a scoped project or focused workstream. The output is a data quality project charter.

## Artifact

Produce a charter with:

1. **Business problem** - decision, process, customer, compliance, cost, or project outcome harmed by poor-quality data.
2. **Data scope** - data categories, systems, entities, fields, time period, and exclusions.
3. **Approach type** - establish business case, establish baseline, determine root causes, implement improvements, monitor controls, individual effort, or embedded project activity.
4. **Stakeholders and roles** - sponsor, data owners, SMEs, technical owners, consumers, and decision makers.
5. **Measures of success** - business impact, quality dimensions, baseline targets, remediation outcomes, and control metrics.
6. **Plan and constraints** - timeline, dependencies, access needs, risks, assumptions, and communication cadence.

## Process

1. Start with business impact, not data defects. Ask what decisions, costs, delays, risks, or customer outcomes make this worth addressing.
2. Choose the smallest project shape that fits. McGilvray's Ten Steps are pick-and-choose; do not force a full program for a focused issue.
3. Define data scope tightly. Broad "customer data quality" efforts need boundaries by system, entity, field, process, and time period.
4. Name who can act on findings. A charter without owners for root causes and remediation creates assessment theater.
5. Decide what evidence will trigger action. Specify thresholds or findings that justify remediation, monitoring, or escalation.

## Failure Modes

- **Defect-first framing** - counting bad records before explaining why the business should care.
- **Unbounded scope** - trying to fix all data quality instead of selecting high-value data and known pain points.
- **Assessment without action** - profiling data when no sponsor or owner can approve remediation.
- **Wrong-sized methodology** - running every Ten Steps activity when the situation only needs impact analysis, root cause, or monitoring.
- **No communication plan** - stakeholders are surprised by findings, effort, or required process changes.

## Output Format

Return the charter, missing inputs, recommended Ten Steps subset, and the first two weeks of work.
