## Sub-process A â€” Naturalize: find one starter contact (only for top targets lacking alumni)

"Naturalize" = turn an **N** into a **Y** in the Alumni column by finding a sympathetic contact through means other than your most recent alumni DB. **Do this only for top-5 employers that have N in Alumni** (you already have starter contacts at the Y ones). Don't do it during Step 1 â€” you'd waste effort on employers you don't care about.

Work the hierarchy **top-down, stop at the first hit** (descending average-rate-of-return per minute):

1. **Most recent alumni database** â€” already done in Step 1.
2. **Undergrad alumni database** (if different) â€” log in, â‰¤3 min, move on if no instant access.
3. **LinkedIn** â€” Advanced People search, Company = target, Current employees only. Pick by: closeness (1st > 2nd > Group degree) â†’ relevance of role â†’ currentness of mutual tie â†’ seniority (a few years senior is ideal â€” networked but not too busy). 25â€“50+ hits? Add your School field to surface "hidden alumni." Skip 3rd-degree/no-connection (paywalled, low ROI).
4. **Facebook** â€” one status update ("Can anyone connect me to someone at <Co>, in <function> if possible?"). Exploits the *strength of weak ties* (divergent networks). Exhaustible â€” use sparingly, only after obvious paths fail.
5. **Fan Mail** â€” Google `"<Employer>" <keyword> "interview with"` to find off-the-beaten-path blog/webzine interviews with an **upper-middle manager** (not the CEO). Highest response quality, but ~5-min timebox â€” addictive and high-variance. Scour the article for an email; if none, try Emails4Corporations conventions, then the site's "Contact Us."
6. **LinkedIn backsolving** â€” find a relevant *job title* on LinkedIn, then Fan-Mail/Google that person for an interview to use as an icebreaker. â‰¤5 min (4â€“5 people), then pick up the phone.
7. **Cold calls** â€” call the main line, ask for the most relevant person you found. Fastest (<1 min), surprisingly effective at small companies. Last because it's informed by the steps above.

Store each found contact (name, role, any article URL) **as an email draft**, not on the LAMP sheet â€” it adds urgency to send. Most searches never go past Fan Mail.

## Sub-process B â€” The 5-Point Email (the outreach artifact)

Rebuild the outreach email from scratch around **social norms, not market norms** (Ariely's couch experiment: no pay matched fair pay; a *small* pay was worst â€” money turns a favor into a transaction). Cut everything about "the value I bring" / your background / why you're a great fit. Advocacy beats qualifications. The email is an honest, concise *favor request* that's about *them*.

The five points:
1. **Fewer than 100 words.** Fits one phone screen, no scroll. Short-and-generic beats long-and-generic. Minimizes typos/tone risk.
2. **No mention of jobs** (subject or body). They already know you want a job (nobody does informational interviews for fun). Asking about a job up front is intimidating â€” Boosters fear they're signing up to vouch for an unknown. *Exception:* if you've already found a specific posting, name it (removes the Booster's "is anything even open?" anxiety). Do **not** apply online yet unless there's an imminent deadline â€” a Booster may make the application unnecessary or coach your messaging.
3. **Connection goes first** (the "hook," in the first sentence). Alum/Group â†’ name the shared school/group. 2nd-degree/Facebook â†’ ask the mutual contact for an intro (warm them up; don't skip them via LinkedIn InMail). Fan Mail/backsolving â†’ reference the article (email nearly writes itself; can drop points 4â€“5). Cold call â†’ skip the connection line, go to the ask.
4. **Generalize your interest** to a logical family of employers ("marketing roles at tech companies in NC"), while still centering their firm. Inoculates against the "we're under a hiring freeze, good luck" brush-off and surfaces **Super-Boosters** â€” Boosters at non-hiring firms who open their *whole network* to you. **Never decline an informational interview over a hiring freeze.**
5. **Maintain control of follow-up** ("if we can't connect by email I'll try you next week"). You own the inconvenience; signals deference, persistence (most seekers quit after one email), and urgency; and it's what makes the 3B7 tracking work.

Canonical template (~91 words body):
```
SUBJECT: <School> <program> seeking your advice
Dear Mr./Ms. <Last>,
My name is <Name>, a <program> student/grad who found your information in the
<School> alumni database. May I have 20 minutes to ask you about your experience
with <Company>? I am trying to learn more about <generalized interest>, and your
insights would be very helpful.
I recognize this may be a busy time for you, so if we are unable to connect by
email I'll try to reach you next week to see whether that is more convenient.
Thank you for your time,
<Name>
```
Don't get creative or "customize" with long personalization â€” it becomes obvious copy-paste spam and risks alienating Boosters who don't need to be delighted, only informed that you need help.

## Sub-process C â€” Booster / Obligate / Curmudgeon triage via the 3B7 Routine

You can't tell segments up front, so use **response time as the proxy**. The segments:
- **Booster** â€” loves their job/school, enjoys helping, has advocacy to give. ~80/20: most of your help comes from this minority. Responds fast (â‰¤3 business days). The *only* target worth optimizing for.
- **Obligate** â€” motivated by guilt; the **worst** segment (high time-cost, low return). Foot-drags, sends mixed signals, "send me your resume and I'll forward it" (forwarding â‰  advocacy). You'd *prefer* they not reply.
- **Curmudgeon** â€” never replies, ever. Cheap to detect, costs little. Not as bad as Obligates because they're honest.

Some 5-Point-Email features (brevity, no-job, generalized interest, owned follow-up) are *designed to alienate* Curmudgeons/Obligates. That's intentional â€” every Obligate you skip frees time for a Booster. Typical 5-Point-Email response rate â‰ˆ **40%**.

**3B7 = set 3- and 7-business-day reminders when you email any new contact** (B = business days; the LAMP sheet is strategy-only, the *calendar* is the tracker â€” spreadsheets are bad alarm clocks):
- **3-day reminder = "is this a Booster?" check.** Reply by then â†’ assume Booster; schedule the informational interview (offer 2â€“3 time windows to reduce their decision anxiety) and **cancel the reminders**. No reply by then â†’ assume Obligate/Curmudgeon and **start a serial-parallel hedge**: email contact #2 at the same employer (don't mention contact #1 â€” no tattling) and set fresh 3/7 reminders for #2.
- **7-day reminder = follow-up.** Now an easy "per my previous emailâ€¦" because you pre-promised it.

The four scenarios:
- **#1 â€” #1 replies in â‰¤3 days:** Booster. Schedule, cancel reminders.
- **#2 â€” #1 silent, #2 replies in â‰¤3 days:** engage #2; still send #1 a soft email follow-up at the 7-day mark (you promised â€” keeping your word is your main pre-interview proof of reliability), worded weakly so #1 stays quiet.
- **#3 â€” neither replies:** at #1's 7-day mark, **switch to phone** (interruptive, faster, fewer language pitfalls â€” "switch from email to phone whenever things stop going to plan"). Add a **10-business-day "backstop" reminder**; if still silent, #1 is a Curmudgeon. Phone #2 at its 10-day mark; then **reboot** with contact #3 (fresh 3/7).
- **#4 â€” odd intervals:** replies between days 3â€“7 â†’ still honor an informational interview if offered. Replies weeks/months later â†’ if you've moved on, thank them, update on progress, move on (no guilt â€” nobody hires a plumber who calls back weeks late).

Process discipline: **serial per employer** (one contact at a time, hedge to #2 only after 3 days) but **parallel across employers** (run 3B7 on ~5 employers at once to start, scale to 10+ later). Never blast 5â€“10 people at *one* employer in parallel â€” that's the "spammed the whole banking floor" horror story. **Responding to an incoming Booster reply within 24 hours always takes priority over sending new outreach** â€” a slow reply to a Booster offering you times is one of the two fastest ways to lose them.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. For each top-5 employer with N in Alumni, **Naturalize**: walk the 7-level hierarchy top-down, stop at first hit, save contact to a Drafts email.
2. For employers with Y, **pick the best starter contact**: role closest to your target, then a few years senior (senior contacts can hand you off; junior can't).
3. **Draft a 5-Point Email** per the five points; pick the connection-line variant matching how you found them.
4. **Send to one contact per employer, ~5 employers in parallel**; immediately set 3- and 7-business-day calendar reminders per contact.
5. **Run 3B7** on each reminder; classify by Scenario #1â€“4; hedge serially to contact #2/#3 as the rules dictate.
6. **Triage-label** each contact Booster/Obligate/Curmudgeon as evidence arrives; pour follow-up energy into Boosters only.
7. **Sanity-check:** reply to any inbound Booster within 24h; confirm every sent email has live reminders and every promised follow-up actually fired.

## Common failure modes

- **Long, "customized," market-norm email.** 200â€“500 words about your value reads like offering $1.05 to move a couch. Use the <100-word favor request.
- **Mentioning jobs in the first email.** Intimidates Boosters and invites the "no openings, good luck" brush-off. Ask for advice/time, not a job.
- **Declining over a hiring freeze.** Kills your shot at a Super-Booster who'd open their whole network. Generalize interest and keep the conversation.
- **Tracking in the spreadsheet / by memory.** Cortisol wrecks recall; the LAMP sheet has no alarm. Use calendar reminders.
- **Waiting forever on non-responders.** "One more day" loops cause decision-anxiety spirals. 3B7 caps any Curmudgeon at ~2 weeks and forces the hedge.
- **Parallel-spamming one employer.** Many simultaneous requests at one firm gets you mocked/blacklisted. Serial within a firm, parallel across firms.
- **Skipping the promised follow-up to #1 because #2 replied.** You gave your word â€” keeping it is your reliability proof. It's not for you to like, it's for you to do.
- **Over-Naturalizing weak targets.** Burning Fan-Mail/cold-call effort on non-top-5 employers. Use the costly methods only for top targets without easier options.
