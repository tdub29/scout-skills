## Phase 0 â€” Research (15 min external + internal prep; more than 15 backfires)

**External (â‰¤15 min):** (1) a DataMonitor360 SWOT if available (or Vault/WetFeet guides), (2) the *positive* headlines on the employer's homepage (so you're never blindsided by "did you see our big headline this week?"), (3) Google the employer for *negative* headlines â€” only to **steer clear** of them (raising them forces the contact to badmouth their firm and that awkwardness gets blamed on you), plus the interviewer's name for LinkedIn/bio/interview history. ## Phase 1 â€” Small talk (~5 min; "follow the energy")

Take genuine interest; let them talk about what *they* want. Systematic openers: "How's your day going?" â†’ "What projects are you working on?" â†’ "Tell me about your background / how you came to work here" (this pre-empts and triggers the reflected "Tell me about yourself"). ## Phase 2 â€” TIARA Q&A (the question algorithm; expert â†’ mentor)

Thesis: **open by treating the contact as an *expert*; progressively reframe them as a *mentor*** (a mentor takes long-term interest; an expert feels "done" after dispensing wisdom). Questions are open-ended, research-free, durable (reusable across contacts, getting more sophisticated over time), and engineered to *never stump* the contact. "Follow the energy" within and between letters.

- **T â€” Trends** (opener; pure business, ego-flattering). "What trends are most impacting your business right now?" / "How has it changed since you started?" / "How will it change in the next few years?" Junior and senior contacts both answer well, at different scope. Upgrade later by injecting what a prior contact said ("rising oil prices are reshaping CPG distribution â€” are you seeing that?").
- **I â€” Insights** (slightly personal; opens disclosure). "What surprises you most about your job?" / "Best lesson learned here?" / "Attribute your success to one trait?" Builds rapport + yields sourceable nuggets for cover letters and later interviews; for career-changers, the answers converge into an accurate picture of fit.
- **A â€” Advice** (adds *empathy*; the expertâ†’mentor pivot). "What can I do right now to prepare for this field?" / "First 30 days to start fast?" / "What do you know now you wish you'd known in my position?" / "If you were me, what would you do?" This makes them invest emotionally and treat your outcome as a reflection of their mentoring. **Take handwritten notes** (typing sounds like you're not listening; longhand forces an immediate post-call summary). These notes are the payload of your first follow-up email.
- **R â€” Resources (contains "the pivot" â€” the single most important question).** Always ask the pivot *first*, before other R questions, before time runs out: **"What resources should I be sure to look into next?"** or **"What next steps would you recommend for someone in my situation?"** It's deliberately vague so a Booster can *gracefully volunteer a person* (referral) without you violating the "this isn't about a job" promise. The answer forks the conversation (jackpot vs. non-jackpot, below). If they ask "what kind of resources?", **deflect to non-human resources** ("What blogs/sites do you read to stay current?" / "Most valuable 10 min of research you do daily?" / "How would I learn the industry's vocabulary?") â€” never directly ask "who should I talk to next?" - **A â€” Assignments** (cuttable; pure usable-info + credibility jolt). "What projects have added the most value?" / "What's grown in importance lately?" / "What have interns/contractors done?" Arms you for "we're not sure how we'd use you â€” what could you do for us?" (common at the 2â€“99-employee firms = two-thirds of US jobs). Answer with the **power of the first draft**: propose 2â€“3 specific value-creating projects (drawn from other informationals) rather than "I'll do anything" (which reads as desperate).

## Phase 3 â€” Next steps + Follow-up

**Jackpot (referral offered â€” expect this to be the exception):** (1) thank the Booster; (2) obligate yourself to update them in ~2 weeks; (3) reach out to the new contact immediately (reply-all if introduced by email, offering specific call times) and send the Booster a separate thank-you; (4) set a 2-week reminder regardless of outcome. ## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **15-min research brief**: SWOT (if any) + positive homepage headlines + negative-headline scan (to avoid) + interviewer Google.
2. **Prep the Big Three** (chronological story; assertion-proof-tieback).
3. **Small talk ~5 min**, follow the energy, mirror length, bridge to Q&A.
4. **Run TIARA** Tâ†’Iâ†’Aâ†’Râ†’A; take handwritten notes; ask **the pivot first** in R.

## Common failure modes

- **Making it about you.** Pitching your qualifications instead of drawing out the contact. TIARA structurally prevents this â€” they should talk most of the time.
- **Asking for a job (or "who should I talk to next?") directly.** Violates the info-only promise, intimidates, and dead-ends the contact. Use the pivot's graceful vagueness; defer to a second chance.
- **Over-researching / stumping the contact.** Know-it-all questions create embarrassment they'll associate with you â†’ no referrals. 15 min is enough; they're the expert.
- **Raising negative headlines.** Forces them to badmouth their firm; awkwardness blamed on you. Research them only to avoid them.
- **Not following up after a Booster helps.** Leaving them in the dark after they spent reputation on you is one of the two fastest ways to lose a Booster (the other: slow reply to offered times). Always obligate the 2-week update.
- **Skipping the Two-Part Close / asking for the referral on the spot.** You forfeit the higher-probability second ask and risk losing a Booster who just needed time to decide.
- **Typing notes / sending unsolicited resumes / declining a non-hiring firm's call.** Typing reads as not-listening; unsolicited resumes look desperate; a non-hiring Super-Booster can open *multiple* firms' networks â€” always take that call.
