## Why this exists

Online applications are a "resume black hole" â€” employers satisfice and hire off internal referrals, so being 1st (or 300th) in an unread resume pile is worthless. LAMP turns an infinite universe of employers into a finite, ranked list using the 80-20 rule: collect three cheap proxy signals (Alumni, Motivation, Posting) instead of researching every company deeply. The whole pass is ~70 minutes and no single step exceeds 15. The ranking, not the list, is the deliverable â€” it tells you exactly who to contact first, second, third.

**The cardinal rule for every column: gather "good enough" info fast, never "perfect" info slowly. Do NOT blend tasks** (don't research while listing, don't rank while researching, don't click postings while scoring). Each task-switch costs goal-shift + rule-activation overhead and invites "cognitive drift." If tempted to dig into an interesting employer/posting, resist â€” proper LAMP returns you to it within two hours.

## The four columns

### L â€” List (~40 minutes, target 40+ employers)

Goal: **"ten employers or ten minutes," whichever comes first**, from each of four generators. Copy employer *names only* into the L column â€” no research, no contact info. Use the **One-Click Rule**: if the info is one click away, take it; if not, add the employer and move on.

1. **Dream employers.** Companies you already aspire to, then *their competitors* (Wikipedia "competitors"/category pages, Yahoo Finance, or Hoovers/OneSource if you have library access). Best for industry-focused searches â€” research on one company transfers to peers.
2. **Alumni employers.** Your most-recent school's alumni database, filtered by city/industry; copy any employer where an alum has an interesting title. Best for geography-focused or undecided searches; also ideal if you're shy (guarantees a warm contact).
3. **Posting search.** Indeed.com (most comprehensive engine) by "what" (degree/title keywords) + "where" (city/state). Limits the list to employers *hiring now*. Do NOT click postings â€” copy employer names only.
4. **Trend-following.** Google `<industry or function> trends`; skim macro articles and harvest *organizations mentioned doing new/interesting things* â€” surfaces smaller, lower-competition employers actively wanting to be found.

Then sort L alphabetically to dedupe. **40 is the floor, not a cap** â€” more is better, it's nearly free to add now and expensive to add later. Include unfamiliar/smaller employers deliberately: 99.9% of US employers have <500 employees, demand for a job scales with brand awareness, so lesser-known firms = less competition + better leverage.

### A â€” Alumni (~10 minutes)

For each employer, search your most-recent alumni database by *employer name only* and enter **Y** or **N** â€” nothing else. "Alumni" is a proxy for *likelihood of finding a sympathetic contact*. Do NOT copy names/locations now; you'll only ever need contact detail for your top ~10, and for one or two people each.
- Out of school many years? Substitute a **LinkedIn People search** (1st/2nd-degree + Group connections).
- Small/few-alumni school is fine â€” even 5â€“10% Ys is enough; the broader "alumni = anyone sympathetic" definition gets expanded later (Step 2) only for top employers that lack literal alumni.

### M â€” Motivation (~5 minutes, NO research allowed)

The single most important sort key â€” a proxy for *your willingness to do the follow-up work*. Rate each employer **using only what you already know**, exploiting **arbitrary coherence**: anchor your favorite at **5**, then rate everything relative to it. 4 = second-tier, 3 = third-tier, 2 = least-preferred-but-known, **1 = don't know it (placeholder)**. Researching here is forbidden â€” it injects a second goal and blows the 5-minute budget. (Location unknown but it matters? Score as if location were neutral, then subtract 0.5, e.g. 4.5 â€” resolve those decimals after sorting.)

### P â€” Posting (~15 minutes, NEVER click a posting)

A proxy for *urgency* â€” a posted job means the applicant flood has started, so a top target with a live posting must be contacted ASAP. Default **3-point scale** via Indeed.com, employer name first ("Use Quotes" for multi-word names):
- Search `"Employer" <job keyword>` â†’ relevant hits = **3**.
- No hits? Search `"Employer"` alone â†’ any hits (skim *titles only* for relevant-but-renamed roles) = **3** if relevant, **2** if hiring-but-not-your-role, **1** if nothing.

Scale variants: **2-point** (1/2, any postings or not) for startups/tiny employers or when you have no reliable keyword; **4-point** for undergrad-internship (add "intern" as the ideal tier, drop it first if zero results) or specialty-degree seekers (degree as 2nd keyword); **5-point** = professional-student-seeking-internship (combine both). If your profession rarely posts (VC, law), fill all 1s â€” it just neutralizes P, leaving Motivation + Alumni to drive the sort.

## How to apply (step-by-step)

1. **Open a spreadsheet** with columns L, A, M, P (set M to show one decimal).
2. **Run the four L generators**, 10-min/10-employer cap each; copy names only, skip a row between generators.
3. **Sort L alphabetically and dedupe**; confirm you're at 40+.
4. **Fill A** (Y/N) from your alumni DB or LinkedIn â€” name search only.
5. **Fill M** (1â€“5) from memory, anchored at 5, no research, 5-minute timebox.
6. **Fill P** (pick the scale that fits your search) from Indeed search results only â€” no click-throughs.
7. **Sort the whole sheet: Motivation desc â†’ Posting desc â†’ Alumni (Y first).**
8. **Sanity-check:** the top ~5 are your immediate Contact targets; resolve any 0.5 decimals or top-of-list "1"s (unknown employers that floated up) with a quick one-time research pass, re-score, re-sort.

## Common failure modes

- **Starting with <40 employers.** Skews to high-profile, high-competition names â†’ longer search, less leverage. 40 forces creativity and surfaces hidden gems. Always pad the list.
- **Blending tasks.** Researching while listing, or clicking postings while scoring P, is the #1 time-killer. Single-goal each column.
- **Perfectionism in a column.** Capturing full alumni records, agonizing over which company to include, reading full postings. "Good enough fast" beats "perfect slow" â€” if you're debating whether to add an employer, just add it.
- **Researching during Motivation.** Forbidden â€” it injects a second goal and destroys the 5-min budget. Rate from memory; unknowns are 1s.
- **Treating the list as a to-do list.** It's a *ranked queue*. Work top-down; sunk-cost guilt ("I already applied to X, so I must pursue everyone I like more than X") is what a sorted list immunizes you against.
- **Subscribing to RSS/job-alert feeds.** Turns a proactive plan into a reactive shotgun. Take Postings as a one-time snapshot and move on.
