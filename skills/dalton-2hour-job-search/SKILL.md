## When to use

The modern job search runs on **internal referrals**, not applications: hiring managers satisfice, drown in online resumes, and interview the few "good enough" candidates someone vouches for. This skill is the systematic way to find those advocates fast. Trigger when the user is:

- Building or prioritizing a list of companies to target ("who should I even go after, and in what order?").
- Trying to get a resume seen, past the application pile, or off the "black hole" of online postings.
- Doing cold outreach / networking to get a referral or internal advocacy at a company.
- Deciding **who** to contact at a target company and **what** to say.
- Figuring out who will actually help vs. quietly waste their time (Boosters vs. Obligates vs. Curmudgeons).
- Planning or running an **informational interview**, or trying to turn a contact/chat into an advocate.
- Setting up follow-up so outreach and warm leads don't go cold.

Broad career-strategy / passion vs. craft / leave-stay-negotiate decisions â†’ **`newport-so-good`**. Charged interpersonal moments (a referral going cold, an awkward rush, a soft rejection) â†’ **`difficult-conversation`**. This skill owns the *systematic networking process*.

## Common failure modes (hub-level)

- **Summarizing instead of producing.** Every reference must output an artifact (ranked list, triaged contacts + email + cadence, TIARA set + close). A router-only answer fails.
- **Blending tasks / chasing perfect info.** The book's whole edge is single-goal steps and "good enough fast." Don't research while listing, click postings while scoring, or over-prep an interview.
- **Treating applications as the goal.** The deliverable is *advocacy* (a Booster who gets your resume read), not a submitted application. Never send an unsolicited resume.
- **Crossing the fence.** Specific EM cold email â†’ `em-cold-email`; life-direction/career-capital calls â†’ `newport-so-good`; charged conversations â†’ `difficult-conversation`.
