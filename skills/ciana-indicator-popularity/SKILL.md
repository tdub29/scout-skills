# Ciana Indicator Popularity

## What this decides

Which handful of indicators to learn first, and how to avoid the beginner error
of stacking several studies that all measure the same property.

## The honest starting position

There is no "right" technical indicator. Selection depends on investment style,
trading strategy, risk tolerance, goals, and how much time is available to learn
an indicator's behavior both alone and in combination. Popularity data tells you
what other participants are watching, which matters because those are the levels
the crowd reacts to. It does not tell you what works.

## What is actually used

Measured across terminal usage, in order of preference worldwide:

| Rank | Indicator | World share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **RSI** | ~44% |
| 2 | **MACD** | ~22% |
| 3 | **Bollinger Bands** | ~12% |
| 4 | **Stochastics** | ~9% |
| 5 | **DMI** | ~5% |
| 6 | **Ichimoku** | ~5% |
| 7 | **Volume at Time** | ~3% |

RSI is preferred roughly twice as much as the next study. Regional ordering is
remarkably stable: RSI runs 39% (Asia) to 50% (Middle East / South Africa),
MACD 20% to 23% everywhere.

The simple moving average is certainly more used than any of these, but is
excluded from the ranking because its use is not purely technical.

## The composition rule (the part that matters most)

Pick roughly half a dozen studies, then **choose a set with different
objectives**. The canonical spread:

- a **smoothing** study â€” MACD
- a **momentum** study â€” RSI
- a **distribution** study â€” Bollinger Bands

Then learn them in this order:

1. Read a reliable introduction covering the range of indicators that exist.
2. Pick about six and dig into their actual calculations and tendencies.
3. Apply each **individually** to a chart and watch how it reacts to price.
4. Only then apply them **together**.

The failure this prevents: RSI plus Stochastics plus Williams %R is not three
confirmations, it is one measurement rendered three times. Agreement among
same-family oscillators is not evidence; it is arithmetic.

## Chart type preference, by region

Useful context when reading someone else's chart or publishing for an
international audience.

| Chart type | Americas | Europe | Asia | MESA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Line | 51% | 55% | 41% | 48% |
| Bar | 32% | 20% | 14% | 20% |
| Candle | 14% | 21% | **43%** | 31% |
| Log | 3% | 4% | 1% | 1% |

Normalizing each region against its share of the sample sharpens it:

- **Asia** uses line charts least in absolute terms, but relative to its sample
  size it over-indexes on them by about 25%; its line and bar preferences are
  roughly equal. Its candle preference is the outlier, far above its share.
- **MESA** prefers the line chart most in absolute terms, but relative to sample
  size it over-indexes on **candles** and under-indexes on line.
- **Log charts** are strongly preferred in Europe, equally preferred in the
  Americas, and largely ignored in Asia and MESA.

## Selection checklist

```
[ ] Does my set span smoothing + momentum + distribution, or is it 3 momentum studies?
[ ] Do I know each one's actual calculation, not just its shape?
[ ] Have I watched each alone against price before combining them?
[ ] Am I counting agreement between same-family indicators as confirmation? (do not)
[ ] For levels the crowd reacts to, am I watching what the crowd watches (RSI, MACD)?
[ ] Chart type appropriate to the audience and the question (log for multi-year
    percentage moves, candles for intraday structure)
```
