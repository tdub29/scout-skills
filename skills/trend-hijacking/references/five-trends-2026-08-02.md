## The 5 trends (view counts + engine + how an app plugs in)

### 1. Saxophone Disaster â€” 131M views (engine: escalation)
- Individual videos: 51M, 47M, 33M.
- Format: start with a small, manageable problem, then escalate step by step until
  the final moment feels unavoidable. Set to a dramatic saxophone sound.
- App plug-in (product = the final beat, never appears until then; the app IS the
  "what if you'd just used this"):
  - Screen-time app: "I'll just check Instagram for 5 minutes" â†’ 4 hours lost.
  - Budgeting app: "I'll just buy one coffee" â†’ $400 in impulse purchases.
  - Fitness app: "I'll skip one workout" â†’ 6 months on the couch.

### 2. Life Is Boring, So I Pretend â€” 23.5M views (engine: escapism)
- Individual videos: 10.1M, 8M, 5.4M.
- Hook: "life is so boring, so I pretend..."
- Format: reframe a mundane daily task as a high-stakes scenario. Cooking dinner =
  MasterChef elimination. Cleaning = crime scene investigation. Studying = CIA briefing.
- Key insight: you're not explaining features, you're making the routine the content;
  the app is just the tool inside the fantasy.
- App plug-in:
  - Language app: studying framed as "training for a secret diplomatic mission."
  - Habit tracker: checking off tasks as "completing missions before the deadline."
  - Meditation app: a session as "entering the simulation."

### 3. Photo Every Hour â€” 10.2M views (engine: comparison)
- Individual videos: 5.3M, 3.1M, 1.8M.
- Format: two people exchange one photo every hour across a full day, shown side by
  side in chronological order with timestamps. The magic is the parallel comparison â€”
  different routines, moods, locations.
- App plug-in (the side-by-side does the selling; comment section debates whose
  routine is better, which drives engagement):
  - Fitness app: photo-every-hour of an app user vs. a non-user; contrast = value prop.
  - Productivity app: structured day vs. unstructured day.
  - Meal-planning app: what someone eats with the app vs. without.

### 4. Editing the Questions â€” 6.8M views (engine: rage-bait)
- Individual videos: 4.2M, 2.7M.
- Hook: "editing the questions to make my boyfriend sound crazy."
- Format: film your partner answering normal questions, then in post replace the

  rage-bait â†’ massive comment engagement.
- App plug-in: relationship / dating / couples-therapy apps, or any app with a
  social/community angle. Deeper play: don't create the content yourself â€” recruit
  creators already making it who can integrate the app naturally ("I edited the

### 5. Guess Our Zodiac Signs â€” 738K views (engine: judgment)
- Individual videos: 404K, 334K. Small trend, insane engagement rate â€” every comment

- Format: show two+ people, ask the audience to guess their zodiac signs from

- Principle: turn your audience into judges. Give options, let them debate; comments

- App plug-in: astrology apps are obvious, but any personality/identity angle works â€”

## The 4-step playbook (source wording)

1. **Identify which trends match your product's core tension.** Every trend has an
   emotional engine â€” saxophone disaster = escalation, "life is boring" = escapism,
   "photo every hour" = comparison. Find the one whose core matches your value prop.
2. **Make the product the punchline, not the pitch.** Worst move: slap your logo on a
   trend. Best move: make the product the natural resolution of the narrative so the
   viewer discovers your app instead of feeling sold to.
3. **One format, one creator, repeat.** Reload proved it with bro hooks, Second
   Thought with selfie-mode reviews. You don't need 10 formats â€” one that works, and
   the discipline to post it 20+ times until the algorithm picks it up.
4. **Move fast.** Trends have a 2-4 week shelf life. Winners adapt within 48 hours of
   a trend peaking. By the time you've had three internal meetings about "whether this
   aligns with our brand," the trend is dead.

Bottom line (source): "You don't need to create demand. You need to redirect attention
that already exists."
