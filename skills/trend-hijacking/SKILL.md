# Trend Hijacking

Most brand short-form fails because it explains the product. Trend hijacking does the opposite: it plugs the product into an entertainment format that already has millions of views and lets the viewer discover the product as the natural payoff. The video is entertainment first; the product is the resolution, not the reason for the video. This is how small screen-time apps (Reload, Second Thought, Useless) each pulled tens of millions of views running a single format on repeat.

## Why it works

A trend already carries the distribution. The algorithm and the audience have proven the format holds attention. You are not building attention from zero, you are borrowing a proven emotional pattern and attaching your product to its punchline. The cost of a miss is one video; the payoff of a hit is a format you can run twenty more times.

## The 4-step playbook

### 1. Match the trend to your product's core tension

Every trend runs on an **emotional engine**, the specific feeling that makes people watch and share. Do not chase a trend because it is big. Chase the one whose engine is the same tension your product resolves.

| Emotional engine | What the viewer feels | Fits a product that... |
|---|---|---|
| **Escalation** | tension building toward a break | takes something small and compounds it, or saves you from a spiral |
| **Escapism** | wanting out of a dull reality | offers relief, novelty, or a better version of a boring routine |
| **Comparison** | measuring self against a before/after or a peer | shows change over time, progress, or a gap it closes |
| **Rage-bait** | mild outrage, the urge to correct | flips a common wrong way of doing things |
| **Judgment** | sizing someone up, guessing, verdict-giving | reveals, scores, categorizes, or exposes something |

Start from your product's tension (what it fixes, what it changes, what it reveals), then pick the trend whose engine matches. A screen-time app resolves the tension of a wasted, spiraling day, so escalation and comparison formats fit it naturally; a productivity feature that shows progress fits comparison.

### 2. Make the product the punchline, not the pitch

The product appears as the natural resolution of the narrative the format sets up. The viewer should *discover* it, not be *sold* it. If the video would still make sense with the product removed, the product is bolted on, not woven in. If removing the product breaks the payoff, you have it right. No feature list, no voiceover explaining benefits, no logo lingering. The reveal does the work.

### 3. One format, one creator, repeat 20+ times

Pick one format and one creator and run it over and over. The algorithm does not reward a single clever video, it rewards a repeatable pattern it can learn to distribute. Most of the twenty will underperform. You are buying the one or two that break out, and once one does, the format is validated and you double down on that exact structure. Variety across formats spreads you too thin to ever get read as a pattern. Depth on one format is the discipline.

### 4. Move fast: 48-hour adaptation, 2-4 week shelf life

Trends have a **2-4 week shelf life**. By the time a trend is obviously huge it is often already fading. Adapt your version within **48 hours** of spotting a trend that matches your engine. Speed of execution beats polish here. A rough on-time video beats a polished one shipped after the trend has cooled.

## Example trend formats (engine in parentheses)

Use these as pattern references, not as things to copy literally. The named trend changes constantly; the engine is what transfers.

- **Saxophone Disaster** (escalation): a calm scene that escalates to an absurd break.
- **Life Is Boring** (escapism): a mundane routine framed against a wished-for escape.
- **Photo Every Hour** (comparison): a day or period documented in intervals, showing change.
- **Editing the Questions** (rage-bait): answers reframed to provoke a "well actually" correction.
- **Guess Our Zodiac** (judgment): the audience sizes people up and renders a verdict.

When a new trend appears, name its engine first, then check it against your product's tension using the table in step 1.

## Proof pattern

The screen-time apps (Reload, Second Thought, Useless) did not run five formats each. Each app found one format that matched its tension and ran it on repeat. Roughly 172M views came from five trends applied this way. The lesson is not the specific trends, it is the discipline: one engine-matched format, repeated, shipped fast.

## Anti-patterns

- Chasing a trend because it is big, not because its engine matches your product.
- Explaining the product on top of the trend instead of making it the punchline.
- One-and-done: making a single video per trend and moving on before the algorithm can learn the pattern.
- Slow execution: shipping the trend version after its 2-4 week window has closed.
- Spreading across many formats at once so none reads as a repeatable pattern.
